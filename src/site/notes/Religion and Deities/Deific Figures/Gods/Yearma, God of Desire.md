*"Sage of Cravings" • "Lady of Convictions"*

## Overview

Yearma (YER-mah), Sage of Cravings, tells the faithful to want things and go after them. Pleasure, love, a dream someone else called foolish: if you want it and it hurts nobody, Yearma says take it. Plenty of people find the faith indecent. What they tend to miss is the one line Yearma never lets anyone cross. Whatever you want from another person, they have to want it too.

The god draws people who have spent their lives being told no. Many of them came from stricter faiths and found that holding everything in made them miserable. Yearma's teaching is short. What you want for yourself is nobody else's business. What you want with someone else is sacred as long as they say yes. Living your whole life by other people's rules is just dying slowly.

### Worshipers

Yearma's followers include lovers their families would never approve of, people whose wants their own culture calls taboo, and anyone who has been told that who they are is unacceptable. The clergy call shame a leash other people use to lead you around.

A Yearman temple is a refuge. People come to work out what they actually want, and the priests help them sort the wants that are theirs alone from the ones that would trample someone else. Much of the clergy's work is with people raised in harsh faiths, unlearning the guilt they were taught. Priests also teach plain things that turn out to be hard: how to say what you need, how to ask, and how to hear no.

The panther is Yearma's sacred animal. It goes where it likes and hunts when it's hungry without apologizing to anyone, and it keeps to its own territory and only approaches another panther that wants it to.

### Tenets of Faith

Those who follow Yearma swear to live by these principles:

1. **My life is as I desire it to be.** I shall make my life my own, not what others wish.

2. **I shall not let others judge me for my desires.** So long as they only affect me, I shall do what I wish with them.

3. **Should my desires involve others, I shall indulge in them so long as the others allow me to do so.** If not, I shall find alternative solutions to sate my cravings.

4. **My desires are but a small part of the desires of my god.** For they desire all.

## History

Yearma was worshiped before the [[History/Great Schism War\|Great Schism War]], and the faith has spent much of its history in hiding. Wherever a ruler or a church clamped down, Yearma's worship went underground and carried on. In the [[History/Ages/Age of Enlightenment\|Age of Enlightenment]] some regions grew more tolerant, as thinkers began questioning old restrictions and found the faith agreed with them.

The bitterest quarrel is with the followers of [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Selcontri, God of Restraint\|Selcontri, God of Restraint]]. A steady stream of Selcontrians has crossed over to Yearma over the years, saying that giving up every desire brought them misery where they had been promised peace. Selcontrians call Yearma's faithful reckless, and Yearmans call Selcontrians cruel to themselves. Neither side has given an inch.

Today Yearma's temples stand openly in the larger cities. The countryside is still suspicious.

The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Casta\|Casta]] honor Yearma for the conviction that life should be lived fully, and Yearma's followers among them are known for choosing their own matches and defending them with the sword.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### Day of Yearning

Held on the 28th of Yeargea, the eve of summer, when the warmth makes people bolder, the Day of Yearning is for finally doing the thing you've been denying yourself. For some that is art, for some it's love, and for some it's just a lazy afternoon.

What only concerns yourself, you do freely. What involves someone else, you ask about honestly. Priests coach the nervous on how to ask and how to take a refusal with grace, and help people find another way when what they want can't be had fairly. The temples stay open all day as places to meet people who want the same things.

Plenty of followers use the day to do something they were told was impractical: paint, wear the clothes they were told not to, or sleep in while everyone else works. In the evening they trade stories about what they finally did.
