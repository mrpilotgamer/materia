*"The Great Bringer of Light" • "Savior of the Downtrodden"*

## Overview

Coptimaine (COHP-ti-mayn), The Great Bringer of Light, is the god people turn to at their lowest. The faith teaches that there is always a reason to keep going, however bad things get, and that despair can be beaten with faith and stubbornness. Coptimaine's light is meant first for the poor, the desperate, and those who fell and are trying to climb back.

Farmers watching a harvest fail pray to Coptimaine. So do laborers ground down by poverty, and former criminals hoping for a second chance. What they hear is that tomorrow can be better and nobody is past saving. The faithful are expected to keep a hopeful face even when things are hard for them, so that the people around them can borrow some of it.

### Worshipers

Most of Coptimaine's followers are the people with the least: the poor scraping by day to day, farmers at the mercy of the weather, and former criminals trying to rebuild their lives. The clergy teach that bringing light to others starts with keeping your own chin up. Despair spreads as easily as hope does, and anyone who spreads it on purpose has to be stood up to.

Coptimaine's temples are in the poorest quarters, where they're needed most. The priests hand out food, find beds for the homeless, and talk with people who've lost their way. Every follower is said to carry a small piece of Coptimaine's light, and even a small kindness counts.

Priests are known for staying cheerful through terrible things. Showing your despair to someone already suffering only makes it worse for them, so they smile through their own troubles. They do a great deal of work in prisons, telling the people there that what they did doesn't have to decide who they become. People who crush other people's hope on purpose, through cruelty or mocking the suffering, will find the clergy turn fierce.

That opposition extends to the undead. Coptimainian doctrine holds that a corpse walking is despair given a body: proof offered to the living that there is nothing ahead worth carrying on toward, and that the dark is where a person ends. The clergy will not permit the argument to stand. Temples in afflicted regions train their own to hunt, and a Coptimainian sent against the undead abandons the cheerful face kept for every other occasion. Clerics who have done the work speak of it as the one duty the faith asks of them that brings no comfort to anyone, least of all themselves.

### Tenets of Faith

Those who follow Coptimaine swear to live by these principles:

1. **Bring light to those around.** Show those in their lowest moments that there are always reasons to carry on.

2. **To bring hope to others, I must show a hopeful face.** Showing despair will only bring despair to others.

3. **I share a small portion of the light my god brings to others.** For they are truly The Great Bringer of Light.

4. **Those who will purposefully bring despair to those around them are to be punished.** They truly are despicable.

## History

Coptimaine was worshiped before the [[History/Great Schism War\|Great Schism War]], always among common folk more than nobles or scholars. In the [[History/Ages/Age of Regrowth\|Age of Regrowth]] the faith helped a great many small communities hold on through hard years.

The [[History/Ages/Age of Expansion\|Age of Expansion]] scattered many of the faithful, as the empires drove them off their land or worked them to the bone. The clergy went with them and built temples in the slums and on the frontiers. In the [[History/Ages/Age of Incursion\|Age of Incursion]] worship swelled, and the priests spent those years handing out aid and keeping broken towns from giving up. Coptimaine is still the god of ordinary people, with far more temples in poor quarters and villages than in rich neighborhoods.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### Day of Aid

Held on the 14th of Coptiama, the last long day of summer light, the Day of Aid is for helping people with your hands. The faithful bring food to the hungry, help neighbors with whatever is weighing on them, fix up the houses of the old and the sick, and lend their work to anyone who needs it.

Temples organize the day, sending groups into the poorest quarters with food, supplies and willing hands. Former criminals who found their way through the faith are often at the front, as proof to others that people can change.

In some towns doves are released at sunset, white birds rising into a gold sky. It has become one of the best-known sights of the faith.
