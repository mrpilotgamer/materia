*"Lord of Patience" • "Master of Close-Combat"*

## Overview

Selcontri (Sell-CON-tree), Lord of Patience, asks the faithful to do what is needed and nothing more. Doing things for show, or for pleasure beyond what you need, is distasteful to a Selcontrian. Every act should be deliberate, and any excess is a lapse in self-control. The leanest path is often the hardest one to walk, because you are always fighting your own impulses. [^1]

Selcontrians don't hold back out of pride, or because they enjoy it. They hold back because they believe it is the best way to live. Restraint doesn't mean mercy or weakness, either. If the moment calls for wrath, a Selcontrian brings it, but only as much as the job needs, with nothing wasted and nothing for show. Many who follow this road find a kind of peace on it, free of the pointless busyness that drives everyone else.

### Worshipers

Selcontri's followers include soldiers who prefer plain, effective tactics to flashy ones, people fighting their way out of an addiction, and monks who have given their lives to mastering themselves. The clergy say a person's real strength is in what they choose not to do. Every unneeded act is effort that could have gone somewhere useful.

Selcontrian temples are bare rooms with what worship needs and nothing else. The clergy live by strict codes governing nearly everything they do, and they are living proof that restraint gets easier with practice and that wants you never feed eventually go quiet.

Priests help people who are struggling to control themselves, teach a lean and efficient style of fighting, and advise commanders on winning without wasting men or supplies. They teach breathing to get through a craving and a simple test for telling a need from an urge. The monkey is the faith's sacred animal. Left to itself it is all energy and mischief, but a trained monkey can sit still, work out problems, and wait for the right moment.

### Tenets of Faith

Those who follow Selcontri swear to live by these principles:

1. **My actions are deliberate.** I do no more than required, for doing more is an act of excess.

2. **In battle my mind is clear.** My battle plans are efficient and to the point.

3. **My god is my pillar.** For they are patient and mindful. They are the exemplary example of restraint.

4. **To restrain myself is not equivalent to being merciful.** If the path I walk requires wrath to be brought down upon foes, then that is the path I walk.

## History

Selcontri was worshiped before the [[History/Great Schism War\|Great Schism War]], and the faith has always done well in lean times, when holding back is how people survive. In the [[History/Ages/Age of Expansion\|Age of Expansion]] the clergy warned that empires taking without limit would collapse. Most people called them gloomy and ignored them, while Selcontrians managed their own stores carefully.

When the [[History/Ages/Age of Incursion\|Age of Incursion]] came, the towns that had lived by Selcontrian habits, taking only what they needed and wasting nothing, came through better than most. Since then many commanders have taken up Selcontrian tactics. The faith's long quarrel with [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel, God of Hatred]] goes on, and the two clergies still argue over whether holding feelings in or letting them out does people more good.

The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Barbois\|Barbois]] honor Selcontri for self-command, and their principle of public magnificence and private restraint holds that no citizen's house should outshine the buildings of the commune.

## Related Entities

**Allies:** [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Dilliminner, God of Caution\|Dilliminner, God of Caution]] shares Selcontri's taste for careful, measured action, and the two faiths agree that planning and restraint prevent waste. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arlaxena, God of Leisure\|Arlaxena, God of Leisure]] also preaches doing only what's necessary, though Arlaxena means rest and Selcontri means everything.

**Rivals:** [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel, God of Hatred]] says let your anger out, and Selcontri says keep it in hand. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Yearma, God of Desire\|Yearma, God of Desire]] says chase what you crave, and many Yearmans are former Selcontrians who couldn't keep denying themselves. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Malbing, God of Consumption\|Malbing, God of Consumption]] honors feasting and eating your fill, where Selcontri teaches eating what you need and no more.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### Day of True Control

Held on the 14th of Selcotar, in the heart of winter when stores run thin, the Day of True Control is for doing what you must and nothing more. The faithful give up every pleasure and every task that can wait, and see only to what can't.

They get up when they have to, eat enough and no more, speak when they need to, and leave every postponable job for tomorrow. Priests help people tell what is truly needed from what only feels needed. Newcomers usually find it hard, and are surprised how much of an ordinary day was never necessary. Old hands say the quiet is worth it.

Some towns hold stillness contests, where people sit silent and unmoving for as long as they can manage. There is no feast at the end of the day, since a feast would be exactly the sort of excess the day is meant to resist.
