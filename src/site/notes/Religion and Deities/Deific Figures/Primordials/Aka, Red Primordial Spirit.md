*"Bringer of Destruction" • "Lord of New Life"*

## Overview

Aka (ah-kah), Bringer of Destruction, is the hard truth that nothing new grows until something old is cleared away. Aka is the volcano that buries a valley in ash and leaves the soil rich for a hundred years, and the wildfire that burns an old forest so that seeds which only wake in fire can sprout. Aka means no harm by it. Without these clearings the world would choke on its own growth and never change.

Eruptions, earthquakes, great waves and wildfires all belong to Aka, and so does autumn, when summer's growth dies back so spring can come again. A village buried in ash is farmland two generations later. Aka's faithful don't pray to be spared. They pray to accept what comes and to have the strength to build again afterward.

## Worship and Clergy

### Domains
**Granted Domains:** Destruction, Fire, Strength, Weather

**Subdomains:** Arson, Catastrophe, Ferocity, Seasons

### Holy Symbol
A spiral of red and black, representing destruction spreading outward from its source, like lava flows from volcanic vents or wildfire expanding from initial sparks, consuming all in its path before eventually burning out.

### Sacred Elements
**Sacred Animal:** Scorpion

**Sacred Colors:** Red and Black

### Worshipers

Aka's faithful are mostly islanders who live with volcanoes, and people who have lived through a disaster and come out changed. They're taught that fighting Aka is pointless pride. Hold back an eruption and the next one is worse. Build knowing that one day it will all be swept away.

Akan temples are blunt about what nature can do, and they teach practical ways to survive it. The clergy keep long records of past disasters, as proof that the quiet years always end. They teach people how to get out in time and how to make their peace with losing a home, or a town, or the people in it. The scorpion is Aka's animal: deadly, at home where little else can live, and only dangerous when it has to be.

Priests tend to live near volcanoes and in places where disaster comes often. They set small controlled fires to keep the big ones from happening, and they apply the same idea to most things in life: accept a little loss now to avoid a great one later. When disaster comes they keep order and lead the evacuation. Panic gets nobody anywhere. The clergy advise against loving any building or place too much, and they remind survivors that ground cleared by disaster can grow things that never had room before.

### Tenets of Faith

Aka's faithful vow to uphold these sacred truths:

1. **Destruction enables creation.** I shall accept that new life requires old growth to be cleared away, whether gently or violently.

2. **Natural disasters serve purposes beyond mortal understanding.** I shall not curse the volcano, the wildfire, or the storm, for they perform necessary work.

3. **All things built will eventually be swept away.** I shall create and construct while accepting that permanence is an illusion.

4. **Attempting to prevent Aka's destructions only ensures more violent eruptions.** I shall accept small catastrophes rather than resist until pressure demands total devastation.

5. **The scorpion kills to survive, not from malice.** I shall understand that destruction is function, not judgment, and that Aka's violence serves balance rather than cruelty.

## The Red Druid Circle

### Overview

The Red never stays in one place. They follow catastrophe across Materia, camping under active volcanoes, on fault lines, in floodplains, on ground still warm from a wildfire. The moment a place calms down and stops threatening to kill them, they pack up. Most circles move every few years. The Red sometimes moves with the season, and a Cinder walker can sleep in a dozen wrecked landscapes between one winter and the next.

They own almost nothing, and they treat attachment itself as a failing. A Red Druid who cannot bear to leave a place, or a person, has already broken faith with Aka's first truth: everything built gets swept away in the end.

### Ways and Practices

Each autumn, they keep the First Clearing in small, burning something they made during the year so the lesson stays in the mind. The roster is never stable. People wander in after living through a flood or an eruption, stay a while, then wander out, and the circle marks neither coming nor going with much ceremony.

### Standing

Outsiders treat them as omens. When the Red Druids walk into a valley, nobody can say whether they came because a disaster is on the way or whether the disaster will arrive because they did.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### The First Clearing

The first hard frost of autumn kills summer's growth in a single night, Aka at work on the smallest scale. At dawn the faithful walk out into the fields and gardens, through the blackened plants, and give thanks for the loss that makes next year's growth possible.

Priests lead the gathering in thinking about loss, of plants first and then of the larger things Aka will one day take. People tell stories of disasters they lived through. In these stories nobody beats the volcano. They survive it.

Many of the faithful destroy something they made over the past year, a carving, a piece of writing or a little shed, to show they can let it go. Where dead brush has piled up, the clergy burn it off. Some towns build small huts just to burn them.

At the end of the day people plant bulbs that will sleep through the winter and come up in spring.
