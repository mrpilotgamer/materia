*"The Majesty of the Forest" • "Ruler of All That Lives"*

## Overview

Midori (mi-DOH-ree), The Majesty of the Forest, is what most people picture when they say "nature." Midori is the living world: forests running past the horizon, plants that turn bare dirt into a meadow, animals moving along trails older than any town, and spring coming back after winter. Midori is the kind side of the wild, the green world that shelters and feeds every creature in it. There are stories of lost children whose way home opened up when the trees moved aside, of hunters saved from a predator by a sudden wall of thorns, and of deer leading travelers out of dangerous country.

Midori is the order in the living world, the way every plant, animal and mushroom in a forest has its place. Midori is said to see through every living eye and grow through every root at once. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit\|Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit]] is the death and rot, and Midori is the growth that comes after, and the two work together. A hunter who takes only what they need finds the forest feeds them year after year. One who kills for waste finds the game gone and the old paths grown over with thorns.

## Worship and Clergy

### Domains
**Granted Domains:** Animal, Community, Plant, Weather

**Subdomains:** Feather, Fur, Growth, Seasons

### Holy Symbol
A spiral of green and orange, representing the seasonal cycle, spring's explosive growth and autumn's preparation for renewal, the continuous turning of life that Midori oversees.

### Sacred Elements
**Sacred Animal:** Deer

**Sacred Colors:** Green and Orange

### Worshipers

The deer is Midori's animal. It moves through the forest without harming it and feeds the predators without being wiped out, wary but never afraid, and that is how Midori's faithful are taught to live in the world.

Midori's followers are mostly druids, who stand between the towns and the wild, and hunters, who take from the forest and respect it. They worship in groves, under old trees, and in meadows where the first spring flowers come up. The clergy build nothing, since walls between the faithful and the forest would defeat the purpose. They teach how to hunt and gather without stripping the land, how to tell one plant from another and what each is good for, and how to pass through the wild without leaving a mark.

Priests mostly live out in the wild, getting to know the animals and the growing seasons. The forest talks, they say, through rustling leaves and animal calls and the way things grow, and anyone who listens can learn what it wants. Taking from the forest is fine. Everything has to eat and shelter somewhere. Waste and greed and wrecking things for no reason are what bring Midori's anger. The clergy speak for the forest when a town's growth starts to threaten it, and they work out bargains that let the town and the wild both get by.

The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dulandi\|Dulandi]] honor Midori for the forests their households are built in, and the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Aj'Snaga\|Aj'Snaga]] honor the spirit of life who returns the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Araki\|Araki]] after death. [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Eldar\|Eldar]] farmers make offerings to Midori at planting, and in [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Hovden\|Hovden]] the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Jotuun\|Jotuun]] honor Midori for the forests, to the disapproval of their desert kin.

### Tenets of Faith

1. **Take only what is needed, use all that is taken.** Waste is the greatest insult to the living world that provides for all creatures.

2. **The forest is not mine to own but to participate within.** I am one small part of nature's vast community, neither above nor outside it.

3. **Growth and decay complete each other.** I shall honor both spring's renewal and autumn's preparation, for neither can exist without the other.

4. **Nature protects those who respect it.** I shall move through wilderness with gratitude and care, never exploitation or destruction.

5. **Even the smallest plant serves the forest's balance.** I shall not judge nature's worth by mortal standards of beauty or utility.

## The Green Druid Circle

### Overview

Ask anyone to picture a druid, and they will picture the Green. The oldest-feeling and most numerous circle, the Wardens keep to deep forest, broad plains, and any country thick with life, shifting on a slow rhythm of decades as a wood's needs change.

### Ways and Practices

Their first law is Midori's first tenet: take only what you need, use all of what you take. They hold themselves to it harder than outsiders expect from people who look so gentle. Their magic moves through animals, plants, and the turning year, letting them speak with beasts, coax growth, and close a wound with the forest's own medicine. Midori grants community, and they take it seriously, governing by council instead of by a single elder and carrying that same care out to the people they deal with.

### Standing

They are the circle the outside world actually meets, because the Green have set themselves up as go-betweens. They bargain with villages whose fields are creeping into the trees, warn settlements when their growth is about to tip the balance, and work out the compromises that let farms and forest share an edge without bloodshed. By old understanding they and the Black Circle are two sides of one job, life and death minding the same wheel.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### The Spring Awakening

On the spring equinox, the 7th of Arlaya, when day and night are equal and the year turns toward warmth, Midori's faithful head out into the forests and meadows to watch the world wake up. Before dawn they go deep into the woods and note every sign of spring they find: buds opening, birds coming back, insects crawling out, green shoots through ground that was frozen a few weeks before. There are no formal prayers. Priests just teach people how to notice.

Druids plant blessed seeds, and hunters let go young animals they raised over the winter, giving back some of what they take. All day long people swap knowledge of what can be foraged and what it's good for. In some places people go on forest walks, getting themselves lost on purpose and trusting Midori to lead them home if they behave well. Seeing a deer during the festival is taken as Midori's blessing.

At sunset everyone gives thanks for getting through the winter and promises not to take more than they need in the year ahead.
