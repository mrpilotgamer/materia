*"The Winter Guardian" • "Bringer of Winds"*

## Overview

Shiroi (she-ROY), The Winter Guardian, is said to have been there when [[Places/Materia\|Materia]] was made. Shiroi is the cold that balances the heat and the stillness after movement. Legends say Shiroi made winter and ice to hold back summer's heat and the sea's warmth, and so set the seasons turning, so that the world would neither burn itself out with endless growth nor drown in endless storms. Shiroi is also the wind that fills a traveler's sails and pushes explorers across empty country, the frost that keeps what warmth would spoil, and the ice that holds water still when letting it run would do harm.

Shiroi keeps things safe by slowing them down. The cold holds off rot, the wind carries ships to harbor, and ice makes a bridge across water nobody could otherwise cross. Winter is a rest between [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Aka, Red Primordial Spirit\|Aka, Red Primordial Spirit]]'s autumn and [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Midori, Green Primordial Spirit\|Midori, Green Primordial Spirit]]'s spring. Without it the world would wear itself out, growing and rotting and never stopping until there was nothing left. The wind is as much Shiroi's as the cold is. You can't see either one, and either can kill you or save you. A caravan caught in a blizzard dies. A ship with a good wind makes port with weeks of food to spare. So Shiroi's faithful pray for a safe journey, never for warm weather.

## Worship and Clergy

### Domains
**Granted Domains:** Air, Travel, Water, Weather

**Subdomains:** Exploration, Ice, Seasons, Wind

### Holy Symbol
A snowflake pattern of white and blue, representing winter's crystalline perfection, the unique geometry of ice that demonstrates order within cold, and the delicate complexity of frost that appears simple until examined closely.

### Sacred Elements
**Sacred Animal:** Polar Bear

**Sacred Colors:** White and Blue

### Worshipers

Shiroi's faithful are travelers who live by the wind and explorers who go into frozen country where Shiroi rules alone. The polar bear is Shiroi's animal. It lives happily where most creatures would freeze, which shows that winter is just a different way of living. It also shows that power and patience can go together: the bear moves slowly all winter and hits hard when it has to.

Worship usually happens outside in winter weather, and the faithful go out into the cold and wind on purpose to understand the spirit better. The clergy keep simple huts in places where they can study the wind and feel winter at its worst. They teach how to survive bitter cold and how to steer by the wind, and that cursing the winter wastes strength better spent getting through it. Winter always ends.

Priests live in the north and in the mountains, where winter rules most of the year, and they know ice and wind and what it takes to stay alive in the cold. They teach navigation by wind and stars and how to dress for the cold, and they counsel people to accept the winter instead of complaining. When they pray, it's often for good winds for travelers, since the same spirit that sends the killing blizzard also sends the gentle breeze that brings a ship home.

The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Valekai\|Valekai]] honor Shiroi on every cliff face and high pass, and a climber who sets out in bad weather without asking the spirit's leave is called a fool. In [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Hovden\|Hovden]], the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Jotuun\|Jotuun]] honor Shiroi for the winds and winters.

### Tenets of Faith

Shiroi's followers pledge:

1. **Winter preserves what warmth destroys.** I shall honor the cold as protection rather than curse it as hardship.

2. **The wind carries both death and deliverance.** I shall respect its power and prepare for both possibilities with equal seriousness.

3. **Balance requires restraint as much as growth.** I shall accept winter's pause in the cycle, understanding that rest enables renewal.

4. **The polar bear thrives where others perish.** I shall adapt to harsh conditions rather than demand the world accommodate my comfort.

5. **Ice creates paths where water blocks passage.** I shall recognize that what appears hostile often enables what seemed impossible.

## The White Druid Circle

### Overview

The White Circle covers more ground than any of the others, because Shiroi rules travel as well as cold. They keep to harsh open country, windswept tundra, high passes, frozen flats where winter runs longest, and they are forever pushing past the edge of the mapped world into places that have no names yet.

### Ways and Practices

They read the wind for navigation, call up a fair breeze for a struggling caravan, and freeze a bridge across water that would otherwise stop a journey cold. Theirs is the patience of the polar bear: long stillness, then one sudden burst of force. They are the hardest and most self-denying of the druids, and prize self-reliance the way the Yellow Circle prizes a warm welcome.

### Standing

Travelers and explorers hold them in high regard and seek a blessing before any hard crossing, because a White druid's guidance can be the difference between reaching port with food to spare and losing a caravan to a blizzard. They give the guidance freely and stay distant all the same, slow to attach and quick to move on. The same wind, they will tell you, carries both death and safe passage.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### The Longest Night

On the winter solstice, the 21st of Mergar, the longest night and the heart of winter, Shiroi's faithful gather somewhere open to the wind and the cold. Even now the year is turning. Tomorrow will be a little longer, and in time spring will come back.

The sun sets earlier than on any other day. Priests lead the gathering in thinking about endurance and patience. People swap what they've learned about getting through past winters. Travelers hang up wind chimes or strips of cloth that catch the breeze and pray for good winds in the year ahead.

The faithful stay outside all night, dressed for it, feeling the full force of the winter. Fighting the cold wears you out, and accepting it keeps you alive. Some towns carve ice sculptures, beautiful things that won't outlast the spring.

At dawn they watch the sun come up. Winter still has weeks to run, but the light is already coming back. The priests read the winds for the season ahead.
