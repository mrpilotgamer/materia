*"Lord of the Night" • "The Balancer"*

## Overview

Kuro (KOO-roh), Lord of the Night, is the other half of everything. Kuro is the night sky after a bright day, the undergrowth in the shade of a thick canopy, and the rot that sets in after a long life. Without things breaking down nothing new could grow, and without darkness light wouldn't mean much. Some believe Kuro's hold over the line between living and dead lets the spirit shape what it means to exist at all, though no mortal claims to understand how.

Kuro was here before any civilization, before mortals as we know them, and before life and death were two separate things. The gods came later. Kuro saw the first sunrise and the first death and saw no difference in how much each was needed. Kuro isn't life's enemy. Kuro is what finishes it. When a fire burns down an old grove, Kuro sees that the ash feeds the soil the next forest will grow from. The faithful believe the pull between these opposites is what keeps the world going.

## Worship and Clergy

### Domains
**Granted Domains:** Erosion, Plant, Repose, Void

**Subdomains:** Ancestors, Dark Tapestry, Decay, Stars

### Holy Symbol
A circle half black, half green, representing the balance between death and life, night and day, decay and growth. The symbol reminds followers that all opposing forces are different aspects of the same natural cycle.

### Sacred Elements
**Sacred Animal:** Maggot

**Sacred Colors:** Black and Green

### Worshipers

Kuro's temples are usually in graveyards, where you can see the dead feeding the grass, or in observatories, where the night sky can be studied properly. The clergy bury the dead so that they go back into the cycle, sit with people who can't accept that they'll die, and teach that being afraid of death or rot means you haven't understood how the world works. Their favorite example is the maggot, which everyone finds disgusting and which the world couldn't do without.

Most of Kuro's followers are gravekeepers, who see the dead properly back into the earth, and stargazers, who read the night sky. The clergy say that death is a change of state, rot feeds new life, and the night shows things the day hides.

Priests keep gardens built to show how decay feeds growth, composting the dead to feed the living plants. They study the stars and chart their movements, teaching that the night sky holds knowledge you can't get in daylight. They do most of their work at night, and they teach that the dark is where things are revealed to anyone willing to look.

The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dulandi\|Dulandi]] honor Kuro at every death, laying their dead among the forest roots to be given back to the spirit.

### Tenets of Faith

Followers of Kuro pledge themselves to these tenets:

1. **All things decay, and through decay, all things are renewed.** Death feeds life, darkness reveals truth, and endings enable beginnings.

2. **I shall not fear the night, the undergrowth, or death itself.** These are natural and necessary, deserving respect rather than dread.

3. **Balance must be maintained between opposing forces.** Neither life without death nor light without darkness can sustain existence.

4. **I shall return all things to the natural cycle.** What the earth provides, the earth shall reclaim, and through this reclamation, new growth emerges.

5. **The maggot teaches truth the beautiful flower cannot.** All parts of nature serve their purpose, regardless of mortal preference or comfort.

## The Black Druid Circle

### Overview

The Black Circle keeps to places where you cannot easily tell death from life: fens, fungal forests where the dead are visibly turning into the living, the deep shade under an old canopy. They move rarely and slowly, because rot keeps its own pace and cannot be hurried. A Black camp can sit in one mire for a generation, tending it like a garden.

### Ways and Practices

They look after endings. Where they pass, the wild dead are returned to the cycle, and where people allow it, the Lantern-Bearers sit with the dying and compost the fallen into soil for the next generation of greenery. Their magic is an odd braid of fungal growth, slow decay, and true rest. They work and worship at night, and they read the stars better than anyone on Materia, charting constellations and conjunctions the rest of the world only blunders into.

But they draw one unyielding line: rot must be allowed its course. They tolerate the slow dead, like a shambling corpse that still sheds flesh to the beetles, a ghost that fades by inches. Because those are still turning, still feeding the earth, still part of the wheel. Wathorians accept this. The slow dead reach their end on their own, so they fall within the pact between [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Wathoria, God of Equity\|Wathoria]] and the Primordials. Their cold contempt is saved for the preserved: vampires who freeze their flesh, liches who cage their souls, mummies who refuse to molder. To halt decay is to halt the cycle, and that is blasphemy. The slow dead are met with quiet indifference; the preserved dead are met with their inevitable end.

Many of Kuro's followers see the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Araki\|Araki]]'s return from death as unnatural, and a few rank them with liches and the other dead that refuse their end. The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Aj'Snaga\|Aj'Snaga]] resent the comparison bitterly and do not worship Kuro.

### Standing

People find them eerie and come to them anyway, because no one is better suited to sit with a dying parent or to talk a wrecked household through its grief. They feel that grief only faintly themselves, having spent too long beside the maggot to be frightened of its work. The Black and the Green Circle are two sides of one job, life and death, keeping the same wheel turning.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### The Night of Balance

On the night of the autumn equinox, the 7th of Malbia, when day and night are equal and the year turns toward decay, Kuro's faithful gather in the open to watch the sun go down. Tonight light and dark are evenly matched, and that balance is the heart of what Kuro teaches.

Before sunset they plant seeds in soil made rich by rotted matter. Priests lead the gathering in quiet thought about death, darkness and decay, the things people fear most. Then the faithful sit through the deepest part of the night with no light at all, learning to find their way and be at ease in the dark. Many bring small animals that died on their own, never killed for the purpose, and bury them. Maggots are sometimes set loose on the compost heaps with a blessing.

The night ends with stargazing until dawn. The stars only show in the dark, the clergy say, so some truths need the night to be seen.
