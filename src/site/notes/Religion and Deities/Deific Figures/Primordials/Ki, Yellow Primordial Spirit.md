*"Sun Emperor" • "The Primordial Flame"*

## Overview

Ki (KEE), Sun Emperor, is the daylight and the warmth that makes things grow. Ki is the sun coming up every morning, the heat that turns seed into harvest, the light that drives off the dark and whatever is hiding in it, and the summer that stands opposite [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Shiroi, White Primordial Spirit\|Shiroi, White Primordial Spirit]]'s winter. When the season is good, the harvest big and the days long and warm, that is Ki being generous. But Ki doesn't have to be. Old stories tell of cities that angered Ki and found themselves under clouds that never lifted, their fields blighted and their harvests failing whatever they tried.

Ki's gifts come so reliably that people forget they are gifts. The sun rises, summer comes back, the crops grow. Daylight is Ki's to give or keep, not something mortals are owed. The [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dali\|Dali]] are especially devoted to Ki, whom they honor as the source of all light. Ki's faithful give thanks all year round, not only at harvest, in case the Sun Emperor looks away and leaves the fields to wither.

## Worship and Clergy

### Domains
**Granted Domains:** Charm, Fire, Sun, Weather

**Subdomains:** Day, Love, Light, Seasons

### Holy Symbol
A radiant sun pattern of yellow and red, representing Ki's energy spreading outward from a central source, the life-giving warmth that touches all things equally, and the primordial flame that has burned since [[Places/Materia\|Materia]]'s creation.

### Sacred Elements
**Sacred Animal:** Canary

**Sacred Colors:** Yellow and Red

### Worshipers

Ki's followers are gardeners, farmers, and the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dali\|Dali]]. Their holy places are open gardens and fields where the sun reaches every corner. Walling yourself off from the light you're worshipping would be absurd. Priests teach when to plant, how to rotate crops, and how to farm with the seasons.

The canary is Ki's bird, as yellow as the sun, and its song greets the morning. Priests get up before dawn to watch every sunrise, the moment Ki's light comes back and drives off [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit\|Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit]]'s dark. They teach that you can't love daylight without having sat through the night, or value a harvest without having lived through a hungry winter. At planting and harvest they bless the fields and give thanks, since all a farmer's work comes to nothing without the light.

The clergy keep careful sun-calendars, recording how long the days run through the year. They warn against thinking any magic or invention could ever take the sun's place. Every morning the sun rises because Ki chooses to let it, and every warm summer day is a kindness. Take it for granted and you invite the very thing everyone fears.

### Tenets of Faith

Ki's worshipers vow:

1. **The sun's rise is gift, not guarantee.** I shall greet each dawn with gratitude rather than expectation, acknowledging Ki's choice to provide light and warmth.

2. **Summer's abundance requires winter's scarcity.** I shall honor all seasons equally, understanding that Ki's power works within the greater cycle rather than dominating it.

3. **Growth cannot occur in darkness.** I shall recognize my fundamental dependence on Ki's light, never taking for granted what enables all life to flourish.

4. **The canary's song announces daylight's return.** I shall celebrate dawn and summer not with silent acceptance but with active appreciation and joy.

5. **Cities that forget the sun's importance will learn through its absence.** I shall maintain respect and gratitude lest Ki's attention turn elsewhere and leave my works to wither in shadow.

## The Yellow Druid Circle

### Overview

The Yellow Circle follows the warmth. They migrate with the seasons and the strength of the sun, wintering in the south or the lowlands and working north into open, sunlit countryside as summer returns. Ki's power cannot be honored in the shade, so they stay where the light falls clear. Of the seven, they wander on the most regular schedule, because the sun is the steadiest gift any Primordial gives.

### Ways and Practices

Every member rises before dawn to greet the sunrise. They keep canaries, whose song calls up the morning and whose health tells them whether the air is good. They run a real hierarchy, which is unusual for druids, ranking elders by how many summers they have seen.

### Standing

They are the most orderly of the circles and the easiest to get along with. Of all druids, they are the likeliest to stroll into a village and the likeliest to be welcomed when they do. Wherever they pass, they teach planting times and crop care, working alongside farmers the way Ki works alongside the field, and they take care to repay a season's welcome with a season's labor.

## Holy Days and Festivals

### The Day of Greatest Light

The summer solstice, the 21st of Maltiam, is the longest day of the year and Ki at full strength. The faithful spend the whole day outdoors in fields and gardens where they can watch the sun cross the sky from one horizon to the other. Even today the year is turning. Tomorrow will be a little shorter, autumn will come, and in time [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Shiroi, White Primordial Spirit\|Shiroi, White Primordial Spirit]]'s winter will return.

Before dawn the faithful find a place to watch the first light touch the land. Priests give thanks for everything growing in the fields and gardens. People spend the day weeding, watering and tending the plants, as their share of the work. Ki gives the light and the warmth, people give the care, and between them they make a harvest.

Every dish on the table is grown in the sun. [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dali\|Dali]] communities celebrate with particular joy. Many people plant sunflowers, which turn to follow Ki across the sky all day, and some release canaries.

At sunset the faithful give thanks for the long light. Summer still has weeks to run, but the days have already started to shorten.
