## Overview

The Valekai are the people of the Wildlands' only mountain range. They live in warrens carved into cliff faces and bored deep into the rock, linked by tunnels, ledges, ropes, and bridges, and they share nearly everything with their clan. They are clever, practical, and endlessly inventive, and they have kept to their heights while empires rose and fell in the lowlands around them.

The Valekai are not a nation. They have no king, no capital, and no borders of their own, and the frontier between the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Cilomutesan Union\|Cilomutesan Union]] and the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Bornumia\|Empire of Bornumia]] runs through their mountains without their leave. They are simply a people, and they intend to remain one.

## Origins & Distribution

The Valekai are older in their mountains than any record the lowlanders keep. Their language, Velikein, is unrelated to Eldaran or to any tongue descended from it, and the Valekai have never been part of the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Eldar\|Eldar]] world. Their own tradition holds that the mountains were theirs before the Eldar came, and some Valekai claim the range as the place where the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Vullukk\|Vullukk]] first climbed into the world. No scholar has proven the claim, and no Valekai has ever needed to.

The Valekai hold most of the range, through the high country along the Union's border with Bornumia. The northernmost peaks were once theirs as well, until the clans abandoned them in 5267, and the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Sonta\|Sonta]] now mine them for ore and stone. Eastward, the foothills belong to the Eldar of Bornumia, and between them the Valekai keep to the heights and the deep rock.

When the army of demons and devils ravaged the continent in 5267, the Valekai withdrew into their warrens, sealed the lower tunnels, and cut the ropes to the valleys below. The clans of the northernmost peaks, closest to the army's path, left their warrens altogether and moved south into the heart of the range.

The army never came into the high country in force. Raiding bands climbed the passes and struck a few of the outer warrens, and the clans still remember the names of those they lost. Beside the ruin of Adriacium and Presium, the Valekai escaped almost untouched, though the northern warrens were never reclaimed. The Sonta came into those peaks in the years after the war, and the clans have not returned.

The Valekai are almost pure Vullukk. The few members of other races among them are mostly spouses, adopted children, or lowlanders taken in by a clan, and they are counted fully as Valekai once they are.

## Social Structure

The Valekai live in clans, each called an *etxe*, a house. An etxe is a clan and its warren at once. Its members may number from a few dozen to several hundred, all sharing one warren, one name, and one store of food, tools, and rope. A clan takes its name from its warren, and a Valekai who marries into another etxe takes that clan's name as their own.

Each etxe governs itself through its *batzar*, the assembly of all its adult members. The batzar meets in the clan's great hall, decides matters by long discussion until the clan reaches agreement, and chooses elders to settle disputes and speak for the clan to outsiders. Elders hold their place by the clan's trust, and a clan that loses faith in an elder chooses another.

The Valekai have little use for the idea of property. Food, stores, rope, tools, the tunnels, and the halls belong to no one, and the clan draws on them as it needs. A Valekai uses the tools in their hand and wears the clothes on their back, and no one would take them while they are in use, but a thing set down is free for the next person who needs it. Lowland talk of owning land, or a mountain, strikes the Valekai as faintly absurd.

Work is shared the same way as everything else. On *auzolan* days the whole clan turns out together to cut new tunnels, repair bridges, replace worn ropes, and clear rockfalls, and no one able to work stays home.

Clans are bound to one another by marriage, trade, and old debts of help. Once a year the clans of the range gather in the *Batzar Nagusia*, the great assembly, to trade, arrange marriages, settle quarrels between clans, and share news.

## Values & Beliefs

The Valekai say that every clan is one rope. A single strand breaks under any weight, and many strands braided together can hold a mountain. The image runs through Valekai life, from the ropes that link their warrens to the way they speak of family, loyalty, and duty. A Valekai who fails the clan is said to have frayed, and one who abandons it is said to have cut the rope.

Cutting the rope is the greatest Valekai taboo. In its plainest sense it means abandoning a climbing partner or a clanmate in danger, and a Valekai who saves themselves by letting another fall is cast out of their etxe. The meaning stretches to any betrayal of the clan for one's own gain, and outcasts are said to have cut the rope whatever their crime.

Cleverness is the Valekai's other great virtue. They prize a quick mind, a clever fix, and a solution no one else saw, and they admire a good trick played fairly as much as honest work done well. Riddles, puzzles, and games of wit fill their evenings. A Valekai who solves a problem that has troubled the clan for years is honored at the next Batzar Nagusia.

The Valekai honor two Primordials above all others. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Murasakino, Purple Primordial Spirit\|Murasakino]] rules the underground and the unknown, and the Valekai honor the spirit in every tunnel they cut. They hold that the deep places of the mountain belong to Murasakino, and before a clan digs into new rock its elders make offerings and ask leave.

[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Shiroi, White Primordial Spirit\|Shiroi]] rules the winds, the ice, and the winter, and the Valekai honor the spirit on every cliff face and high pass. A climber who sets out in bad weather without asking Shiroi's leave is called a fool, and one who comes back is called lucky.

The Valekai see magic as one more kind of cleverness and treat it without fear or reverence. Magic is Murasakino's gift, and the Valekai use it the way they use a pulley or a lever. A clan's best mage is valued the way its best engineer is.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language

Velikein shares nothing with Eldaran or its descendants, and lowlanders find it hard to learn. It is full of hard consonants and clipped sounds, and it has an enormous vocabulary for rock, rope, weather, snow, and the state of a climb. Most Valekai who trade with the lowlands also speak Sontan or Eldaran, and they switch between languages with ease.

### The Warren

A Valekai warren lives in two worlds at once. Its face is a cliff, carved with ledges, balconies, windows, and doorways, joined by stairs cut into the rock, rope bridges, and counterweighted lifts that raise people and goods from one level to the next. Behind the cliff, tunnels run deep into the mountain to storerooms, workshops, sleeping chambers, cisterns, and the great hall where the batzar meets.

The deep tunnels keep the warren warm through the long mountain winters, and the Valekai's darkvision makes the dark no hardship. Vent shafts carry smoke and stale air up through the rock, and bellows and fans of Valekai design keep air moving in the deepest chambers. Each generation extends the warren, and the oldest have been carved for longer than anyone can say.

Between cliffs, the Valekai speak by whistling. Their whistle-speech mimics the tones and rhythm of spoken Velikein, and it carries across valleys and down tunnels far beyond the reach of a shout. A skilled whistler can hold a whole conversation with someone on the far side of a gorge. Lowlanders who hear it for the first time often mistake it for birdsong.

### Knowledge and Hospitality

The Vullukk love of facts shapes Valekai custom without ruling it. A guest who comes to a warren is expected to bring something new: a piece of news, a fact the clan has never heard, a story from far away. The host answers with one of their own. A guest who brings nothing new is still welcome, but their visit is spoken of as a disappointment.

Many clans carve their knowledge into the walls of their oldest tunnels, where each generation adds what it has learned. The deeper the tunnel, the older the knowledge, and the elders can walk a visitor back through centuries of the clan's memory.

### Life Events

A Valekai child is carried up to the cliff face on the day of their naming, so the wind can learn their name. At fifteen a young Valekai makes their first solo climb to a high point chosen by the elders. When they return, the clan presents them with their first rope, braided from strands contributed by every adult in the etxe.

Marriages join one Valekai to another's etxe, and the couple braid their ropes together as part of the ceremony. The dead are laid to rest in sealed chambers deep in the warren, given back to Murasakino in the heart of the mountain.

### Dress and Food

Valekai dress is made for climbing and cold. They wear close-fitting layers of wool and felt under hooded cloaks of fur and hide, soft boots with gripping soles, and gloves that leave the fingertips free. Most carry a coil of rope and a small bag of tools wherever they go. For bright snow they wear visors of carved bone or wood with narrow slits to cut the glare.

The Valekai keep sheep and goats on the high pastures, and cheese, milk, and dried meat are the backbone of their diet. In the deep tunnels they grow mushrooms and other plants that need little light. They trade stone, crafted goods, and guide services with the lowlands for grain, salt, and wine, and meals are cooked and eaten together in the clan's great hall.

## Cultural Identity

A Valekai is known by their etxe, their warren, and their rope. They measure themselves by the strength of the clan they belong to, the clever solutions they have found, and the knowledge they have added to its walls. To be Valekai is to be one strand of a rope that has held for longer than any empire below.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The Valekai trade with both of their neighbors. They guide Sonta miners and merchants through the passes, sell them stone and worked goods, and buy grain and salt in return. With the Eldar of Bornumia they trade in much the same way. The trade is steady and mostly friendly, though the Valekai watch the Sonta mines with unease. The mines cut into peaks the clans once called home, and every new shaft cuts rock that Murasakino has not been asked to give.

The frontier between the Union and Bornumia runs through Valekai country, and both nations count the range within their borders. The Valekai do not. They pay no taxes they can avoid and answer to no governor, and so far neither nation has found it worth the trouble to force the matter in the high country. Most clans hope it stays that way. A few elders warn that the lowlands have long memories and short patience.

Younger Valekai are drawn to the lowland cities, where their cleverness and their climbing are in demand. Some come home with new ideas and new tools, and some never come home at all. The clans argue over whether the lowlands are enriching them or bleeding them dry.

## Distinctive Traits

- **Whistle-speech**, carried across valleys and down tunnels
- **A coil of rope** carried everywhere, often braided by the whole clan
- **A new fact or story** offered as a guest's gift
- **Carved knowledge** on the walls of the oldest tunnels
- **Offerings to Murasakino** before cutting into new rock
