## Overview

Barbois culture is built on the citizen, the assembly, and the great public work. Its people hold that every citizen stands equal before the law, that a free people governs itself through open debate, and that a republic should build on a scale worthy of the people it serves. They raise domed halls, broad squares, and long straight avenues, and they live their private lives with a plainness that surprises visitors who have seen their cities.

Their architecture inherits the old [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Eldar\|Eldar]] love of permanence and turns it toward a single purpose, since what the Barbois build, they build for everyone.

## Origins & Distribution

Barbois culture took shape in the north of what is now the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Republic of Korthak\|Republic of Korthak]], across the old border between the Empire of Adriacium and the Oligarchy of Presium. When both fell in 5267, the northern cities were left to fend for themselves. In the city-state wars that followed, many of them fell under captains and warlords who seized power by force and ruled as they pleased.

The Barbois trace their beginning to the risings that threw those captains down. One city after another declared its people free and equal citizens, answerable only to laws they had made in open assembly. The ideas spread across the northern plain faster than any army could march, and the cities that shared them came to share a language, a style of building, and a sense of themselves as one people. When Korthak took shape as a republic, the Barbois gave it much of its shape and its capital at Saventiaria.

Today Barbois culture is dominant across northern Korthak, from the grassland plain to the forests of the north coast. The south of the country belongs to the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Casta\|Casta]], and the two cultures share the republic as rivals and partners.

Barbois culture draws from many peoples, and no race makes up a majority. [[Species and Cultures/Races/Daemedi\|Daemedi]] are the most common, followed closely by [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Humans]] and [[Species and Cultures/Races/Vullukk\|Vullukk]], and members of every other race live among them.

## Social Structure

The Barbois took Eldar citizenship and extended it to everyone. The old Eldar distinction between full citizens, residents, and foreigners has been abolished, and anyone who makes a home in a Barbois province and swears the civic oath becomes a citizen with a voice and a vote. No titles of nobility are recognized, and the Barbois address one another as *citoyen* or *citoyenne*, citizen, whatever a person's wealth or office.

The basic unit of Barbois life is the *foyer*, the hearth, meaning the household living under one roof. A foyer is smaller than the old Eldar familia and holds no authority over its members beyond what they grant it. Adult children leave to found their own foyers, and property is divided equally among heirs.

Each town governs itself through its *commune*, an assembly of all its citizens that elects a mayor and a council for fixed terms. Each province sends elected deputies to a provincial assembly, and the provinces send their own deputies to the councils of the republic. Barbois politics is argued loudly and in public, in assembly halls, on the steps of the commune, and in the newssheets pasted to city walls.

The *salon* is where ideas are tested before they reach the assembly. A salon is a regular gathering in a private house, hosted by a citizen of wit or learning, where guests debate philosophy, law, science, and the arts. A well-regarded salon can shape the politics of a whole province, and its host holds influence that no office confers.

## Values & Beliefs

The Barbois hold three virtues above all others. *Franchise* is freedom under law, the right of every citizen to live, speak, and trade as they choose within laws they have helped to make. *Parité* is equality, the principle that no citizen stands above another before the law or the assembly. *Concorde* is fellowship, the bond of mutual duty among citizens that holds a republic together.

The Barbois insist that freedom without law is no freedom at all. They point to the century of the faceless emperor in Adriacium, who gave his people a single law and let every other restraint fall away, as proof of what unbounded liberty becomes. Barbois philosophers argue this point at length, and it sets their idea of freedom apart from the older creeds of liberation.

Their faith follows from their civic life. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Maltrunity, God of Benevolence\|Maltrunity]] is honored for the principle that the goods of a community belong first to those in need, and Barbois communes keep public granaries and almshouses in his name. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Selcontri, God of Restraint\|Selcontri]] is honored for self-command. The Barbois hold that a citizen should live plainly, spend within their means, and never act merely for show. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Malbing, God of Consumption\|Malbing]] keeps his place in the harvest and the funeral rites, as he does across [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]].

Selcontri's restraint and the grandeur of Barbois building meet in a principle every citizen knows: public magnificence, private restraint. A republic may build as grandly as it can afford, because what it builds belongs to all and is made to last. A citizen who builds their own house grander than the public buildings around it commits the gravest breach of Barbois custom, a claim to stand above their fellows set in stone for all to see.

The Barbois treat magic as one learning among many, studied in academies alongside law and mathematics. They trust it most when its workings are open to inspection and debate, and they regard secret or hereditary magical traditions with suspicion.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language

Barbois is descended from the Eldaran of the northern cities, and an educated Barbois can read Eldar law with study. It has become a language of its own, rounded and precise, and prized for argument. The Barbois take pride in the clarity of their speech, and their academies publish dictionaries and grammars to keep it that way.

### Grand Architecture

Every Barbois town is laid out around its *Maison Commune*, the house of the commune, where the assembly meets and the town's records are kept. It faces a great public square, and the town's principal avenues run straight from the square to its gates. In the larger cities, domed assembly halls, academies, libraries, and theaters line the squares, built of pale stone with columns, pediments, and long rows of tall windows.

Great projects are known as the *Grands Ouvrages*, the great works. A commune proposes a work in assembly, funds it by public subscription, and chooses its architect by open competition. Barbois architects are among the most honored citizens of the republic, and the finest of them are remembered by name for generations. A town counts its Grands Ouvrages the way a family might count its ancestors.

The Barbois rebuilt many of the old Eldar roads, bridges, and aqueducts after 5267, and they maintain them with pride. Their own public works are built to the same standard of permanence. A Barbois bridge is expected to outlast the grandchildren of those who paid for it.

Private houses are another matter. A Barbois home is built well and kept plain, with pale walls, simple furnishings, and good glass in the windows. Wealth shows in the size of a family's subscription to the public works far more than in the house it lives in.

### Life Events

A child's birth is recorded in the register of the commune, and neighbors bring gifts of bread and salt to the foyer. At eighteen, young Barbois swear the civic oath in the public square before the assembled commune, and their names are entered on the rolls of citizens. From that day they may vote, speak in assembly, and stand for office.

Couples marry before the mayor in the Maison Commune, and the marriage is entered in the register as a contract between equals. Many add a religious blessing afterward, though the civil ceremony is the one the law recognizes. The dead are buried in public cemeteries laid out as gardens, and each commune keeps a monument to its citizens who fell in 5267 and in the wars that followed.

### Dress and Food

Barbois dress is sober and well made. Citizens wear long coats, waistcoats, and breeches or skirts of good wool in dark colors, with white linen at the throat. On civic days every citizen wears a cockade, a rosette of ribbon in the colors of their province, pinned to the coat or hat. Rich and poor dress much alike, and conspicuous finery draws the same disapproval as an oversized house.

The Barbois table is generous and unfussy. Bread is the staple, and each commune regulates its bakers to keep the price of a loaf within reach of every foyer. Cattle and sheep from the central plain supply cheeses, butter, and meat, and wine comes by trade from the vineyards of the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Nox\|Empire of Nox]]. Meals are shared, and a salon rarely meets without them.

### Festivals

The greatest Barbois festival is the *Fête de la Concorde*, held each year on the anniversary of the first rising against the captains. Every commune gathers in its square for a common meal at long tables that run the length of the avenues, and citizens of every station sit side by side. The day ends with the renewal of the civic oath, spoken aloud by the whole commune together.

## Cultural Identity

A Barbois is a citizen first, of a commune, a province, and the republic. They measure themselves by the laws they helped to make, the works their commune has built, and the arguments they can win in assembly or salon. To be Barbois is to believe that free and equal people, governing themselves in the open, can build anything.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The Barbois and the Casta share Korthak and disagree about almost everything. Barbois citizens see Casta counts and barons as the very captains their ancestors threw down, and they find the Casta duel barbaric. The Casta, for their part, find the Barbois preachy and their assemblies endless. Yet the two need each other. Korthak sits between the soft power of Nox and the trade of the Union, and neither culture can afford to let its quarrels split the republic.

Within Barbois lands, debate never stops. Some argue that the Grands Ouvrages have grown too costly and that a commune's money belongs in its granaries and almshouses first. Others answer that a people who stop building have begun to decline, a lesson they learned from the Eldar. Radicals press to spread the Barbois model to the Casta provinces, while moderates warn that pushing too hard would break the partnership that keeps Korthak whole.

The Barbois view the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Cilomutesan Union\|Cilomutesan Union]] and its [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Sonta\|Sonta]] with friendly rivalry, as fellow republicans who disagree about masks and much else. They regard the Empire of Nox with unease. Its council does most of the governing, but no assembly chose the Chaos Lord who has stood above it for 170 years, and Barbois philosophers find that arrangement hard to forgive.

## Distinctive Traits

- ***Citoyen* and *citoyenne*** as the address for every person, whatever their rank
- **The civic cockade** in provincial colors, worn on civic days
- **Plain private houses** facing grand public squares
- **The salon**, a gathering for debate hosted in a private house
- **Pride in public works**, with a town's great works remembered by name
