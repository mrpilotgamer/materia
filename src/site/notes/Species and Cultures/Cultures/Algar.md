## Overview

The Algar learned the hard way that freedom and goodness need each other. Its people descend from those who lived through the century of Adriacium's faceless emperor, when a single law let every freedom run unchecked. They came out of that century with a love of ceremony, rank, and written procedure, and with a stubborn refusal to give up the freedoms their ancestors knew.

The Algar are the most varied people in [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]], and they live under the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Nox\|Empire of Nox]], a state raised from Adriacium's ruins by the [[Vera\|Chaos Lord Vera]].

## Origins & Distribution

The Algar descend from the people of the Empire of Adriacium, one of the great realms of the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Eldar\|Eldar]] world. In 5167 Adriacium's emperor was murdered, and a faceless emperor took his place. For a century he ruled by a single law, that no one may infringe on the freedom of another, and enforced nothing else. People of every race lived in Adriacium beside monsters and outsiders of every kind, demons, devils, and undead among them.

Adriacium did not survive the [[History/Timeline\|war of 5267]], which its own army began. Vera, one of the adventurers who fought that army, raised the Empire of Nox from its ruins in the same year. The people who rebuilt their lives under her became the Algar.

The Algar have distanced themselves from everything the faceless emperor did. They honor the long history of Adriacium before 5167, its roads, its law, and its learning, and they remember the century that followed as a warning. They call it the Faceless Century, and they do not speak of it lightly. What they kept from it, through Vera's influence, is a conviction that freedom is precious. What they rejected is the belief that freedom alone is enough.

Algar culture is dominant across the whole of Nox and along the northwestern edge of the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Republic of Korthak\|Republic of Korthak]]. Algar traders, scholars, and officials are found throughout the western Wildlands.

No people in the Wildlands is more mixed. [[Species and Cultures/Races/Daemedi\|Daemedi]] are the most common by a wide margin, their numbers swelled by a century of unions with outsiders. [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Humans]] and [[Species and Cultures/Races/Vullukk\|Vullukk]] are common, and members of every other race live among them. After 5267, outsiders who had lived peaceably under the faceless emperor and would swear to Nox's laws were allowed to remain, and their descendants are part of Algar life today, though no new outsiders have been allowed to settle since.

## Social Structure

The basic unit of Algar life is the *oikos*, the household, made up of a family and anyone living under its roof in its service. An oikos is smaller and looser than the old Eldar familia. Its members owe it loyalty and help, but any adult may leave it and found their own, and Algar law protects that right.

Rank in Algar society comes from *dignities*, titles of honor granted for service to the state. A dignity is held for life and cannot be inherited, and each one carries a place in ceremony, a style of address, and a color of dress. An Algar family may produce dignitaries for generations, but each must earn their own title. Old Adriacian nobility survives only as memory and family pride.

The Algar prize learning, and nothing shows it more plainly than the examinations. Any free person may sit them, whatever their race or station, and they test law, letters, reckoning, and history. Passing marks a person as educated and opens the way into the *service*, the body of officials who carry out the empire's business, and from there toward a dignity. Families of every race and station push their children toward the examinations, and an oikos celebrates a passing result as it would a wedding.


## Values & Beliefs

The Algar hold three virtues above all. *Eleutheria* is freedom, the right of every person to live, believe, speak, and choose for themselves. *Philanthropia* is the love of others shown through doing good, in charity, in justice, and in the protection of the weak. *Taxis* is order, the ceremony, procedure, and law that keep freedom and goodness from being lost.

The Algar believe the Faceless Century proved what freedom becomes without the other two. Freedom without goodness left the strong to prey on the weak, and freedom without order left no one to stop them. They hold that a free people must choose to do good, and must build the order that lets them go on choosing it.

Algar custom protects freedoms that neighboring peoples do not always grant. Any person may worship as they choose, leave their oikos, travel, or petition the council. No Algar may be bound to another's service, and the Algar treat bonded labor with horror. The greatest Algar taboo is taking another person's freedom for one's own ends, whether by chains, by threat, or by magic that bends the will.

[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Wathoria, God of Equity\|Wathoria]], the Judge of Souls, holds first place in Algar devotion. For a century the dead walked freely in Adriacium, and the Algar honor the god who sends every soul to its rightful place and stands against those who refuse their passing. Algar funerals are careful and solemn, and the Algar see to it that no one is left unburied. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Malbing, God of Consumption\|Malbing]] keeps their place at harvests, as they do across the Wildlands.

Magic is respected and closely recorded. The service keeps registers of mages and their specialties, and magic that binds a will, calls on outsiders, or raises the dead falls under strict law. The Algar keep the Eldar Wards of Binding as a working office, the *Phylakes*, whose members guard the borders between the planes and answer to the council.


## Daily Life & Customs

### Language

Algarian is descended from the Eldaran of Adriacium. It keeps more of the old vocabulary of law and government than its neighbors' languages, and it is written in an ornate script that Algar scribes take great pride in. Formal Algarian is elaborate, full of titles and courtesies, and a letter to an official may spend its first paragraph on proper forms of address.

### Ceremony and Rank

Ceremony is the heart of Algar public life. Every dignity has its place in procession, its seat in assembly, and its form of address, and the Algar know these by heart. The Eldar custom of colored hem-stripes survives in Algar dress, where each color now marks a class of dignity and purple is reserved for members of the council.

To outsiders the ceremony can seem stiff. To the Algar it is a guarantee. When everyone knows their place and the forms are followed, no one can rule by whim, and every person can see who holds power and by what right.

### Mosaics and Architecture

Algar builders favor domes, arches, and courtyards, and their great buildings glitter inside with mosaics of colored stone and glass. Mosaics cover the walls and domes of temples, council halls, and wealthy houses, showing scenes of history, the gods, and the virtues. The Algar rebuilt and expanded much of what Adriacium had built, and they take pride in having restored the empire's roads, harbors, and aqueducts.

Vera's image appears in few mosaics and fewer statues. The Algar understand that she does not care for them.

### The Hippodrome

Every large Algar city has its hippodrome, a long track for chariot racing, and outside the Eleutheria the races are the greatest public spectacle in Nox. Racing is organized by factions, each known by its color, each with its own stables, drivers, and devoted supporters. Faction loyalty often runs in families, and supporters of rival colors argue about politics, religion, and much else. Riots between factions are rare but remembered, and the council keeps a close eye on the stands.

### Life Events

An Algar child's name is entered in the registers of the service, the first of many records that will follow them through life. At sixteen, a young Algar comes of age and may sit the examinations, enter a trade, or leave their oikos. Marriage is a contract registered with the service, and either party may end it by law.

The dead are washed, dressed, and buried with care, and prayers are said to Wathoria for their safe judgment. No Algar community leaves a body unburied, and a family that neglects its dead is shamed.

### Dress and Food

Algar dress is long and layered, with tunics and robes of linen, wool, and silk, belted at the waist and trimmed with the colors of rank. Wealthy Algar wear rich brocades and jewelry of copper, silver, and gold. Everyday dress is simpler, though even a farmer owns one fine garment for festivals and court.

The Algar table draws on the vineyards, orchards, and river valleys of Nox. Wine is the drink of every meal, and Algar vintages are prized across the Wildlands. Bread, cheese, orchard fruit, fish, and spiced stews fill the table, and a formal meal follows an order of courses as fixed as a procession.

### Festivals

The greatest celebration in Nox is the *Eleutheria*, the festival of freedom, held in Lincugus at the turn of each new year. As the old year ends and winter begins, the streets are hung with lamps and lit with bonfires, and for three days and three nights the whole capital gives itself over to celebrating the freedom the Algar have kept and the good they have built with it. Every household opens its doors, and food and drink are shared freely with anyone who comes, neighbor or stranger.

Each district of the city celebrates in its own way, and people drift between them to find the music, food, and company they like best. One district fills its streets with dancing, another with long tables of Algar wine and cooking, another with the work of the empire's craftsmen, mosaic-makers, coppersmiths, vintners, and cooks, set out for all to see and taste. The Algar take pride in the things their empire does well, and the Eleutheria is when they show them off.

The racing factions lead the festival's great parades. Each faction spends the whole year preparing its procession, with musicians, dancers, costumes in its colors, and great decorated floats, and the parades wind through the city to the hippodrome, where the crowd acclaims the finest. Winning the parade is an honor some supporters prize above any victory on the track.

For the length of the festival the forms of rank are set aside. Dignitaries go without their titles and colors, officials celebrate in the streets beside everyone else, and no one is addressed as anything but a fellow citizen. The Algar say the Eleutheria is the one time each year when taxis rests so that eleutheria can dance. Other Algar cities hold their own, smaller Eleutheria, but every Algar hopes to see the one in Lincugus at least once.

## Cultural Identity

An Algar is known by their oikos, their dignity or trade, and their faction at the races. The question an Algar asks of a life, their own or anyone's, is what they did with their freedom, and whether any good came of it.

The Algar regard Vera as both a founding emperor and a distant one. She raised Nox from the ruins, and few Algar alive remember a time without her. Yet she leaves the governing to her council and is seldom seen, and most Algar know her as a presence behind the council more than as a ruler they might meet. They mark her comings and goings with no ceremony, knowing she would not want it.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The balance between freedom and order is argued in every generation. Some Algar hold that the council and the service have grown too large, and that registers and procedures are chains of a gentler kind. Others answer that the Faceless Century began with too little order and ended in fire. A few, fewer each year, still speak fondly of the freedom of that century, and the Phylakes watch them closely.

Every Algar knows that Nox has never been without Vera, and few like to ask what would follow if it were.

The Algar regard the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Barbois\|Barbois]] as kindred spirits in their love of freedom and as naive in their faith that equality alone can hold a people together. They find the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Casta\|Casta]] lords' courtship of Vera faintly amusing, since no one knows better than the Algar how little she cares for flattery. They respect the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Sonta\|Sonta]] rule that power must never wear a mask, and understand better than anyone why it exists.

## Distinctive Traits

- **Elaborate courtesies**, with titles and forms of address used precisely
- **Colored trim** marking a person's dignity, with purple reserved for the council
- **Mosaics** in temples, halls, and wealthy houses
- **Fierce loyalty** to a racing faction and its color
- **Careful funerals**, with no body left unburied
- **The Eleutheria**, three days of shared food, drink, and parades in Lincugus at each new year
