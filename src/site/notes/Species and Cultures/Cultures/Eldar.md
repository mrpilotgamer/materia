## Overview

The Eldar are builders who think in centuries, a people whose cultural identity rests on permanent infrastructure and civic institutions that outlast any individual or government. Where Eldar roads run, civilization follows. Conquest is no part of how this happens. It comes instead from the practical demonstration that organized communities with written law and maintained infrastructure simply work better.

Eldar culture once spread across the whole of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]]. Since the war of 5267 it has held only the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Bornumia\|Empire of Bornumia]], the heartland where it was born. The peoples of the west have become cultures of their own, and the Eldar regard them with a mix of kinship and regret. They are proud of what they kept, conscious of what they lost, and certain that their way remains the foundation of Wildlands civilization.

## Origins & Distribution

Eldar culture originated in the eastern Wildlands, in the modern-day Empire of Bornumia, emerging from agricultural communities in the mountain foothills and eastern forests. The need to build permanent infrastructure on unstable ground where planar boundaries thin forged the Eldar obsession with durable construction and forward-thinking planning.

This infrastructural focus, combined with practical literacy for recording land claims and contracts, allowed Eldar culture to spread westward during the [[History/Ages/Age of Expansion\|Age of Expansion]]. Eldar cultural identity proved highly assimilative. Those who adopted Eldaran language, legal frameworks, and civic participation were welcomed as equals regardless of ancestry. In time the culture covered the continent, from Bornumia in the east through the Oligarchy of Presium in the centre to the Empire of Adriacium in the west.

In 5267 an army of demons and devils marched out of Adriacium and ravaged the continent. Within months the western lands fell to ruin. Their people were killed or scattered, and there was no longer a culture left to hold together. The survivors built again from the ruins, and while Bornumia recovered from the attacks that had reached into its own lands, the peoples of the west grew into the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Algar\|Algar]], the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Barbois\|Barbois]], the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Casta\|Casta]], and the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Sonta\|Sonta]]. Each kept something of the Eldar and each became its own people.

Eldar culture today is dominant across the Empire of Bornumia. Its people are mostly [[Species and Cultures/Races/Daemedi\|Daemedi]] and [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Humans]], in roughly equal numbers, with few members of other races among them.

## Social Structure

The Eldar organize society around the *familia*, an extended household including blood relations, adopted members, valued servants, and client families bound by mutual obligation. A prominent *familia* might encompass dozens of people across multiple generations, all owing loyalty to the *paterfamilias* (family head, regardless of gender despite the name's etymology).

Class stratification follows a practical model inherited from imperial citizenship law, in which status can be earned. *Cives* (full citizens) possess voting rights and legal privileges. *Incolae* (resident non-citizens) may own property and practice trades but cannot vote. *Peregrini* (foreigners) require citizen sponsors for legal proceedings. Citizenship passes through both parents, and can also be earned through military service, significant public works funding, or adoption into a citizen *familia*. This creates a society where social mobility exists while requiring wealth, service, or powerful patronage to achieve.

The Empire of Bornumia is ruled by its emperor, and imperial law stands above every town and province. Within that law, each Eldar town governs its own affairs. Its citizens gather each year in the *comitia*, the assembly of citizens, to elect the town's magistrates: two *duoviri* who preside over the town and its courts, and *aediles* who keep its markets, roads, and public buildings. Magistrates who complete their year join the *curia*, the town council, for life. A citizen's vote reaches no higher than the town, but within the town it is guarded jealously.

## Values & Beliefs

The Eldar hold three virtues above all others: *Disciplina* (self-control and order), *Pietas* (duty to family, community, and gods), and *Virtus* (excellence and capability, originally martial but now broader).

They practice pragmatic polytheism, worshiping whichever gods prove effective for specific needs. Farmers honor [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Midori, Green Primordial Spirit\|Midori]] during planting, soldiers make offerings to [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Mendura, God of Courage\|Mendura]] before battle, and merchants invoke [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Riavexect, God of Want\|Riavexect]] when negotiating. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Malbing, God of Consumption\|Malbing]] receives particular reverence in Eldar culture for reasons of practical history and never theological devotion: imperial expansion required consuming resources efficiently, and Eldar funeral rites emphasize the cycle of consumption and return that Malbing represents.

The greatest Eldar taboo is *infamia*, bringing public shame upon one's *familia* through cowardice, broken oaths, or flagrant dishonesty in official proceedings. Private lies matter far less than public honor. Magic is viewed as a tool like any other, neither inherently good nor evil, though hedge magic and divine casting are more respected than arcane study, which the Eldar associate with elitism and impracticality.

The thin planar barriers of the Wildlands gave rise to the *Wards of Binding*, civic magistrates who maintain knowledge of planar entities and guard against them. Before 5267 many younger Eldar dismissed the Wards as superstitious make-work. The war ended that. Since the demons and devils reached into Bornumia, the Wards have been taken with deadly seriousness, their posts are sought after, and every town of any size keeps one.

Outsiders earn respect through competence and adaptation to Eldar customs, while those who refuse to learn Eldaran or respect local law are dismissed as barbarians. The Eldar fear cultural stagnation above all external threats. Their histories teach that the old Eldar empire decayed from within long before any enemy reached it, a lesson invoked again and again in political rhetoric.

## Daily Life & Customs

Eldaran is spoken throughout Bornumia, and educated Eldar learn Classical Eldaran for legal and historical texts. This creates a diglossia where formal occasions demand archaic phrasing. The languages of the western peoples, Algarian, Barbois, Castan, and Sontan, all descend from Eldaran, and an educated Eldar can follow their legal texts with effort. Eldar clothing favors practical layered tunics in undyed wool or linen for common folk, with citizens distinguished by colored hem-stripes indicating their *familia* and station: red for military service, blue for merchants, green for agricultural wealth, and purple, rare and expensive, for political office.

Architecture emphasizes permanence above all, and even modest Eldar homes use stone foundations and tile roofs. Bornumia's long winters shape its buildings, which favor thick walls, steep roofs that shed snow, and hearths at the center of every house. All Eldar structures share characteristic features: columned entryways, wooden where stone is unavailable, decorative friezes depicting legal maxims or historical events, and household *lares* shrines in doorways.

The Eldar greeting depends on status. Equals clasp forearms, subordinates bow slightly, and superiors nod. All formal meetings begin with an offering to the household's *lares* (ancestral spirits). Meals serve as social occasions where business is conducted, and refusing shared food insults the host's honor. Coming-of-age ceremonies occur at sixteen, when youths undergo the *Probatio*, a day-long test of literacy, mathematics, history, and basic combat skills, after which they receive adult names and citizenship rights if born to citizens, or begin their path toward earning citizenship.

## Cultural Identity

Eldar identity centers on language, legal tradition, and the concept of *civilitas*, the quality of being civilized, which the Eldar define as respecting contracts, maintaining public infrastructure, and participating in civic life. Birth alone does not make someone Eldar. Adoption of these principles does, and the empire assimilated countless peoples by extending citizenship to those who learned Eldaran, followed imperial law, and served in legions.

Bornumian Eldar are mostly Daemedi and Human, each bringing distinct perspectives. Daemedi emphasize the spiritual aspects of *pietas*, and Humans drive commercial pragmatism.

The Eldar take particular pride in their roads, aqueducts, and bridges, many still functioning centuries after construction, and see infrastructure as physical proof of cultural superiority. They distinguish themselves from neighboring cultures through their written legal codes, set against the oral traditions of many other peoples, through their multi-generational thinking that builds for grandchildren's grandchildren, and through their conviction that civilization is something built by deliberate effort, generation after generation.

## Adaptation & Tensions

Eldar culture demonstrates remarkable flexibility when living under foreign rule or in diaspora. They simply recreate their civic institutions at whatever scale circumstances permit, forming *collegia* (civic associations) that function as quasi-governments within their communities. This adaptability coexists with cultural chauvinism. The Eldar genuinely believe their way is superior and often act with condescending patience toward "less developed" peoples.

Most Eldar regard the four western cultures as cousins, peoples who share their law and their roots and who went their own way after the fall. Some take a harder view. They see the Algar, Barbois, Casta, and Sonta as lost branches of Eldar civilization, broken off by catastrophe, and hope to see them become Eldaran again. The Eldar themselves are torn over their place in the world. Some carry the pride of a heartland that outlasted its offshoots, while others hold that Bornumia remains the true civilization of the Wildlands, waiting for the rest to return.

Generational conflict pits traditionalists who insist on Classical Eldaran against reformers arguing that archaic practices cause stagnation. Class tensions simmer between old citizenship families and newly elevated *Incolae* who resent second-class status.

## Distinctive Traits

- **Forearm clasp greeting** between social equals, palm-to-elbow grip
- **Colored hem-stripes** on clothing indicating family line and occupation
- **Classical Eldaran phrasing** in formal speech, especially legal contexts ("Let it be known that..." "By authority vested..." etc.)
- **Household shrines** to *lares* visible in doorways, always with fresh offerings
- **Obsessive punctuality** and time-keeping, with Eldar measuring meetings in *horae* (hours) marked by public timekeeping
