## Overview

Casta culture is built around the familia, the tower, and the blade. Its people live under counts and barons, gather in great family houses, and settle their quarrels of honor in formal duels fought by rules every Casta knows by heart. They compete in everything: in the height of their towers, the splendor of their feasts, the artists they patronize, and the skill of their swordsmen.

The Casta kept more of the old [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Eldar\|Eldar]] household than any of their neighbors, and they built on it a society of lords, vassals, and rival houses that the Eldar would hardly recognize.

## Origins & Distribution

Casta culture took shape in the south of what is now the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Republic of Korthak\|Republic of Korthak]], across the old border between the Empire of Adriacium and the Oligarchy of Presium. When both fell in 5267, the southern cities, like the northern ones, fell into the hands of captains and warlords who seized them by force.

In the north, the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Barbois\|Barbois]] rose against those captains and threw them down. In the south, the captains held on. Over the generations they turned their conquests into counties, gave lands to their followers as baronies, and married their children into the old Eldar families of the cities they ruled. What began as rule by the sword became rule by inheritance and custom, and the Casta came to see their lords as a natural part of the order of things.

Casta culture is dominant across southern Korthak, in the valleys of the interior and along the ragged peninsulas of the south coast. Most Casta live in the sheltered valleys and on the peninsulas, where the land is kindest. A few hardy families hold lands at the far southern tip, where the taiga meets the frozen sea, and they are respected across the Casta lands for bearing the cold.

Casta culture draws from many peoples, and no race makes up a majority. [[Species and Cultures/Races/Daemedi\|Daemedi]] are the most common, followed closely by [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Humans]] and [[Species and Cultures/Races/Vullukk\|Vullukk]], and members of every other race live among them.

## Social Structure

The Casta build their lives around the *familia*, the extended household of the Eldar. A familia takes in blood relations, spouses, adopted members, retainers, and client families bound to it by mutual obligation, and it may number in the dozens or hundreds. The *paterfamilias* leads it, whatever their sex, and a familia's name, its tower, and its honor pass together from one generation to the next.

Above the familie stand the lords. A *conte*, or count, rules a county from its chief city, holds its courts, and commands its militia. Beneath the count stand the *baroni*, the barons, who hold castles and lands as the count's vassals and owe him soldiers and counsel. Counts and barons are drawn from the greatest familie, and their titles pass by inheritance, though a lord who cannot keep order may find a rival familia ready to take his place.

Korthak is a republic at its top, and the Casta provinces take part in it in their own way. Each county sends delegates to the councils of the republic, and in Casta lands the count and barons choose them. Within a county, the count's rule is the law, bound only by the old Eldar codes and the laws of the republic.

Common people belong to a familia as members or clients. A client familia owes service, labor, and loyalty to its patron, and the patron owes it protection, justice, and help in hard years. Moving from one patron to another is possible but slow, and a familia that abandons its clients loses honor quickly.

## Values & Beliefs

The Casta measure life by *onore*, honor. A familia's honor rests on the courage of its members, the loyalty it shows its clients and allies, the grandeur of its house and table, and the skill of its blades. Honor is gained in public and lost in public, and every Casta knows where their familia stands against its neighbors.

Two gods shape the Casta spirit. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Millary, God of Jealousy\|Millary]] is honored for the ambition that drives a familia to outdo its rivals. The Casta hold no shame in envy, and a familia that inspires envy in its neighbors counts it proof of success. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Yearma, God of Desire\|Yearma]] is honored for the conviction that life should be lived fully, in love, feasting, art, and the pursuit of one's own ambitions. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Malbing, God of Consumption\|Malbing]] keeps his place at harvests and funerals, as he does across [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]].

The greatest Casta taboo is killing outside the code. A Casta may challenge an enemy to a duel and kill them in it with honor. To ambush them, poison them, or strike them unwarned is murder, and it shames the killer's entire familia. The duel exists to channel the feuds of rival houses into fights with rules, and those who break its rules are treated as enemies by every familia.

Magic is welcome in a Casta court. Counts keep mages as advisers and retainers, and a famous mage brings honor to the familia that shelters them. The code of the duel forbids magic unless both parties agree to it beforehand, and a duelist caught using a hidden spell is disgraced.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language

Castan is descended from the Eldaran of the southern cities, and an educated Casta can read Eldar law with study. It has grown into a musical, expressive language, rich in words for honor, insult, and courtesy, and spoken with a great deal of gesture. The Casta love a well-turned insult almost as much as a well-fought duel.

### The Duel

The *duello* is the heart of Casta honor. Any Casta of age may challenge another over an insult, a broken promise, or a wrong done to their familia. The challenge is delivered in writing as a *cartello*, which names the offense and the challenger's demand for satisfaction. The one challenged may apologize, accept, or name a champion to fight in their place.

Each duelist chooses a *padrino*, a second, and the seconds agree on the terms: the weapons, the place, the time, and how the duel will end. Most duels end at first blood or when one party yields. A duel to the death requires both parties to agree and the count to permit it, and the laws of the republic have made such duels rare. Refusing a lawful challenge without apology costs a familia honor, though no one is forced to fight.

Casta youth learn the blade in the *sala d'armi*, the fencing hall, under a *maestro* whose reputation rests on the success of his students. The rapier is the Casta weapon of choice, and the great fencing schools compete for students as fiercely as the familie compete for honor. A famous maestro is as celebrated as a famous painter.

### The Tower

Every familia of standing builds a tower. A Casta town rises as a forest of stone towers, each the fortified house of a familia, where it keeps its treasures, shelters its members in times of feud, and displays its standing to the world. The height of a tower is the plainest measure of a familia's pride. By old custom no tower in a county may rise higher than the count's, and a familia that tries is declaring itself a rival for the county.

### Patronage and Feasting

Casta lords and great familie compete through the artists they keep. Painters cover the walls of towers and chapels with frescoes, sculptors fill the squares, and poets compose verses in praise of their patrons. A count's court is judged by the talent it gathers, and a familia may bankrupt itself commissioning a chapel grander than its rival's.

Feasts are Yearma's gift and Millary's contest at once. A great feast runs for hours, course after course, with music, dancing, and poetry between them. A host who sets a poor table loses honor, and a guest who leaves early insults the house.

### Life Events

At sixteen a young Casta receives their first blade from the paterfamilias before the assembled familia, and with it the right to issue and accept challenges. Marriages are arranged between familie as alliances, though Yearma's followers are known for making matches of their own choosing and defending them with the sword. The dead are laid in the familia's tomb beneath its chapel, and their deeds are recited at a feast held in their honor.

### Dress and Food

Casta dress is rich and colorful. Doublets, gowns, and cloaks are cut to show off fine cloth, and each familia's livery colors mark its members and clients. Nobles and duelists wear a sword at the hip as part of formal dress.

The Casta table leans on the forests and the sea. Game, wild boar, mushrooms, and river fish come from the valleys, and the peninsulas supply fish and shellfish. Barley and grain are cooked into thick porridges and breads, and wine comes by trade from the vineyards of the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Nox\|Empire of Nox]]. Every great feast ends with sweets, and a familia's cooks guard their recipes as closely as its secrets.

### Festivals

The greatest Casta festival is the *Palio*, a horse race held each summer in the chief city of every county. Each district of the city fields a horse and a rider under its own banner, and the race runs through the streets and around the main square in a few minutes of chaos. The winning district feasts for a week, and old rivalries between districts are as fierce as those between familie.

## Cultural Identity

A Casta is known first by their familia, then by its tower and its lord. They measure themselves by the honor of their name, the height of their tower, the artists at their table, and the courage of their blades. To be Casta is to belong to a house and to fight for its place in the world.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The Casta and the Barbois share Korthak as rivals and partners. Casta lords find the Barbois preachy and their assemblies endless, and they resent Barbois talk of spreading equality into their counties. The Barbois call Casta counts the heirs of the captains their ancestors threw down. Yet the partnership holds, because Korthak sits between Nox and the Union and neither culture can afford to let its quarrels split the republic.

Within the Casta lands, the familie feud without end. Rising merchant and banking familie buy their way into the old nobility and build towers that crowd those of the barons. Younger children who inherit no land often take up the sword for hire, and bands of Casta duelists and soldiers serve as mercenaries across [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]].

The Casta regard the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Sonta\|Sonta]] of the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Cilomutesan Union\|Cilomutesan Union]] as useful trading partners and poor company, too fond of councils and suspicious of any lord. They view the Empire of Nox with more respect than their Barbois neighbors do. A lord who stands above a council and leaves it to govern looks much like a count among his barons, and more than one count has sought Vera's favor.

## Distinctive Traits

- **The duel**, fought by formal challenge, seconds, and agreed terms
- **Family towers** rising over every town, none higher than the count's
- **A sword at the hip** as part of formal dress for nobles and duelists
- **Livery colors** marking each familia and its clients
- **Lavish feasts** with music and poetry between the courses
