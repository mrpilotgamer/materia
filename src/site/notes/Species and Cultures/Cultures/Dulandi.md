## Overview

Dulandi culture has magic at its heart. Every [[Species and Cultures/Races/Mannee\|Mannee]] is born able to work magic, so among the Dulandi it is as ordinary as farming or walking, used to light the hearth, shape the wood of a home, and call rain to a dry field. Its people hold that magic is a birthright and that a person's worth shows in what they can do with it. They also hold that the self lives in a person's name and magic, whatever shape the body happens to wear.

## Origins & Distribution

Dulandi culture began in the forests of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Imnas\|Imnas]], and it is as old as anyone can remember. Scholars debate whether it is the oldest culture still strong in [[Places/Realms/Alora/Alora\|Alora]], and the Dulandi themselves see little reason for debate. Its households have lived among the same trees and hills for longer than any record they keep, and their oldest stories were already old when the first kingdoms rose in the Age of Enlightenment.

Today Dulandi is dominant across Imnas, in the cool northern forests and the eastern rainforests alike. A few isolated villages lie south of the Imnas border, in the strip of tropical rainforest along the northern foot of the mountains, where borders have moved over the centuries and the villages have stayed put. Sireth, the floating capital, is Dulandi as well, though its mages live at a remove from the forests below.

The Mannee are the great majority of the Dulandi, and the culture is shaped around them. Members of other races live among them in small numbers, and they are Dulandi in every way that matters to their neighbors.

## Social Structure

The foundation of Dulandi society is the *aelwyd*, the household, a word that also means the hearth. An aelwyd is an extended household of kin, fosterlings, and apprentices living together in one stand of trees or one hillside. Its head, the *pennaeth*, is its most capable mage, and the role passes to whoever surpasses them in skill.

Standing among the Dulandi comes from magical capability. It does not matter whether that capability was born strong or built through long study, and a [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Human]] who masters magic by learning earns the same respect as a Mannee born to it. Those who cannot work magic, chiefly members of other races, are counted less capable, though no one counts them lesser people, and they seldom have much influence in an aelwyd or a village.

Households bind themselves to one another through *altram*, fosterage. A young Dulandi is often sent to grow up in another aelwyd whose pennaeth has the skill they need, and the two households are joined from then on by the child they shared. A fosterling owes their foster household the same loyalty as their own, and many Dulandi count their foster kin closer than their birth kin.

Sireth's mages govern Imnas, and they are drawn from the most accomplished mages of the culture. The people of the forests still count them as Dulandi, but they are removed from the people they govern, living aloft among their schools and libraries while the households below keep the old ways.

## Values & Beliefs

The Dulandi hold three values above the rest.

*Dúchas*, birthright, is the gift of magic each person is born with and the bond between a household and its forest. Dúchas is never earned and can never be taken away, but it can be wasted, and a Dulandi who leaves their gift untended is pitied more than any other.

*Crefft*, craft, is the gift made great by work. Study, practice, and mastery are how a Dulandi honors their dúchas, and the skill a person builds is the measure of their standing.

*Cof*, memory, is the long memory of the oldest culture still strong. Storytellers called *seanchaí* keep the histories of their households and the old tales of the forest in their heads, and a household that forgets its own past is thought to have lost part of itself.

The Dulandi hold that the self, the *anian*, does not live in the body. A Mannee can change their shape to many other kinds and variations of humanoid, so a face or a form says little about who someone is. A person is known by their name and by their magic, which carries a character of its own that those who know them can recognize in any shape.

The greatest taboo is the taking of another's name, passing oneself off as another person by name and claim. In a culture where any shape can be worn, the name is what others trust, and a Dulandi who steals one shames their whole aelwyd.

The Dulandi worship the Primordials, and [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Murasakino, Purple Primordial Spirit\|Murasakino]] above them all, since Murasakino gave magic to mortals and the Dulandi hold that gift as their birthright. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Midori, Green Primordial Spirit\|Midori]] is honored for the forests and [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit\|Kuro]] at every death. The Dulandi know the old stories that name [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arloya, God of Devotion\|Arloya]] the Angel of the Azure Expanse and regard her place in the lore with respect, but they keep their worship for the Primordials.

The leylines are Murasakino's gift flowing through the land, and the Dulandi speak of them with reverence. Some follow them. The *crwydriaid*, wanderers, are semi-nomadic scholars who move with the leylines as they shift, studying their currents and settling for a season wherever the magic runs strongest.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Magic in Daily Life

Magic runs through every part of a Dulandi day. Fires are lit with a word, rain is coaxed onto dry fields, and living wood is shaped into walls and stairs. Children learn small workings the way children elsewhere learn to fetch water, and a household's chores are shared out by what each member can do. A visitor from another culture notices first how little anyone remarks on it.

### Language and Courtesy

The Dulandi greet one another with *dangos*, the showing. Each speaks their name and offers a small mark of their magic, a spark of light, a breath of wind, or a curl of green, so the other knows them whatever shape they wear. Old friends often skip the name and show only the magic, as a mark of how well they know each other.

The language has no gendered words, since the Mannee are entirely unisex. Its speech is musical, and the Dulandi set much of what they wish to remember to verse and song.

### Homes and Villages

Dulandi homes are built in and throughout the trees, or into the hills so that they blend with the land around them. Many are shaped from living wood, grown and coaxed into walls, stairs, and platforms over generations. The oldest stand in trees older than any record, and a household's tree is as much a part of it as its name. Villages spread through the forest, joined by walkways and gliding platforms, and from the ground a large one can be hard to see at all.

Sireth stands apart. It is a modern city of stone and glass, built like other great cities, with schools, libraries, and the halls of the mages who govern Imnas. The households of the forest look up at it as a foreign place, though it is their own capital.

### Life Events

A newborn is given a childhood name, which they keep until they come of age. Their first small working, often a spark or a breath of wind within their first years, is celebrated by the whole household.

A young Dulandi comes of age when their pennaeth judges them ready. They make their first full glide from the household's highest platform before their kin, and on landing they speak the adult name they have chosen for themselves. The childhood name is set aside from that day, and calling an adult by it is a deep insult.

Each death is honored with a wake. The household gathers to keen for them, and the seanchaí recites the deeds of their life so they pass into the household's memory. The body is laid among the roots of the forest and given back to Kuro.

### Food

The Dulandi table is simple and drawn from the forest. *Cawl*, a slow stew of roots, greens, and meat, is the everyday meal, eaten with oatcakes and dark bread. Honey, nuts, berries, and mushrooms gathered from the forest are prized, and mead is poured at every feast. Hospitality is warm but plain, and a guest is offered bread and honey on arrival.

### Dress and Adornment

A Mannee's body can change shape at will, so Dulandi clothing is draped and wrapped, never fitted to one form. Cloaks, wrapped tunics, and long scarves in forest colors are common to every household. Cloaks are held with a ring brooch, and the brooch carries the knotwork mark of the wearer's aelwyd, so a Dulandi's household can be read even when their shape cannot.

### Arts and Architecture

Music and verse are the highest Dulandi arts. The harp is played in every household of standing, and the finest bards weave magic into their songs. Knotwork is carved into living wood throughout a village, and the patterns of a household's carvings tell its history to those who can read them.

### Festivals

*Calan Gaeaf*, the first day of winter, opens the year. Households put out every fire and relight them together from one flame, and the night is held to be the one when the veil between the living and the dead grows thin. Families leave a place at the table for their dead and tell their stories until dawn.

The *Eisteddfod* is the great gathering of the Dulandi, held each summer. Households across Imnas send their best to compete in verse, music, and magic, and the finest bard or mage of the year is chaired, given a carved seat and the honor of the gathering. Winning the chair can raise an aelwyd's standing for a generation.

## Cultural Identity

A Dulandi is known first by their name and their magic, and second by their aelwyd. Shapes change, but the name a person chose at their coming of age and the character of their magic do not, and those two things are what the Dulandi trust. A Dulandi abroad may wear any form the company requires and still feel wholly themselves.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The gap between Sireth and the forests is the oldest tension in the culture. The mages who govern Imnas live in a modern city above the trees, and the households below feel that their rulers have grown distant from the people they rule. Sireth's mages, for their part, find the forest households slow to change.

A second gap runs between mages and non-mages. Those who cannot work magic are not scorned, but they have little say, and some argue that a culture which prizes crefft should judge them by other crafts as well. Traditionalists answer that dúchas is the heart of the Dulandi, and a culture that forgot it would no longer be Dulandi at all.

The Dulandi regard their [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Portizian\|Portizian]] neighbors as a young people, lively and ambitious but short of memory. Relations with the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Aj'Snaga\|Aj'Snaga]] are cooler. About four hundred years ago Imnas took the easternmost island from [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Yoichizu\|Yoichizu]] in a conflict, and the island is still Aj'Snaga in culture. The Dulandi count it an old matter long settled, and the Aj'Snaga do not.

## Distinctive Traits

- ***Dangos***, the greeting of a spoken name and a small mark of magic
- **Ring brooches** carrying the knotwork mark of a household
- **Small workings** used without remark in every daily task
- **A chosen adult name**, with the childhood name set aside for good
- **Harp and verse** at every gathering, often woven with magic
