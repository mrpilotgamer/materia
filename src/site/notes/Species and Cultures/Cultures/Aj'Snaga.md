## Overview

Aj'Snaga culture is built around death and return. The [[Species and Cultures/Races/Araki\|Araki]] who make up nearly all of its people rise again a month after dying, as young adults who remember almost nothing of who they were. The Aj'Snaga treat each return as a reincarnation, a new person who carries the old one's family name and a few fragments of the old one's memory. They are a warrior people of clans and castles, of sea and forge, and they prize courage and honor all the more because every death still costs a person their whole self.

## Origins & Distribution

Aj'Snaga culture began on the eastern side of what is now [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Yoichizu\|Yoichizu]], in the wetlands of the northeastern peninsula. Its first clans lived on raised houses above the marshes and grew rice in the flooded fields, and from there the culture spread along the coasts and through the forests of the eastern arm and south to the bay at Killow.

The Araki are the great majority of the Aj'Snaga by a wide margin. Members of other races live among them in small numbers, and their deaths are mourned with a weight the Araki reserve for no one else, since they will not return.

The culture also holds the easternmost island of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Imnas\|Imnas]], which Imnas took from Yoichizu in a conflict about four hundred years ago. The islanders are loyal to the culture and keep its customs, but after four centuries, and with so few of them, they are mostly indifferent to who rules them.

## Rebirth

When an Araki dies and their body lies undisturbed for a month, they rise again as a young adult with almost no memory of their past life. The Aj'Snaga call the returned *umarekawari*, the reborn, and treat each return as a reincarnation. The reborn is a new person. Their old rank, standing, marriages, debts, and grudges are wiped clean, and they owe nothing for the deeds of the life before.

Yet something carries over. The reborn keeps the family name of their clan and belongs to it still, though they start again at its lowest standing. Old friends often seek them out and try to win their friendship anew, and many succeed. Each reborn takes a new given name for their new life, and the clan records the name of every life its members have lived.

The month between death and return is the *tsuya*, the vigil. The body is laid out in the family's house or a quiet room of the clan, and family or close friends keep watch over it day and night until it wakes. Keeping a long tsuya faithfully is a deep act of love, and those who keep it usually choose the reborn's new name.

Fragments of memory that survive rebirth are called *kakera*, shards. The Aj'Snaga honor them as all that remains of the person who came before. A reborn who speaks a shard, a name, a song, or a skill they never learned, is listened to with reverence, and families write the shards down beside the record of the life they came from.

## Social Structure

The foundation of Aj'Snaga society is the clan, the *uji*. A clan is a great extended family sharing a name, a crest, lands or ships, and a history that runs through many lives of its members. Its head, the *sōryō*, leads it in war and peace and speaks for it to the clans above and below.

Warriors, the *bushi*, stand highest. Below them come the smiths, held in more esteem among the Aj'Snaga than in most cultures, then the farmers and fishers, and last the merchants. A clan's place in that order passes to its reborn members along with its name, but within the clan every reborn must earn rank again from the beginning. A great general who dies returns as the lowest of their clan's warriors.

The sea clans hold the coasts. These are bushi clans whose lands are harbors, straits, and islands, and whose warriors fight from the decks of their ships. They command Yoichizu's fleets, and their captains count among the most honored bushi of all.

## Values & Beliefs

The Aj'Snaga hold three values above the rest.

*Yūki*, courage, is the will to face death. Death returns an Araki's body but takes everything else they were, so a warrior who stands their ground risks their whole self as surely as anyone of another race. The Aj'Snaga count that courage no less real for the return.

*Meiyo*, honor, is a person's good name and their clan's. A life lived with honor is recorded in the clan's histories under that life's name, and a death met bravely brings honor to the clan for generations.

*Chūgi*, loyalty, binds each person to their clan and each clan to its lord. Loyalty does not survive a death, since the reborn owe nothing, so it is given anew in each life and valued all the more for it.

The greatest taboo is the destruction of the dead. To destroy an Araki's brain during the tsuya ends them forever, and doing it to anyone who has not committed a grave injustice is the worst crime the Aj'Snaga know. In rare cases it is done deliberately, as the final punishment of one who has wronged their people, and the Aj'Snaga call that punishment *shūen*, the ending.

Close behind it comes the holding of a reborn to the deeds of their past life. Taking vengeance on a reborn for a wrong their former self committed, or demanding they pay a former self's debt, shames the one who does it and their clan.

The Aj'Snaga honor [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arloya, God of Devotion\|Arloya]] as the god of loyal service, and many bushi dedicate their swords to them. Among the Primordials they honor [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Aoi, Blue Primordial Spirit\|Aoi]] for the sea that carries their ships and [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Midori, Green Primordial Spirit\|Midori]] as the spirit of life who returns them.

The Aj'Snaga do not worship [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Kuro, Black Primordial Spirit\|Kuro]]. Many of Kuro's followers see the Araki's escape from death as unnatural, and a few go as far as to rank them with liches and other undead that cannot die, a comparison the Aj'Snaga resent more than almost any insult.

The Aj'Snaga regard magic as a tool of war and craft. Battle mages serve alongside bushi, and smiths who can work magic into a blade are among the most honored craftsmen in the culture. Healing magic is prized as well, since it can spare a person the loss of a life.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Courtesy

The Aj'Snaga bow in greeting, lower to those of higher rank. When an old friend meets someone newly reborn, they greet them as a stranger with the words *hajimemashite*, "I meet you for the first time," and introduce themselves from the beginning. The courtesy honors the reborn as a new person, and the friendship that grows afterward is counted a new one.

### Homes and Towns

In the wetlands, houses stand on stilts above the water and the rice fields, linked by raised walkways and boats. Elsewhere, Aj'Snaga houses are built of timber with deep eaves, paper screens, and sliding doors, and floors of woven mats. Each clan of standing keeps a castle, and towns grow up around them, the bushi living closest to the walls and the merchants farthest out, and Killow is the greatest castle town of them all.

### The Sea and the Forge

Aj'Snaga fleets mix swift boats for raiding with great armored warships carrying hundreds of bushi, built like floating castles with towers for archers. Life at sea belongs mostly to the sea clans and the fishing villages, though most Aj'Snaga live within reach of the coast.

The forge holds a place in the culture second only to the sword it makes. Aj'Snaga smiths fold and refine their steel through many stages, and a great blade is named, recorded, and passed down through a clan for generations. Most of the iron they work comes from [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Pimestalia\|Pimestalia]]. The Aj'Snaga find the dependence unfortunate, but they have no other choice without stirring conflict, and the trade continues.

### Life Events

A child born to an Araki family comes of age at fifteen in the *genpuku*, when they take an adult name and are given their first sword or tools of their trade.

A reborn has no childhood and no genpuku. When they wake, those who kept the tsuya give them their new name, and the clan receives them in a short ceremony as its newest member.

A marriage ends with a death, since the reborn owe nothing to their former self's vows. A widowed spouse may court the reborn anew, and such second courtships are a favorite subject of Aj'Snaga songs and plays.

### Food

Rice is the heart of the Aj'Snaga table, grown in the paddies of the wetlands and eaten at every meal. Fish, seaweed, pickled vegetables, and soups of fermented bean paste accompany it, and rice wine is poured at feasts and ceremonies. Tea is prepared and served with great care, and the serving of tea to a guest is an art of its own.

### Dress and Adornment

Everyday dress is a wrapped robe tied with a sash, worn with wide trousers by bushi. Every clan has its *kamon*, a crest worn on clothing, banners, and armor, and an Aj'Snaga can name the clans of a hundred crests at a glance. Bushi wear two swords as the mark of their class.

### Arts

The Aj'Snaga stage plays of past lives, in which masked actors portray the dead speaking through the shards of the living. Ink painting, poetry, and the arrangement of gardens are practiced by bushi as well as artists, and a warrior who cannot write a poem is thought only half educated.

### Festivals

The greatest festival is the Festival of Lanterns, held each summer in honor of those who came before, the past lives of the living. Each family writes the names of its members' former lives on paper lanterns and sets them afloat on the sea or the river at dusk. Reborn often join in for lives they cannot remember, and some find a shard waking as the lanterns drift away.

## Cultural Identity

An Aj'Snaga is known first by their clan. The family name is the one thing that passes through every death unchanged, and with it come a crest, a history, and a place among the clans. A given name belongs to one life only. The clan name belongs to all of them, and the Aj'Snaga say a person is the latest life of their clan.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The Aj'Snaga argue over the shards. Traditionalists hold that the past life is finished and the shards are to be honored, never pursued. Others seek out their shards deliberately, through meditation, old records, and the company of those who knew their former selves, hoping to recover what they were. Traditionalists see in this a refusal to live one's own life.

The sea clans and the land clans compete for honor and for the favor of their lords. The sea clans hold the fleets, and the land clans hold most of the castles.

The Aj'Snaga look on the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dulandi\|Dulandi]] with some coolness. The island Imnas took four centuries ago is remembered on the mainland, even if the islanders themselves have stopped caring, and the Dulandi honor Kuro at every death, which sits poorly with a people Kuro's faithful call unnatural. Relations with the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Portizian\|Portizian]] nations are businesslike, though no Aj'Snaga likes that the iron of their swords comes from Pimestalia.

## Distinctive Traits

- **The tsuya**, a month-long vigil over the dead until they wake
- ***Hajimemashite*** spoken to a reborn friend, to begin again
- **Clan crests** on clothing, banners, sails, and armor
- **Named blades** passed down through a clan for generations
- **Lanterns floated at dusk** for the past lives of the living
