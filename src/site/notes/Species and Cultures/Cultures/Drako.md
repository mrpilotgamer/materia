## Overview

Drako is the culture of the dragons and of those who live among them. It began with the dragons of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Narathatath\|Narathatath]] and was taken up by the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Drakmed\|Drakmed]], who are believed to be the humanoid descendants of dragons. Its people regard the dragons who rule them with loyalty and pride, and a dragon old enough to rule is obeyed.

Drako is the dominant culture of Narathatath, where dragons fly through the major cities and a council of ten dragons governs. Collecting sits at the heart of Drako life. Every dragon keeps a hoard, and many of the Drakmed keep collections of their own.

## Origins & Distribution

Drako culture is believed to be as old as dragons themselves, which would make it one of the oldest cultures in [[Places/Realms/Alora/Alora\|Alora]]. It began in the mountains of Narathatath, on the western continent of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Crimson Lands\|The Crimson Lands]], and it remains a highland culture today.

Before 4287, dragons ruled both continents of the Crimson Lands, and humans lived under Drako rule. The Human Uprising of that year broke dragon rule on the eastern continent and founded [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/The Rayhostian Theocracy\|The Rayhostian Theocracy]], and few humans remained in Narathatath afterward. Along the coasts of the strait between the two nations, Drako blends with [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Anor\|Anor]] into the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Trow\|Trow]] culture.

## Social Structure

Dragons are the ruling class. The council of ten governs Narathatath as a whole, and beyond the council, the Drako defer to any dragon old enough to command respect. Standing among dragons grows with age, and the oldest are held to be the wisest and the strongest.

Daily life is organized around holds. A hold is the circle of households attached to a single dragon, which offers protection and a share of its wealth in return for service. Members of a hold work as stewards, smiths, gem-cutters, scribes, and keepers of the hoard, and their standing depends on how close their service brings them to the dragon.

Allegiance in a hold is personal and follows the dragon. A Drakmed who falls out of favor, or who is courted by an older dragon, may leave one hold for another, and a family may serve several dragons over the generations.

Drakmed families trace their descent through the kind of dragon they claim as an ancestor. Scale color and the nature of a family's breath, whether acid, cold, lightning, or fire, are marks of that lineage and points of pride.

## Values & Beliefs

[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Riavexect, God of Want\|Riavexect, God of Want]] is the patron of the Drako. Dragons collect by nature, and many Drakmed follow their example, keeping collections of coin, gems, crafted objects, or knowledge, though not all of them do.

Drako value age and possession above most things. A long life is held to bring wisdom and power, and what a person keeps is taken as a measure of their worth. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel]] is widely worshiped for the god's teaching that anger should be channeled and used. The Drako count controlled wrath as a strength, and a display of temper that serves no purpose is seen as a mark of youth.

The gravest Drako taboo is theft from a hoard. Stealing from a dragon is held to be the worst crime a person can commit, and even taking from a Drakmed's modest collection brings lasting shame.

Most Drako regard the Theocracy as a nation of traitors or as a rival, and opinion divides between the two. The humans of the Theocracy are remembered as a people who once lived under dragon rule and chose to leave it.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language and Gesture

The Drako speak Draconic, the language of dragons.

Draconic is written in a script suited to engraving, and the Drako record what matters in stone and metal. Contracts, genealogies, and the inventories of hoards are engraved on metal plates meant to outlast their writers, and a hold's archive of plates is kept alongside its hoard.

A younger person greets an elder with a bow, turning the head to one side to show the neck, a gesture taken from the way young dragons yield to older ones. Equals exchange a shallower bow, and an elder answers either greeting with a nod.

### Life Events

Drako youth come of age by presenting a first keeping to the dragon of their hold. A first keeping is the first piece of a collection acquired by one's own effort, and the dragon's acceptance of it marks the youth as an adult member of the hold. Among dragons, adulthood comes when a young dragon leaves its parent's lair to claim a lair and hoard of its own.

Marriages are made by contract. The terms are engraved and witnessed, and each family gives pieces from its collection to the other as a pledge.

The dead are interred in tombs cut into the mountains, with a chosen piece of their collection beside them, and the rest passes to their heirs by the terms of an engraved will. The death of a dragon is a far larger matter. Its hoard must be divided or claimed, and the inheritance can unsettle a hold for years.

### Food

Narathatath has little farmable land, and much of its food is imported, both to cover the shortfall and to satisfy the dragons' demand for variety. Drako cooking leans heavily on imported spices, and the Drako take pride in dishes that are strongly seasoned.

What grows locally comes from terraced plots on the mountainsides and from herds kept on the high pastures.

### Dress and Adornment

Gems and precious metals are plentiful in Narathatath, and jewelry is worn at every rank. The Drako treat it as a collection carried on the body, and the quality of what a person wears shows their standing. Members of a hold often wear a token in the color of their dragon.

### Arts and Architecture

The most esteemed Drako crafts are smithing and gem-cutting. The masterwork weapons, armor, and jewelry of Narathatath are made under the supervision of dragons, and dragon-guarded vaults hold goods of great value for those who pay for their keeping.

Drako cities are built for dragons. Buildings rise in stepped terraces cut into the mountainsides, with broad flat roofs and platforms at their summits where dragons land and rest. Great halls have doorways and vaulted ceilings sized for a dragon to enter, and courtyards stand open to the sky.

## Cultural Identity

A Drakmed is known by the dragon they serve, the draconic ancestry their family claims, and the collection they have built. Of these, only the collection is entirely their own doing, and it is the one a Drakmed is most often asked about.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The oldest division in Drako culture runs between chromatic and metallic dragons. Chromatic dragons are held to be more selfish and metallic dragons more selfless, and the holds that serve each tend to take on the character of their dragon. The rivalry has not broken into open conflict, and by tradition both kinds share the council five seats apiece.

Inheritance is a second source of strain. A dragon's hoard is the largest fortune in its hold, and when a dragon dies, rival heirs, older dragons, and the council may all press a claim to it.

Outsiders often find the Drako attention to possessions unsettling. A Drako host may know the worth of every object in the house, and a guest can expect to be asked what they collect early in any acquaintance.

## Distinctive Traits

- **A bow with the neck shown** when greeting an elder
- **A personal collection**, kept by most, however modest
- **Engraved records**, with contracts and inventories set down in metal
- **A hold token** worn in the color of the dragon a person serves
- **"What do you keep?"**, a common question between new acquaintances

## Example Names

**Drakmed:** Kazrethar, Ondrasil, Thuranek, Veshkaal, Gorvaruun, Irraketh, Mirvaane, Azhrekka, Neshtuul, Qaravex, Sahrezin, Tovraal, Khessadra, Zaarkhen, Isharreth, Umbrasha

**Dragons:** Dragon names follow the pattern of the council, such as Vraxthaleon, Nethysara, and Solanneth, and are often paired with an earned epithet like "the Ironforge" or "the Wise."

*Note: A Drakmed is named by a given name and their hold (e.g., "Veshkaal of Vraxthaleon's hold"). A Drakmed who changes holds takes the new dragon's name, and their old hold is remembered only in the engraved genealogies.*

## Pronunciation

Drako (DRAY-koh), Draconic (drah-KON-ick)
