## Overview

Trow is the culture of the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Trox\|Trox]] who came up from the deep caverns beneath [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Crimson Lands\|The Crimson Lands]]. It began underground as a culture of hunters, and it has changed slowly on the surface, where the Trow live between the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Drako\|Drako]] of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Narathatath\|Narathatath]] and the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Anor\|Anor]] of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/The Rayhostian Theocracy\|The Rayhostian Theocracy]].

The Trow are a tribal people, living in small holds bound by kinship. They are the builders of the region and, by necessity, its sailors. They see themselves as one people divided into two communities, one on each shore of the strait that separates the two nations.

## Origins & Distribution

Trow culture took shape in the deep caverns, where the Trox hunted the monsters of the underground. Its people surfaced on both shores of the strait within a few years of each other, and the stories passed down since hold that they came up fleeing something. What they fled is not told, or is not known.

On the surface, the Trow found themselves between two older cultures, and both have left their mark. From the Drako they took the word hold for their tribes, and from the Anor a respect for [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Mendura, God of Courage\|Mendura]]. The Trow remain most common along the coasts of the strait, on the eastern shores of Narathatath and the western shores of the Theocracy.

## Social Structure

Trow society is tribal. The Trow call their tribes holds, since the Drako hold resembled the tribes they had known underground. A hold is an extended kin group that lives, works, and hunts together.

A hold is led by whoever it most respects, often its most capable hunter or builder, and the position is not inherited. Decisions of weight are reached by consensus among the adults of the hold. What the hold gathers or earns is shared among its members, and no one eats while others in the hold go hungry.

A Trow hold sits within the territory of a dragon in Narathatath or a noble in the Theocracy. It owes that ruler its duties but manages its own internal affairs as it sees fit.

## Values & Beliefs

[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel]] has long been the main god of the Trow, and the god is said to favor the form of a Trox. The Trow do not always honor the three gods of the Anor, but they respect Mendura. Those in Narathatath pay heed to [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Riavexect, God of Want\|Riavexect]] out of respect for the Drako who worship them.

Sharing is the first Trow value, and the gravest Trow taboo is hoarding food or goods while the hold is in need. Alfugel's teaching that anger should be channeled suited a people who hunted monsters in the dark. The Trow hold that anger belongs to the hunt and the task, while anger turned on one's own hold is a disgrace.

The memory of the surfacing runs deep. The Trow have been to the caverns since, but they go with hesitation, since each generation has been told that their ancestors were fleeing something below.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language and Gesture

The Trow speak their own language, carried up with them from the caverns.

The Trow greet one another by raising their four smaller hands open and empty. The gesture comes from the dark of the caverns, where showing that no small hand held a blade or a tool was a courtesy between hunters.

### Craft and Work

The Trow are builders. Trox engineering gives the Theocracy its reputation for stone masonry and large-scale construction, and the Trow bring the same skill to both shores.

The Trow are sailors by necessity. They lack the flight that carries dragons across the strait, and the boats of the Trow are how most traffic between the two shores moves. The Trow of Narathatath's eastern shores helped establish the nation's new navy.

The smaller hands do the fine work of Trow craft. Carvers work the bone and chitin of hunted monsters into tools, charms, and figures. Knotting, netting, and fine joinery are done with the small hands while the larger arms hold the work steady.

### Crossing the Strait

Crossing between the two communities has never been free. Tension between the two nations has kept the Trow of each shore apart for most of their history, though crossings have become more common in recent years.

## Cultural Identity

A Trow belongs first to their hold, then to their people across both shores, and only then to the nation whose territory they live in. The surfacing is the story every Trow knows, told from both shores in much the same way, and it is the thread that ties the two communities into one people.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The oldest Trow argue that the old ways are thinning. Young Trow on each shore take up more of the customs of their neighbors, such as Drako collecting in Narathatath or Anor piety in the Theocracy. Elders fear the two communities will drift until they are two peoples.

The caverns divide the Trow as well. Most hold that whatever drove their ancestors up is best left undisturbed. A smaller number believe the Trow will not be whole until they know what they fled, and some of them have gone below to find out.

## Distinctive Traits

- **The open-hand greeting**, four smaller hands raised open and empty
- **Holds**, the Drako word the Trow adopted for their tribes
- **Carved bone and chitin**, worked from the monsters of the deep
- **Boats across the strait**, the Trow way of crossing what dragons fly over
- **The surfacing story**, told on both shores
