## Overview

Portizian culture is a culture of many peoples living together. It began among humans on the coast of what is now [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Imestalia\|Imestalia]], but it has taken in so many others over the centuries that no race is a majority any longer. Its people prize honor, the law of their towns, and the shared life of neighbors of every kind, and they have spent two centuries arguing over what those values require. Two nations share the culture today, and each holds the other has it wrong.

## Origins & Distribution

Portizian culture began on the coast around Quichollow, among humans who settled there long ago. Some historians believe those first settlers crossed the strait from [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Crimson Lands\|The Crimson Lands]], but no record proves it, and the question is still argued in the universities. The culture spread inland from the coast, along the river valleys, onto the grasslands around the mountains, and across the eastern slopes to the shores of Morlas Bay.

As it spread, it took in every people it met. Settlers of other races were welcomed into Portizian towns on the same terms as the first families, and in time the culture stopped belonging to any one race. Today Portizian is dominant in both Imestalia and [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Pimestalia\|Pimestalia]], and its people include [[Species and Cultures/Races/Human\|Humans]], [[Species and Cultures/Races/Mannee\|Mannee]], [[Species and Cultures/Races/Araki\|Araki]], [[Species and Cultures/Races/Daemedi\|Daemedi]], and many others, none of them a majority.

In 5240 civil war split the Portizian lands in two. Pimestalia broke away, arguing that the nobles of Imestalia were abusing their power and causing strife, and that power should be spread among the people. The culture survived the split whole, but the two nations now read its values in opposite ways. Imestalia holds to centralized order, and Pimestalia holds to the freedoms of its people.

## Social Structure

The foundation of Portizian society is the family. A family shares its honor, its property, and its name, and the deeds of one member reflect on all the rest. Families of long standing keep careful records of their lineage, and even humble families can name their grandparents' grandparents.

In Imestalia the old nobility endures. The great noble houses hold lands and offices under the crown, and below them the lesser nobles, the *hidalgos*, hold titles with little land and much pride. In Pimestalia the founders broke with that tradition at the founding. Pimestalia keeps no titles, and its offices are filled by election.

Every Portizian town is governed by its *concejo*, the council of its *vecinos*, the citizens who live there and have sworn to its laws. The concejo chooses or confirms the *alcaldes*, the magistrates who judge disputes and keep order. Each town holds a *fuero*, a charter setting out its laws and its people's rights, and Portizians guard their fueros jealously. In Imestalia the crown or a lord grants the fuero and confirms the alcaldes, while in Pimestalia the vecinos elect them and the fuero is held to belong to the town alone.

## Values & Beliefs

The Portizians hold three values above the rest, and the two nations read each of them differently.

*Honra*, honor, is the good name of a family. It is won through loyalty, courage, and keeping one's word, and lost through cowardice, deceit, or betrayal. Every Portizian guards their family's honra, and a slight against it is not forgotten.

*Fuero*, law, is the charter of rights and duties that binds a town together. To an Imestalian, fuero means order, a law granted from above and kept by all. To a Pimestalian, it means liberty, the rights each person holds and no lord may take.

*Convivencia*, living together, is the shared life of neighbors of every people. A Portizian town counts its many races as a strength, and a good neighbor is one who keeps the peace of the street whatever their blood. Both nations honor convivencia, and each claims the other neglects it.

The greatest taboo is to turn a vecino out for their race or people. Driving away a neighbor who has sworn to the town's fuero breaks the oldest promise of Portizian life, and the family that does it loses its honra in every town that hears of it.

[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arloya, God of Devotion\|Arloya]] is the most honored god among the Portizians. Their temples stand in every town, and their faithful servants, the knights and the officials who serve with loyalty, look to them for their reward. Each nation honors them in its own way. Imestalians pray to them as the patron of loyal service to the crown, and Pimestalians as the patron of loyalty to one's neighbors and one's cause. [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Maltrunity, God of Benevolence\|Maltrunity]] is honored as well, and the brotherhoods that keep their alms feed the poor of every town.

The Portizians regard magic as an *oficio*, a trade like smithing or law, learned through study and practiced for a living. Mages are respected professionals, and every town of any size keeps one or more for hire. Mannee born into Portizian towns are known to have a gift for it, and many take up the trade, but the culture judges them by their skill as it would any other craftsman.

## Daily Life & Customs

### Language and Courtesy

It is a Portizian courtesy to greet a person in their own language, even if only with a word or two. Most Portizians know greetings in several tongues, picked up from neighbors, and a traveler who hears their own language spoken in welcome in a Portizian market is not soon to forget it. A greeting in plain Portizian is never rude, but a greeting in one's own tongue is a mark of real regard.

### Towns and Homes

Portizian towns are built around the *plaza mayor*, a great square where markets, festivals, and the meetings of the concejo take place. Walls ring the older towns, and castles stand over the river valleys and the passes of the mountains, so many that the Portizian lands are crowded with them. Houses are built of stone, brick, or whitewashed earth with tiled roofs, and the better ones open onto a shaded courtyard.

Builders of many peoples work side by side, and each brings the crafts of their own people. A single Portizian temple may hold carving in one people's style, tilework in another's, and a roof built by a third, and the Portizians count such buildings among their proudest.

### The Mesta

The grasslands around the mountains are herding country, and each year great flocks of sheep move between the high summer pastures and the lowland winter ones. They travel along the *cañadas*, broad droving roads protected by law, under the care of the *Mesta*, the herders' guild. The cañadas cross the border between the two nations, and the Mesta is one of the few bodies that still answers to both.

### Life Events

A newborn is named at Arloya's temple before their *padrinos*, godparents who promise to stand by the child for life. The bond of *compadrazgo* joins the godparents and the parents as well, and families often choose padrinos of another race or people to bind their households together.

At a wedding the couple exchange *arras*, thirteen coins passed between them as a pledge to share all they own. The wedding is held at Arloya's temple and followed by a feast in the family's house or the town's plaza.

When a Portizian dies, family and neighbors hold a vigil through the night beside the body. The dead are buried the next day, and the family keeps a year of mourning in dark clothes.

### Food

The Portizian table runs on bread, olive oil, wine, and garlic. *Cocido*, a slow stew of chickpeas, meat, and vegetables, is the everyday meal of the countryside, and roast lamb from the Mesta's flocks is the meal of every feast. Sheep's cheese, cured ham, and saffron are prized, and the coast eats fish and shellfish stewed with rice. Portizian cooks borrow freely from the kitchens of every people among them, and a town's dishes often name the people they came from.

### Dress and Adornment

Everyday dress is a wool tunic and mantle, from the Mesta's flocks, worn in the earthy colors of the dyers. Imestalian nobles wear fine cloth and furs and display their family's arms. Pimestalians favor plain dress as a point of principle, and a Pimestalian official in rich clothes draws suspicion.

### Arts

The *romances*, ballads of heroes, feuds, and old wars, are sung in every plaza, and new ones are still composed about the civil war. Songs of devotion to Arloya are gathered into great songbooks, often illuminated. In the universities, scholars translate the books of many peoples into Portizian, and the translators' schools are among the most respected in the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Azure Expanse\|Azure Expanse]] outside Imnas.

### Festivals

The *romería* is a pilgrimage and festival in one. Each spring a town walks out together to a shrine of Arloya in the countryside, with music, banners, and wagons of food, and spends the day feasting before walking home.

Each town also keeps the day of its fuero, when the alcaldes read the charter aloud in the plaza mayor and the vecinos swear to it again. In Pimestalia the founding of the nation in 5240 is kept as a great festival, while in Imestalia the same day passes without any mark at all.

## Cultural Identity

A Portizian is known first by their family and second by their town. The name a person carries, and the fuero they have sworn to, tell other Portizians whose honra they share and whose law they keep. Race comes a distant third, and a Portizian who asked a neighbor's race before their family would be thought ill-bred.

## Adaptation & Tensions

The deepest division is the one between the two nations, and Portizians do not agree on what it is. Some see one people divided only by the government each wants. Others see two peoples who share a language and little else, each with its own ideals. The two views run through families, towns, and the universities, and many Portizians have changed their minds more than once.

Old families who trace their line to the first settlers sometimes look down on newcomers, whatever their race, and newcomers resent the slights. Convivencia forbids turning anyone out, but it does not forbid snobbery, and the old families make the most of the gap.

The farmers of the valleys and the herders of the Mesta have quarreled for generations. Flocks on the move trample fields, and the Mesta's rights along the cañadas are older than most farms.

The Portizians admire the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Dulandi\|Dulandi]] for their magic and resent them for their aloofness. Many Portizian mages train in the schools of Imnas, and come home with a respect for the Dulandi they keep to themselves. With the [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Aj'Snaga\|Aj'Snaga]] relations are businesslike. Pimestalian iron feeds the forges of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Yoichizu\|Yoichizu]], which gives the Pimestalians a hold on their neighbor that the Imestalians watch closely.

## Distinctive Traits

- **A greeting in the other's own tongue**, however few the words
- **The plaza mayor** at the heart of every town
- **Two surnames**, one from each parent
- **Padrinos chosen across peoples** to bind families together
- **Romances sung in the plaza**, old and new

*Note: Portizians use a given name followed by two surnames, the first from one parent and the second from the other (e.g., Jimena Díaz Ruiz). Old families often add the place their line comes from (e.g., Rodrigo Díaz de Quichollow). In Imestalia nobles are addressed as don or doña. Pimestalia abolished the titles, and its people address one another as vecino or vecina.*
