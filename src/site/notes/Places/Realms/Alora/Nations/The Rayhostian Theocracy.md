# The Rayhostian Theocracy

<!-- GAP: The current monarch is a descendant of Queen Seraphina. Name and title are left open. -->

## Overview

The Rayhostian Theocracy holds the eastern continent of [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Crimson Lands\|The Crimson Lands]]. It was founded in 4287, when a human uprising broke dragon rule over the region and divided the old dragon-ruled territory in two. The Theocracy has been ruled primarily by humans ever since, and it has been home to the largest [[Species and Cultures/Races/Trox\|Trox]] population for the past few hundred years.

The nation worships three gods together: [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Mendura, God of Courage\|Mendura]], [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arloya, God of Devotion\|Arloya]], and [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Coptimaine, God of Belief\|Coptimaine]]. The faith is written into its government, its law, and its schools. A monarch reigns by divine mandate, but major decisions require the approval of both the nobility and the church.

## Geography

The Theocracy's continent is the larger of the two in the Crimson Lands, and it is mostly forest and plains. Its northern coasts face the polar seas, and a narrow strait to the west separates it from [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Narathatath\|Narathatath]]. A deep bay reaches far into the continent from the southeast, and the capital, Hearhand, stands near its head.

## Government

The government of the Theocracy has two sides, the nobility and the church. Administration and military affairs belong to the nobility, while the church sees to daily life and theological affairs and is described under Religion below.

A king or queen heads the nobility and is held to rule by divine mandate. Beneath the monarch stands a feudal hierarchy of dukes, counts, barons, and knights, and every noble must receive a religious blessing to hold position. The nobles hold the land, and the local Virtue Councils of [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Anor\|Anor]] villages and cities answer to the lord of their territory.

The two sides meet in the Celestial Court. Its members are drawn from the Divine Council of the church, joined by envoys sent from the dukes. The High Triad does not sit on the Court. Major decisions of state require the Court's approval from both sides. The Court also hears the gravest criminal cases and blesses the political alliances made through noble marriages.

## Religion

The church is organized in three tiers. At its head is the High Triad, three high priests, one for each god, whose decisions must be unanimous. Below them sits the Divine Council of senior clergy, some of whom also sit on the Celestial Court. Local temples keep Virtue Keepers, who specialize in one god while honoring all three, and traveling Triad Speakers carry the faith to remote areas.

The Great Temple stands in Hearhand, built to the three-sided plan of Anor temples with an altar to each god around a central sanctuary. Every citizen owes a tithe of one tenth of their income, which supports the temples and their aid to the poor. The Great Library, also in Hearhand, holds the nation's religious texts, historical chronicles, and treatises on virtue, along with correspondence with learned institutions across the realm.

## Law

The law of the Theocracy rests on the Sacred Codex, which interprets divine will through the three virtues. Its courts favor rehabilitation over punishment, and mediation is the preferred means of settling disputes, while community service allows redemption for most crimes. Serious offenses go before the Celestial Court, where they receive both secular and religious judgment.

A defendant may appeal to divine judgment through a Virtue Trial by completing tasks that demonstrate courage, devotion, or hope, and success can reduce or remove a punishment. Capital punishment is reserved for those who reject all three virtues through repeated and willful acts of evil, and it is carried out only after three trials and three appeals to divine mercy.

Arcane practitioners must register with the temple authorities and dedicate their abilities to virtuous purposes. The Circle of Virtue oversees magical practice to ensure that its users serve the good of the community.

## Military

The Radiant Guard combines military and religious enforcement. Its knights are sworn to all three gods and are expected to be moral examples as well as warriors, and its patrols protect the nation's trade routes. Knights train at military academies, where their education includes the study of virtue.

The Theocracy also maintains a capable navy.

## Economy

The Theocracy's fertile land produces a surplus, and it exports grain, livestock, and dairy. A fishing industry works the waters of the bay. Its hardwood forests of oak and maple supply lumber, and its quarries yield limestone and marble for worked stone and sculpture. Clay deposits support pottery and brickmaking, and iron ore is present in moderate quality.

Trox engineering gives the Theocracy a reputation for stone masonry and large-scale building projects, and it sells those services abroad. Religious artwork, illuminated texts, and virtue-certified crafts also command premium prices.

The Theocracy imports precious metals and luxury goods for temple ornamentation, incense, exotic spices, and fine fabrics for ceremonial use. It also imports books, along with metal tools and weapons.

The Merchant's Guild requires its members to swear virtue oaths of honest dealing, and foreign merchants pay virtue taxes that fund community works. Coins struck in the Theocracy bear the symbols of all three gods.

## Peoples and Culture

[[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Anor\|Anor]] culture is dominant across most of the Theocracy. Along the coasts of the strait facing Narathatath, the more common culture is [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Trow\|Trow]], the culture of the Trox who came up from the caverns, shaped over time by both Anor and [[Species and Cultures/Cultures/Drako\|Drako]]. Humans form the ruling majority, and the Trox population is the largest anywhere. Trox communities hold [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel]] in special reverence.

Children attend temple schools, and higher learning continues in seminaries for priests, military academies for knights, and civic schools for administrators. Epidemics are fought through a coordinated response by the temples and the government.

## History

The Theocracy was born in the Human Uprising of 4287, which split the dragon-ruled territory of the Crimson Lands into two nations. Anor tradition holds that the three gods aided the rebels in their victory.

The centuries that followed were marked by tension with Narathatath, though open conflict between the two nations was rare. Most events of note in that period were internal to the Theocracy.

In 5267 the party [[Adventures And Stories/Parties/Retribution\|Retribution]] crossed the ocean to warn of the faceless emperor ruling Adriacium, and the Theocracy sent reinforcements to [[Places/Realms/Alora/Regions/The Wildlands\|The Wildlands]]. Alongside mages from [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Imnas\|Imnas]], its soldiers helped halt an army of demons and devils inside the [[Places/Realms/Alora/Nations/Empire of Bornumia\|Empire of Bornumia]], and the army broke apart when Retribution banished [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Rouge, the Red Progenitor Demon\|Rouge]].

The two nations have kept an extended peace for the past 170 years. In that time they have established multiple trade treaties through the diplomatic efforts of Slyrsomyr the Bridgemaker, Narathatath's chief diplomat to the Theocracy. Relations have improved over the past century, though the two nations remain wary of each other.

## Capital

Hearhand, population 25,370, stands near the head of the deep bay that reaches into the continent from the southeast. It is the seat of the monarch and the home of the Great Temple and the Great Library.

## Pronunciation

Rayhostian (ray-HOH-stee-uhn), Hearhand (HEER-hand)
