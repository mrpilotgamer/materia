*"Hell, where sympathy withers and malice blooms"*
*"Home to devils"*
*"The plane where the rule of law reveals evil's heart"*

## Overview
The Nine Hells of Baator, called Baator or just Hell, give travelers more to imagine, treasure hunters more to want and paladins more to fight than any other plane. It is the plane of law and evil taken to their limit: cruelty planned in advance and carried out with care. Every devil in the Nine Hells answers to a higher law than itself, which in practice means every devil resents its rank and schemes to rise. Most will do anything, however foul, to get ahead. At the top sits [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Noir, the Black Progenitor Demon\|Noir]], who has never been beaten. That is the law of the Nine Hells.

Baator can match any lower plane for sheer variety of vileness. The devils say they are smarter, subtler and more dangerous than other fiends. A demon revels in drooling, mindless power. A devil always has an agenda, a plan of attack, and a carefully worked-out plan for revenge if it ever needs one.

Baator has nine layers, each lower than the last, like ledges stepping down into a deeper and deeper pit. Each step down gives a traveler a better view of the whole. The layers fit together like pieces of a puzzle, and every layer you descend shows more of how the puzzle fits. That temptation is itself a kind of evil.

The Nine Hells hold minor, major and noble devils. The Dark Eight are eight powerful pit fiends who command the main devil armies in the Blood War. Above them are the Lords of the Nine, each ruling one layer, with power somewhere between a great pit fiend and a demigod. All of them answer to Noir, Lord of the Nine, who rules from Nessus at the bottom.

The politics of Baator have swung back and forth for thousands of years, pushed along sometimes by clever speeches or quiet poison and sometimes by open war between devil armies. The city of Dis on the second layer has been besieged more than once. In all the time Noir has ruled from below, no revolt has managed to replace him, even though he once tricked his rivals into believing he had been overthrown.

Devils rule the Nine Hells now, but some sages think they took the plane from a much older, stranger race, now just called the ancient baatorians. What's left of that race may still live in remote corners of Hell.

## Divine Realms
**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Noir, the Black Progenitor Demon\|Noir]]**, Leader of the Strong, rules the Darkened Badlands in Nessus, the ninth layer, and from there he rules the whole of Hell's hierarchy. Noir is strength and hierarchy, and he is known to have some control over the laws of nature.

**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Riavexect, God of Want\|Riavexect]]**, God of Want, also makes a home in Baator.



## The Dark Eight
The Dark Eight are the generals of the Blood War, eight pit fiends who meet in council every sixty-six days. They are enormously powerful, and they still answer to the Lords of the Nine and, in the end, to Noir.

The eight are Baalzephon, Corin, Dagos, Furcas, Pearza, Zapan, Zaebos and Zimmimar. They command the devils under them and meet in the fortress of Malsheem in Nessus. Besides planning the next attack in the Blood War, they have a say in which devils get promoted.

When they aren't leading armies, which is rare, they live in Nessus. When they are out on hellish business, they often take the form of an evil-looking human.

## Planar Links
As on every lower plane, the River Styx runs through Avernus, the top layer. Branches and waterfalls of it can be found on every layer.

Portals to other planes are fairly common and usually look like free-standing rings of red light. A well-known gate stands in the town of Ribcage in the Outlands. Travelers usually need an invitation from a Lord of the Nine to get through this Cursed Gate, though the devil who guards it, Paracs, can be bribed. A landing of the Infinite Staircase also leads to Minauros, the third layer, somewhere in the hanging city of Jangling Hiter.

The way between two layers is always at the lowest point of the upper layer and the highest point of the one below. Where a mountain, a fortress spire or some other tall structure reaches up from the ledge below, a traveler can climb down. Otherwise, stepping off the lowest edge means a long fall. The distance seems to change, but it is at least half a mile, and anyone without a way to slow down pays dearly when they land.



## Inhabitants
Devils are the biggest population: barbazu, cornugons, erinyes, gelugons, hamatula, narzugons, osyluths, pit fiends, spinagons and countless others at every rank. Devils of every kind love making complicated deals with travelers and with mortals on material worlds, and anyone who makes one usually lives to regret it, even if they don't live long. Every devil is looking for ways to gain power and be promoted to a higher form.

Besides devils, the plane is home to hellcats, hell hounds, imps, kytons, nightmares and even rakshasas. A few tough mortals have made permanent homes here, living in strong fortresses guarded by lesser devils bound by short-term contracts.

## Petitioners
The Nine Hells have several kinds of petitioners. Evil, proud and ambitious souls who cared nothing for others and had no empathy end up here. Most become ghost-white shades, hollow copies of their mortal bodies, which devils cruelly bend and twist into painful shapes. Only when a soul has been worked over so thoroughly that it is finally and truly killed does its essence merge with the plane. Devils or gods who hold a realm in Hell often shape its petitioners to fit some particular horrible style.



The most vile petitioners become lemures. Only the most evil mortals end up as lemures, and they usually land here no matter which god they worshipped in life. Every other devil despises them, and they do the lowest jobs in whatever devil group they belong to. At the start of any battle in the Blood War, lemures are the shock troops sent forward to soak up the enemy's attacks. They look like disgusting blobs of melted flesh with roughly human chests and heads, and where the agony hasn't twisted them away, hints of the petitioner's old face still show. Lemures have no minds, but they respond to telepathic commands from other devils and obey the strongest devil nearby.



## Travel and Conflict
Getting around the Nine Hells is a lot like getting around Materia, and even moving between layers is fairly easy for anyone who knows where the crossing points are. The dangers are particular to each layer: the fireballs of Avernus, the red-hot iron of Dis, the killing cold of Cania and the downdraft over the Pit each threaten travelers in their own way, and no account written anywhere else really does them justice.

## Layers

### Avernus
The first layer is a wasteland of burned, rubble-covered plains, broken up by mountains and stony foothills. Armies of armored devils stand guard day and night, always gathering for some new attack in the Blood War. The air glows blood red, and balls of fire drift randomly across the sky, sometimes exploding with terrible force. Any traveler not sheltered in a building or cave has a real chance, on any given day, of being right in the middle of one of those explosions.

Streams of blood run across Avernus and eventually join the River Styx. Nobody knows where the blood comes from, though the devils say it is the blood of every victim Avernus has ever claimed.



**Bronze Citadel:** It may once have been a citadel of bronze. Now it is an ugly, plain city covering dozens of square miles, surrounded by twelve rings of walls bristling with war machines. It is packed with petitioners, lemures and soul shells together, along with hundreds of thousands of lesser devils headed for the Blood War. Avernus is the most likely place for a big demon attack to land, so new fortifications are always being added, and work gangs of soul shells, lemures and imps keep expanding the city without rest. There is so much building going on that any given wall is about as likely as not to be held up by scaffolding made of bone.

**Pillar of Skulls:** A huge landmark built of real skulls, trophies taken by devils in the Blood War. Most are twisted demon skulls, ranging from tiny to the size of a house, and the pillar is more than a mile tall. It stands near the part of the ledge where getting to the next layer is an easy walk down a very tall metal spire of the city of Dis, which reaches up through the haze between the layers. Lots of devils and petitioners use the spire's winding staircase, and plenty fall off it, some by accident and some not. In a huge cave near the foot of the pillar lives an especially terrible guard of the ledge: Vexathrys.

**Vexathrys:** The Chromatic Wyrm, honored by evil dragons, and by some of them as the first child of [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Riavexect, God of Want\|Riavexect]]. Each of her five heads is the color of one kind of evil dragon. She enjoys awful pastimes like torture, quarreling and destruction. Vexathrys guards the way between Avernus and Dis near the Pillar of Skulls, though she rarely comes out except to fight off demon attacks. Five consorts attend her at all times, powerful male great wyrms colored red, black, green, blue and white. Her hoard, hidden deep in her cave, is said to be worth as much as the treasuries of a hundred worlds.



**Lord of the First: Bel.** In the middle of the Bronze Citadel is the fortress of Bel, a very powerful pit fiend who rules Avernus. Besides being lord of the layer, he is a general in the Blood War, though not one of the Dark Eight. Bel betrayed the old Lady of the First, Zariel, to get the job. His underlings whisper that he still keeps her locked up somewhere deep in the Bronze Citadel, draining her hellish power into himself to make himself stronger while slowly turning her into just another soul shell. Bel has succeeded where many before him failed, but his rise seems to have stalled, because the other Lords of the Nine don't back him, except maybe Noir.

### Dis
The second layer is a burning city of iron. Red heat glows inside scorched iron walls, and smoke rises in a dark haze over the whole layer, because here the city is the layer and the layer is the city. Some say that a traveler who walks far enough can leave Dis behind and find that it sits inside a great ring of mountains.

Rows of red, glowing buildings stretch to every horizon, rising and falling with the hidden ground beneath. Here and there the mansions of important devils and Blood War officers break up the skyline. Every iron wall glows with heat, and even brushing against one burns.

The iron cobblestones glow too. Without iron-soled boots, a visitor is soon screaming and burning in the street. Under the streets are prisons full of screaming petitioners, Blood War prisoners and mortals kidnapped from material worlds, and their wailing can usually be heard through small vents in the city walls.

Some districts have markets and bazaars where creatures from many planes meet, mostly to buy or sell shady goods or to hire a bloodthirsty band of mercenaries for work only a devil could love. The streets are crowded. Parades of devil nobles, trailed by lemures and horribly sculpted soul shells, fight for room with the endless work gangs heading out to repair, expand or rebuild the city on the orders of Dispater, Lord of the Second.



**Iron Tower:** It can be seen from anywhere in Dis, rising high over the city and through the haze. Its shape shimmers constantly, shifting between textures and building styles. The Iron Tower is Dispater's private stronghold, and inside its walls he is very nearly unbeatable. That's why he rarely leaves it and prefers to rule through erinyes messengers.



**Lord of the Second: Dispater the Archduke.** Unlike Bel, Dispater doesn't fit neatly into any one type of devil, and nobody remembers where he came from, only that he is an archdevil. Like the other Lords of the Nine, he is an elite devil whose power is still growing, and pit fiends fall before him. He usually appears as an unnaturally tall humanoid with dark hair, small horns and splendid clothes, always holding his badge of office, a rod of unique power. Dispater never takes chances and rarely leaves the Iron Tower. Only a summons from Noir brings him out. In Hell's politics, he and Mephistopheles, Lord of the Eighth, are allies, and they usually scheme together against Baalzebul of the Seventh.

### Minauros
The third layer is a stinking, polluted swamp. Acid rain, bitter winds and hail sharp enough to slice skin rule the bog. In some places the marsh water is cold enough to freeze over, and in others it boils and steams with hellish heat.

Terrible nameless things swim in the murky water, and even devils are afraid to go far from their cities. At the layer's lowest point, a steady trickle of slimy water pours off into a long fall to Phlegethos, and many lost travelers and petitioners have been swept over into that deadly drop.

**The City of Minauros:** The great city the layer is named for, also called The Sinking, because its stone weight is always slipping deeper into the filthy water. Only the endless work of petitioners and slaves keeps it from going under completely, and it still sinks a little further into the mud every year. The ruins underneath are said to hold huge treasures, and they may be what's left of a town dragged in from the Outlands thousands of years ago.

**The City of Jangling Hiter:** The City of Chains hangs over the bog on thick metal chains, and kytons rule it. Its lowest parts just brush the churning swamp, and the strength of the chains keeps the mud from pulling it under the way it has pulled under so many other cities. Nobody knows what the chains are attached to, but a good guess is that they pass through the boundary between layers and fasten to the underside of Dis above. The hail clouds hide the answer. Kytons usually treat each other as equals, but they often defer to an especially clever kyton named Quimath, who lives in Panos Qytel, a three-towered building like a cathedral in the middle of the city.

**Lord of the Third: Mammon the Viscount.** An archdevil with power close to a god's. He looks like a thirty-foot snake with a human chest, head and arms, and he carries a spear like a harpoon whose magic is the least of what he can do. He rules from the center of Minauros, in a huge building like a mausoleum. Mammon is a traitor even by traitors' standards. He once joined Dispater and Mephistopheles against Noir, then was the first to throw himself at Noir's feet when Noir won at the end of the failed rebellion called the Reckoning. Few of the other Lords would ever trust him again, so he is the least likely to join the next revolt.

### Phlegethos
The fourth layer is a place of fire and pain, much like the Elemental Plane of Fire. Rivers of liquid fire cover the land, and flames dance in the air itself. Anything that can't stand fire burns up before long.



**Abriymoch:** A city of hardened lava, obsidian and crystal, crouched in the crater of a volcano that has nearly died out. It is said to be built on the grave of a god Noir killed. The city gives some shelter from the fire of the layer, though some districts are still open to the flames. A legion of more than five thousand hamatula devils is stationed here, held back in case a demon attack ever gets this deep into Hell. A pit fiend named Gazra commands them and answers directly to the Lords of the Fourth, Fierna and Belial, who live in a palace of jagged obsidian on one edge of the crater.

**Lords of the Fourth: Lady Fierna and Archduke Belial.** Both are archdevils who look like humans with a slightly devilish edge. Fierna fights with a blade of fire she can call up whenever she wants, and Belial with a powerful polearm. Fierna is Belial's daughter. He lets her wear the crown in public, while in truth the two of them rule Phlegethos together.

### Stygia
The fifth layer is a land of cold and ice, most of it buried under a sea of grinding ice floes and icebergs. The only open water is the rushing River Styx, and icebergs and fiendish sharks make sailing it dangerous. Lightning tears across the sky constantly, so there are few flying creatures apart from those that can't be hurt by it. Many devil cities and castles are built on ice floes.

**Tantlin:** Called the City of Ice, the biggest city on the layer. Like the smaller ones, it is built on an ice floe, but it also has a harbor on the nearby Styx. A pit fiend rules it but rarely leaves her citadel, which leaves the streets to gangs that make their own laws at each other's expense. The gangs are a mixed lot of devils, with a few evil mortals from the planes running among them. Even with all the chaos, Tantlin works as a trading stop between planes thanks to its place on the Styx. Its people can never forget the real ruler of the layer for long, because Levistus is frozen deep inside an iceberg, called Levistus's Tomb, floating in the middle of the harbor. He can't move, but he knows everything that happens on his layer and can speak telepathically with any devil of Stygia within ten miles.

**Lord of the Fifth: Prince Levistus.** Nobody knows what Levistus looks like, frozen as he is deep inside the iceberg. From outside he is just a big dark blot seen dimly through the ice. Noir froze him there for an old betrayal. Noir lets him rule Stygia by speaking through other devils' minds, but will not set him free. Levistus is scheming all the same. If he ever breaks loose, his revenge on the other lords, and on Noir most of all, is sure to follow.

### Malebolge
Ash falls on Malebolge forever, one flake for every violent death in the mortal world. Visitors to the sixth layer are often struck by its grim beauty, the gray snow of ash settling silently over thick forests. Then they notice the walls and understand. Malebolge is a prison bigger than anyone can measure. Thick forests, harsh wastelands and jagged mountains cover the layer in a checkerboard, and each square, as big as a country, is edged by a giant spiked wall. Together those walls form huge curtains that spread out from a gigantic structure in the middle: the wings of the archdevil Moloch's horned citadel, which covers the whole layer. Malebolge holds two realms: Moloch's endless fortress, Citadel Ba'al, and the layer's huge bolgias, the courtyards, pits and prison lands between the citadel's endless walls.

**Melqart Keep:** The heart of Citadel Ba'al, and so the heart of Malebolge. The walls of Moloch's iron fortress rise up and up with no end in sight, vanishing into the haze of falling ash. Inside, countless legions of cornugons guard huge parade grounds, military libraries and endless planning halls, one for every world a devil has ever set foot on, each holding Moloch's detailed plans to conquer it. Giant siege weapons and traps that can destroy souls defend the fortress, and new ones are always being added as Moloch chases his impossible goal of a fortress no one could ever break into.

At the center of the keep, maybe the best-guarded spot on any plane, is a huge magical model of Hell's nine layers: the Eye of Ba'al. Through it, Moloch watches every one of Hell's armies, both in the Pit and on countless other worlds, and can command any force anywhere, from the biggest devil army to the lowest lemure. If the Eye ever fell, Hell's wars, and countless others across the multiverse, would stop dead. Few prizes matter more to Heaven's armies, and few things matter more to Hell's legions to protect.

**Lord of the Sixth: General Moloch.** Everyone who burns joins Moloch's armies. A being of boiling rage, the Lord of the Sixth is perfect discipline and focused destruction rolled into one. As general of Hell's armies, he trains his legions without rest to be the greatest fighting force in the multiverse, and few could honestly argue that they aren't. He is known as the Ashen Bull and the God of Fires. He demands total obedience, rewards the skilled and ruins the weak, though in the end nobody survives serving under the General of Hell, not even the greatest warriors.

### Maladomini
The seventh layer is dotted with ruined, empty cities. Under a sky black with blood, petitioners quarry stone, carve it and build new cities for the Lord of the Seventh. Mine pits, slag heaps and brackish canals cover the land like sores, and new cities rise on top of the ruins of old ones. Each time one is finished, the Lord is unhappy with it, and his subjects have to start over.

The abandoned cities aren't empty. They shelter petitioners who have escaped their devil tormentors, beasts from other planes who got especially lost, and planar travelers who would rather not be found.

**Malagard:** The city currently being built, and home of Baalzebul, Lord of the Seventh. It is beautiful, with perfectly straight avenues, fountains both delicate and terrible, and towers rising arrow-straight into the black-red sky. It is the greatest city Maladomini has ever seen, and nobody doubts it will be abandoned in turn as Baalzebul chases hellish perfection in everything.

**Grenpoli:** The City of Diplomacy, under a dome, with only four gates in its outer wall. Visitors are searched thoroughly on the way in and all weapons are taken away for the length of their stay. Fighting and open magical aggression are against the law, and lawbreakers are killed on the spot. The Political School of the Nine Hells is here, where ambitious devil nobles come to study treachery and deceit. An erinyes named Mysdemn Wordtwister rules Grenpoli.

**Lord of the Seventh: Baalzebul.** He was once an archon of Celestia named Triel, cast down into the Nine Hells and quickly made a devil by Noir in a dreadful ritual. His relentless drive for perfection served him well as he climbed the ranks, and he finally became lord of the Seventh. He took the position. He didn't inherit it. He overthrew the old lord and erased every mention of them. Having left his old name behind with his old loyalty, he is also called the Lord of Flies, because not even a fly escapes his web of plots. Like nearly every other lord, he plotted against Noir during the Reckoning, and as punishment Noir turned his once-angelic body into a melted, sluglike shape. These days he only openly plots against Mephistopheles, but his endless anger at Noir still burns, and so does his hunger for the throne.

### Cania
The eighth layer is another land of cold, and Cania's cold bites so deep it seems alive. Glaciers moving as fast as a running man grind and crash into each other, sending avalanches down on anything caught between the fighting walls of ice. The cold cuts through the warmest ordinary clothing, and anyone without shelter dies quickly. Moving glaciers often uncover thousand-year-old bodies, earlier victims of the same merciless cold.



**Mephistar:** A blue-white jewel of ice, a mighty citadel sitting on an equally impressive glacier called Nargus. Clouds of vapor roll constantly off the sides of the icy spire. Mephistopheles, Lord of the Eighth, controls where the glacier moves, and Nargus has rolled over and crushed many smaller glaciers, along with its share of rival fiendish armies that didn't see it coming. Inside, Mephistar is warm, full of lavish heated baths, scented fires and hell-bright tapestries. Gelugon nobles live in that warm haven, waiting on their lord's whims.

**Shapes in the Ice:** Some of Cania's glaciers have strange shapes frozen deep inside them. The frosted ice blurs everything, so it's hard to tell what is trapped in these prisons. Now and then a bold mortal melts a shaft down toward an especially interesting blot. Some turn out to be devas and archons frozen in the middle of a fight with spiny creatures nobody can identify. Others are abandoned cities, some ordinary-looking and some utterly alien.

**The Pit:** Huge glaciers form a ledge around a dark pit several hundred feet across. The Pit is completely dark and is the main way down to the lowest layer. One devil-guarded staircase zigzags down the face of a glacier, with a guard tower full of gelugons on every landing. A traveler can skip the stairs and dive straight into the Pit, but a supernaturally strong downdraft makes flying there risky. Anyone who goes in has to find a steady channel of air in the swirling wind or be smashed against the icy walls. Those who find one float down to Nessus without any more trouble.



**Lord of the Eighth: Mephistopheles.** Mephistopheles once staged his own overthrow, when he was replaced by Baron Molikroth. Molikroth was secretly Mephistopheles himself. That trick is over now, and so are the lives of Molikroth's supposed fellow plotters. The Lord of the Eighth looks like a nine-foot humanoid with hell-red skin, horns and wings, gripping a magic polearm that burns forever, and he likes to wrap himself in dramatic capes as black as empty space. Like the other archdukes, he failed to overthrow Noir, and like many of them, he kept his position after the Reckoning. His greatest rival is Baalzebul, and his court is thick with plots against the Lord of Flies.

### Nessus
The ninth layer is the deepest part of the Nine Hells, a plain shattered by rifts deeper than the deepest ocean trench. Many of its canyons run thousands of miles down into plain, dead stone. Most look natural, but some look as if they were cut or blasted into the land. Rumor says a branch of the Styx flows here and there, dropping into trenches and trickling across the layer. Few know how to find it, if it exists at all.

**Malsheem:** Right below the boundary between Cania and Nessus lies a rift of incredible depth and width, and the Pit above leads down into it. Malsheem, the Citadel of Hell, rises out of that trench in a dark, elegant, fiendish beauty. It is monstrously big, climbing miles above the plain, and like an iceberg, its visible towers and walls are small next to the vast halls hidden in the trench. Malsheem is the biggest known fortress on the outer planes, big enough to house millions of devils, an army larger than any ever sent into the Blood War. Noir, master of Malsheem and undisputed King of the Nine Hells, keeps those hordes in reserve for a terrible battle he foresees, one that will make the Blood War look small. The citadel is so huge that it can hardly be mapped, and only Noir knows its secrets.

**Serpent's Coil:** Brutally silenced rumors say there is more to Noir than he lets on. The story goes that his true body lies in the deepest rift of Nessus, called the Serpent's Coil, and that the figure the other devils see in Malsheem is a magical projection or some kind of stand-in. The rift, carved by Noir's falling body when he first came to the Nine Hells, spirals inward for hundreds of miles. His giant body, miles long, is said to lie there still, his wounds still open, his acid-black blood pooling in the hollows of the rift, fouler than anything.

Where did Noir fall from? Was he once a great god thrown down from Elysium or Celestia, or is he even older, as the rumor hints? He may be some fundamental being whose very existence holds the multiverse in its current shape. Nobody who tells the story of Noir's true form lives more than a day after saying it out loud. Even so, dusty scrolls in hard-to-reach libraries, Demogorgon's citadel in the Abyss among them, record it. That is, if it's anything more than a fairy tale.



**Lord of the Ninth: Noir, King of the Nine Hells.** [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Noir, the Black Progenitor Demon\|Noir]], Leader of the Strong, is a Progenitor and far greater than any archdevil. He rules from the Darkened Badlands and from Malsheem, and he is rarely seen. He only shows himself when the elite of the Nine Hells gather in one of his hundred audience halls. He has been challenged many times, most recently in the Hell-wide rebellion called the Reckoning, and he has never lost.
