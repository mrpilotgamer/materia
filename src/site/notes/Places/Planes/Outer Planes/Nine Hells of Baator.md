*"Home to devils"*
*"The plane where the rule of law reveals evil's heart"*

## Overview
The Nine Hells of Baator, called Baator or simply Hell, satisfy the imagination of travelers, the greed of treasure-seekers, and the battle-fury of paladins better than any other plane. It is the ultimate plane of law and evil, the epitome of premeditated and crafted cruelty. Every devil of the Nine Hells obeys a higher law than itself, which in practice means each one chafes and rebels within its caste. Most will undertake any plot or action, however foul, that advances them. At the top of the hierarchy sits Asmodeus, who has yet to be bested. That is the law of the Nine Hells.

Baator competes with any other lower plane for sheer diversity of vileness. The devils are more cunning, more subtle, and more dangerous than other fiends, according to the devils. A demon revels in slavering, insane power. A devil always has an agenda, a plan of attack, and a carefully conceived plot for retribution should one become necessary.

Baator consists of nine layers, each lower than the last, like ledges stepping down into an ever-deeper pit. Each descent grants a traveler a better view of the whole. The layers fit together like puzzle pieces, and every new descent reveals more of how the puzzle assembles. The enticement is itself an evil one.

The Nine Hells house minor, major, and noble devils. The Dark Eight are eight powerful pit fiends commanding the primary devil armies in the Blood War. Above them stand the Lords of the Nine, each controlling a single layer, ranking somewhere between scion pit fiend and demideity. All of them answer to Asmodeus, Lord of the Nine, who rules from Nessus at the bottom.

Baatorian politics have rocked back and forth across the millennia, advanced sometimes by artful rhetoric or subtle poison and sometimes by open warfare between devil armies. The city of Dis on the second layer has withstood siege more than once. In all the time Asmodeus has ruled from below, no revolt has succeeded in replacing the lord of the ninth layer, despite a ploy of Asmodeus's own design that once allowed his rivals to believe he had been dethroned.

Though devils govern the Nine Hells, some sages hold that they commandeered the plane from a far older and stranger race now called simply the ancient baatorians. Remnants of that race may still inhabit isolated portions of the Hells.

## The Dark Eight
The Dark Eight are the generals of the Blood War, eight pit fiends who hold council every sixty-six days. Their power is immense, and they answer nonetheless to the Lords of the Nine and ultimately to Asmodeus.

The eight are Baalzephon, Corin, Dagos, Furcas, Pearza, Zapan, Zaebos, and Zimmimar. They rule the devils beneath them and meet in the fortress of Malsheem in Nessus. Alongside planning the next Blood War offensive, they hold a hand in deciding which devils are promoted.

On the rare occasions when they are not leading their armies they reside in Nessus. Away on hellish errands, they frequently appear in malefic human guise.

## Planar Links
The River Styx flows through Avernus, the uppermost layer, as it does through all the lower planes. Offshoots and waterfalls of it are found on every layer.

Portals to other planes are reasonably common and usually appear as free-standing hoops of reddish light. A well-known gate stands in the town of Ribcage in the Outlands. Travelers generally require an invitation from a Lord of the Nine to pass the Cursed Gate, though Paracs the gatekeeper, a devil, can be bribed. A landing of the Infinite Staircase also provides access to Minauros, the third layer, somewhere within the suspended city of Jangling Hiter.

The connecting points between two layers always lie at the lowest point of the higher layer and the highest point of the next one down. Where a mountain, hellish fortress spire, or other tall structure rises along the ledge below, a traveler can climb down. Otherwise, stepping off the lowest ledge-like projection sends them plummeting. The distance is subjective and appears to be at least half a mile, and anyone without a means of slowing the fall arrives at considerable cost.

## Inhabitants
Devils make up the largest population: barbazu, cornugons, erinyes, gelugons, hamatula, narzugons, osyluths, pit fiends, spinagons, and countless others throughout the hierarchy. Devils of every sort delight in making complex bargains with travelers and with mortals in the material world, and those who deal with one usually live to regret it, even when they do not live long. Every devil seeks ways to increase its own power and win promotion into a higher form of devilhood.

Beyond devils, the plane is home to hellcats, hell hounds, imps, kytons, nightmares, and even rakshasas. A few hardy mortals have established permanent homes here, living in mighty fortresses defended by lesser devils lawfully bound to short-term contracts.

## Petitioners
Several kinds of petitioner are found in the Nine Hells. Evil, proud, ambitious souls unconcerned with others and empty of empathy find their way here. Most take the form of ghost-white shades, shells of their mortal bodies, which devils cruelly mold and shape into twisted and agonized forms. Only when a soul has been so thoroughly worked that it is truly and finally slain does its essence merge with the plane. Devils or deities holding a particular hellish realm often shape the petitioners there to conform to some specific macabre aesthetic.

## Travel and Conflict
Moving about the Nine Hells is much like moving about the material world, and even passing between layers is reasonably straightforward for anyone who knows where the connecting points lie. The hazards here are local, never general: the fireballs of Avernus, the heated iron of Dis, the killing cold of Cania, and the downdraft over the Pit each threaten travelers in their own way, and none of them are described adequately by any account written elsewhere.

## Layers

### Avernus
The first layer is a wasteland of charred, rubble-strewn plains, its monotony broken by mountains and talus-sided foothills. Legions of mail-clad devils keep perpetual watch, always assembling for some new cross-planar Blood War sortie. Blood-red light suffuses the air, and fiery balls flit randomly across the sky, sometimes detonating to terrible effect. Any traveler not sheltered by an artificial structure or a cave stands a real chance, on any given day, of finding themselves at the center of such a detonation.

Runnels of blood flow in streams across Avernus and eventually join the River Styx. The origin of that blood is unknown, though the devils hold it to be the blood of every victim Avernus has claimed.

**Bronze Citadel:** What may once have been a citadel of bronze is now a brutish, unimaginative city covering dozens of square miles, ringed by twelve concentric walls each bristling with war machines. Petitioners fill it, lemures and soul shells together, alongside hundreds of thousands of lesser devils bound for the Blood War. Because Avernus is the likeliest beachhead for any massed demonic attack, fortifications are added continually, and work gangs of soul shells, lemures, and imps expand the city without pause. The construction is pervasive enough that any given wall is as likely to be held up by bone scaffolding as not.

**Pillar of Skulls:** A massive landmark built of actual skulls, devilish trophies taken in the Blood War. Most are twisted and demonic, ranging in size from minuscule to the size of a house, and the pillar rises more than a mile. It stands near the part of the ledge where access to the next layer is an easy walk down a particularly tall metal spire of the city of Dis, which reaches up through the haze between layers. That spire sees considerable devil and petitioner traffic on its spiral stairwell, along with a good number of falls both accidental and otherwise. A massive cave mouth near the pillar's base holds a particularly terrible sentinel of the ledge: Tiamat.

**Tiamat:** The Chromatic Dragon, revered by evil dragons, her five heads each bearing the color of one kind of evil dragon. She enjoys such wretched pastimes as torture, bickering, and destruction. Tiamat guards the access between Avernus and Dis near the Pillar of Skulls, though she rarely stirs forth except to counter demonic incursions. Five consorts attend her at all times, powerful male great wyrms of red, black, green, blue, and white. Her hoard, hidden deep in her cavern, is said to equal the treasuries of a hundred worlds.

**Lord of the First: Bel.** At the center of the Bronze Citadel stands the personal fortress of Bel, a pit fiend of great power who controls Avernus. Beyond his lordship he is a general in the Blood War, though not one of the Dark Eight. Bel betrayed the former Lord of the First, Zariel, to take the position. His subordinates whisper that he still keeps her prisoner somewhere deep in the Bronze Citadel so that he may siphon her hellish power into himself, increasing his own abilities while slowly reducing her to another soul shell. Bel has succeeded where many before him failed, and his climb now appears stalled, since he lacks the support of the other Lords of the Nine, with the possible exception of Asmodeus.

### Dis
The second layer is a burning city of iron. The red glow of infernal heat burns within scorched iron walls, and a pall of smoke rises to form a dark haze across the whole layer, because here the city is the layer and the layer is the city. Some claim that a traveler who walks far enough can leave Dis behind, finding it concealed within a great ring of mountains.

Ranks of red, glowing buildings extend to every horizon, rising and falling with the hidden terrain beneath. Palatial mansions of particularly important devils and Blood War officers break the cityscape here and there. Every iron wall glows with heat, and even casual contact burns.

The iron cobblestones glow as well. Without iron-shod boots a visitor is soon writhing and burning in the street. Screaming petitioners, Blood War captives, and mortals kidnapped from the material world fill subterranean prisons beneath those streets, and the sound of their lament is usually audible through small vents in the city walls.

Some sections host markets and bazaars where creatures from many planes meet, most seeking to buy or sell questionable wares or to hire a bloodthirsty band of mercenaries for work only a devil could love. Crowds fill the streets. Parades of devilish nobility attended by lemures and horribly sculpted soul shells contend for room with the countless work gangs sallying forth to repair, extend, or remodel the city at the order of Dispater, Lord of the Second.

**Iron Tower:** Visible from every part of Dis, reaching high above the city and piercing the layer's haze. Its form shimmers constantly, shifting between textures and architectural styles. The Iron Tower is Dispater's personal redoubt, and within its walls he is very nearly invulnerable, which is why he rarely leaves it and prefers to rule through erinyes messengers.

**Lord of the Second: Dispater the Archduke.** Unlike Bel, Dispater resists classification as any particular type of devil, and his origin is not recalled beyond the fact that he is an archdevil. Like the other Lords of the Nine he is an elite devil whose powers are waxing, and pit fiends fall before his onslaught. He usually appears as a humanoid of supernatural height with dark hair, tiny horns, and resplendent garb, always holding his badge of office, a rod of singular power. Dispater never takes risks and rarely leaves the Iron Tower. Only Asmodeus's call brings him out. In Baatorian politics he and Mephistopheles, Lord of the Eighth, are allies, generally scheming against Baalzebul of the Seventh.

### Minauros
The third layer is a fetid swamp of mire and pollution. Acidic rain, bitter winds, and flesh-slicing hail rule the bog. In places the marsh water is cold enough to have iced over, and in others the dank water boils and steams with infernal heat.

Terrible creatures with no names swim the murky waters, and even devils fear to stray far from their cities. Where the layer runs lowest, a constant dribble of slimy water sluices into a long fall down to Phlegethos, and many a lost traveler and petitioner has been caught up in that lethal descent.

**The City of Minauros:** The metropolis that gives the layer its name, called also The Sinking, because the stony weight of it slips forever beneath the cesspool waters. Only the eternal labor of petitioners and slaves keeps it from succumbing entirely, and it still sinks a little farther into the mud each year. The ruins below are said to hold vast riches, and may represent a town pulled out of the Outlands millennia ago.

**The City of Jangling Hiter:** The City of Chains hangs suspended above the bog on thick metal links, ruled by kytons. Its lowest portions just graze the roiling swamp, and the strength of the chains keeps the mire from dragging it down as so many other cities have been dragged. No one knows what structure the chains finally connect to, though a reasonable guess is that they pierce the layer boundary and fasten to the underside of Dis above. The hail-laden clouds conceal the matter. Kytons generally consider one another equals, yet they often defer to a particularly clever kyton named Quimath, who resides in Panos Qytel, a cathedral-like triple-towered structure at the city's heart.

**Lord of the Third: Mammon the Viscount.** An archdevil possessing almost deity-level power. He resembles a thirty-foot serpent with a human torso, head, and arms, and wields a harpoon-like spear whose magic is the least of his abilities. He rules from the center of Minauros, inhabiting a mausoleum-like structure of similarly massive proportions. Mammon is a turncoat's turncoat. Having once allied with Dispater and Mephistopheles against Asmodeus, he was the first to abase himself before the Lord of the Ninth when Asmodeus proved victorious at the close of the failed rebellion known as the Reckoning. Few of the other Lords would trust him again, which makes him the least likely to take part in the next internal revolt.

### Phlegethos
The fourth layer is a place of fire and pain much like the Elemental Plane of Fire. Rivers of liquid fire rule the land, and dancing flames consume the air itself. Anything that cannot withstand fire is immolated before long.

**Abriymoch:** A city of hardened magma, obsidian, and crystal squatting in the caldera of an almost extinct volcano. Its foundation is said to be the grave of a deity slain by Asmodeus. The city offers some protection from the layer's fiery environment, though certain quarters remain open to the raging flame. A legion of more than five thousand hamatula devils is stationed here, held in reserve should a demonic attack pierce this far into the Hells. A pit fiend named Gazra commands them and answers directly to the Lords of the Fourth, Fierna and Belial, who reside in a palace of jagged obsidian built on one lip of the caldera.

**Lords of the Fourth: Lady Fierna and Archduke Belial.** Both are archdevils taking the form of slightly devilish-looking humanoids. Fierna fights with a blade of fire she summons at will, and Belial with a powerful ranseur. Fierna is Belial's daughter, and he lets her wear the mantle of leadership in public while the two rule Phlegethos together in fact.

### Stygia
The fifth layer is a realm of cold and ice, most of it drowned beneath a sea of crushing ice floes and icebergs. The only open water is the rushing River Styx, and icebergs and fiendish sharks make navigating it dangerous. Lightning rips constantly through the sky, so flying creatures are rare except those proof against it. Ice floes serve as foundations for many devilish cities and castles.

**Tantlin:** Called the City of Ice, the largest city of the layer. Like the smaller ones it is built on an ice floe, though it has a harbor bordering the nearby Styx. A pit fiend rules it and rarely stirs from her citadel, and that hands-off approach leaves the streets to various gangs dispensing their own law at one another's expense. The gangs are a motley assortment of devils, with a few planar mortals of evil disposition running among them. Despite the disarray, Tantlin works as a cross-planar trading stop thanks to its position on the Styx. Its residents cannot forget the ruler of the layer for long, because Levistus is frozen deep within an iceberg, Levistus's Tomb, floating in the middle of the harbor. Immobile though he is, Levistus remains fully aware of events on his layer and can communicate telepathically with the other devils of Stygia within a ten-mile radius.

**Lord of the Fifth: Prince Levistus.** No one knows what Levistus looks like, frozen as he is deep within an iceberg. From outside, the prince appears as a great dark clot blearily made out through the ice. Asmodeus imprisoned him there for a past betrayal, and though he permits the prince control over Stygia through mental contact with other devils, he will not free him. The plots of Levistus run deep regardless. Should he ever break loose, his vengeance on the other lords, and on Asmodeus above all, would surely follow.

### Malebolge
Ash falls endlessly upon Malebolge, one flake for every violent death suffered in the mortal world. Those who come upon the sixth layer are often shocked by its grim beauty, the charred snow coating dense forests in silent gray drifts. Then they notice the walls and understand. Malebolge is a bastille beyond all measuring. Dense forests, harsh wastelands, and craggy mountains cover the layer in a geographical checkerboard, each country-sized cell edged by a gigantic spiked fortification. Together those borders form vast curtains radiating outward from a titanic central structure, the impenetrable wings of the archdevil Moloch's layer-spanning horned citadel. Two realms exist within Malebolge: Moloch's immeasurable fortress, Citadel Ba'al, and the layer's vast bolgias, the courtyards, pits, and prison realms divided by the citadel's endless walls.

**Melqart Keep:** The heart of Citadel Ba'al and so of Malebolge itself. The mounting fortifications of Moloch's iron bastion rise apparently without end, disappearing into the haze of falling ash. Within, untold legions of cornugons guard vast parade grounds, military libraries, and innumerable strategy halls, one for every world a devil has ever set foot upon, each housing Moloch's meticulous plans for its conquest. Titanic siege weapons and soul-annihilating traps defend the fortress, with constant additions striving toward the archdevil's unattainable standard of impregnability.

At the keep's center, perhaps the best-guarded point in all the planes, lies a vast magical representation of Hell's nine layers: the Eye of Ba'al. Through it Moloch oversees every one of Hell's legions within the Pit and on countless other worlds, and can command any force anywhere, from the largest diabolical army to the lowliest lemure. Were the Eye to fall, Hell's wars and countless others throughout the multiverse would be interrupted instantly. Few objectives matter more to Heaven's forces to capture, or to Hell's legions to protect.

**Lord of the Sixth: General Moloch.** All who burn join the armies of Moloch. A being of seething wrath, the Lord of the Sixth embodies absolute discipline and directed destructive force together. As general of Hell's armies he trains his infernal legions endlessly toward being the greatest martial force in the multiverse, a claim few can realistically dispute. Known as the Ashen Bull and the God of Fires, he expects absolute obedience from his followers, rewarding the skilled and ruining the weak, though none survive the General of Hell's command in the end, the greatest of warriors included.

### Maladomini
The seventh layer is dotted with ruined, abandoned cities. Beneath a blood-black sky, petitioners quarry, carve, and build new cities for the Lord of the Seventh. Mine pits, slag heaps, and brackish canals cover the land like sores, and newer cities rise on the backs of older ruins. As each is finished, the Lord's dissatisfaction forces his subjects to begin again.

The abandoned cities are not empty. They shelter petitioners who have fled their devilish tormentors, beasts from other planes that became especially lost, and planar travelers who prefer not to be found.

**Malagard:** The city currently under construction, home to Baalzebul, Lord of the Seventh. It is beautiful, with perfectly straight boulevards, fountains of delicate yet terrible aspect, and towers rising arrow-straight into the blood-black sky. It is the greatest city Maladomini has yet seen, and nobody doubts it will be abandoned in its turn as Baalzebul pursues hellish perfection in all things.

**Grenpoli:** The City of Diplomacy, domed, accessible only through one of four gates in its exterior. Entry follows a thorough search during which all weapons are confiscated for the duration of a visit. Strife and open displays of magical aggression are against the law, and lawbreakers are killed immediately. The Political School of the Nine Hells stands here, where enterprising devilish nobility come to study treachery and deception. An erinyes named Mysdemn Wordtwister rules Grenpoli.

**Lord of the Seventh: Baalzebul.** Once an archon of Celestia called Triel, cast down into the Nine Hells and promoted swiftly to devilhood by Asmodeus in a dread ritual. His relentless drive for perfection served him well as he climbed the ranks, culminating in his ascension to lord of the Seventh. The position was taken, never inherited. He deposed the earlier lord and expunged all mention of that entity. Having left his former name behind with his former allegiance, he is also called the Lord of Flies, because not even a fly escapes his net of intrigues. Like virtually every other lord he plotted against Asmodeus during the Reckoning, and in punishment Asmodeus transformed his formerly angelic body into a melted, sluglike form. He now openly plots against Mephistopheles alone, though his undying anger for Asmodeus burns yet, as does his ambition for the throne.

### Cania
The eighth layer is another realm of cold, and Cania's cold penetrates so bitterly that it seems a living thing. Glaciers moving as fast as a running man grind and crash against one another, sending avalanches down on any creature caught between the battling ice titans. The cold cuts through the warmest natural clothing, and unsheltered areas kill quickly. Moving glaciers often reveal thousand-year-old corpses, past victims of the same merciless chill.

**Mephistar:** A blue-white jewel fashioned of ice, a mighty citadel perched on an equally impressive glacier named Nargus. Clouds of vapor roll constantly off the sides of the icy spike. Mephistopheles, Lord of the Eighth, controls the glacier's movement, and Nargus has flowed over and obliterated many lesser glaciers along with its share of rival fiendish armies caught unaware. The interior of Mephistar is heated, and lavish heated baths, scented fires, and hell-bright tapestries fill it. Gelugon nobles abide in that warm sanctuary, waiting on their lord's whims.

**Shapes in the Ice:** Some Canian glaciers entomb alien shapes frozen in the heart of the ice. The frosted glacier distorts vision, so what is buried in such a prison is difficult to make out. Enterprising mortals occasionally melt exploratory shafts toward particularly intriguing blots. Some prove to be devas and archons frozen mid-fight against spined creatures of unknown origin. Others are abandoned cities of prosaic or alien design.

**The Pit:** Mighty glaciers form a ledge surrounding a dark pit several hundred feet across. The utterly lightless Pit is the primary access down to the lowest layer. One devil-guarded stairway cuts back and forth down a glacier's face, with a guard tower bristling with gelugons on each landing. A traveler may forgo the stairs and plunge directly into the Pit, though a supernaturally strong downdraft makes flying there a chancy proposition. Anyone entering it must find a stable tunnel of air within the swirling wind or be dashed against the icy side. Those who find one descend to Nessus without further trouble.

**Lord of the Eighth: Mephistopheles.** Mephistopheles once engineered his own coup, when he was replaced by Baron Molikroth. Molikroth was secretly Mephistopheles himself, and that duplicity has now ended, as have the lives of Molikroth's supposed co-conspirators. The Lord of the Eighth appears as a nine-foot humanoid with hell-red skin, horns, and wings, gripping a magic ranseur that burns eternally, and prefers to drape himself in dramatic capes as black as the deepest void. Like the other archdukes he failed to unseat Asmodeus, and like many of them he retained his position once the Reckoning was over. His greatest rival is Baalzebul, and his court is thick with plots against the Lord of Flies.

### Nessus
The ninth layer is the deepest realm of the Nine Hells, a plain shattered by rifts deeper than the deepest ocean trench. Many of its ravines and canyons reach thousands of miles into undifferentiated, dead stone. Most appear natural, though some look cut or blasted into the land. Rumor holds that an offshoot of the Styx flows here and there, dropping into trenches and trickling across the layer. Few know how to reach that tributary, if it exists at all.

**Malsheem:** A rift of incredible depth and width lies immediately below the boundary between Cania and Nessus, and the Pit above leads down into it. Malsheem, the Citadel of Hell, rises from that trench in dark, elegant, fiendish beauty. The stronghold is monstrously large, climbing miles above the plain, and like an iceberg its visible spires and fortifications are small beside the vast halls contained within the trench itself. Malsheem is the largest known citadel in the Outer Planes, big enough to house millions of devils, an army larger than any fielded in the Blood War. Asmodeus, master of Malsheem and undisputed King of the Nine Hells, holds those hordes in reserve for a cataclysmic battle he foresees, one that will dwarf the petty maneuverings of the Blood War. The citadel is so vast as to be very nearly unmappable, and its secrets are known to Asmodeus alone.

**Serpent's Coil:** Brutally repressed rumors suggest there is more to Asmodeus than he admits. The story runs that his true form resides in the deepest rift of Nessus, called the Serpent's Coil, and that the shape all the other devils see in Malsheem is an image projected by magic or an avatar of some kind. The secret rift, formed by Asmodeus's plummeting body when he first arrived in the Nine Hells, spirals inward across hundreds of miles. His titanic, miles-long form is said to rest there still, his wounds yet unhealed, his acid-black blood pooling in the hollows of the rift, a substance fouler than foul.

From where did Asmodeus fall? Was he once a greater deity cast down from Elysium or Celestia, or is he older still, as the rumor hints? He may represent some fundamental entity whose mere existence pulls the multiverse into its present configuration. Nobody who tells the story of Asmodeus's true form lives more than a day after saying it aloud. Dusty scrolls in hard-to-reach libraries, Demogorgon's citadel in the Abyss among them, record the knowledge regardless. Assuming it is anything but pure fancy.

**Lord of the Ninth: Asmodeus, King of the Nine Hells.** Asmodeus is at the least an archdevil and may possess the power of a true deity. He resides in Malsheem and is rarely seen. Only when the elite of the Nine Hells gather in one of a hundred audience chambers does he make his presence known, generally in the form of an unprepossessing humanoid with black hair, a black goatee, and eyes of flame. He has been challenged many times, most recently during the Hells-wide rebellion called the Reckoning, and has never been defeated.
