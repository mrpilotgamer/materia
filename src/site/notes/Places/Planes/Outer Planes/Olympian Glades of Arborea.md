## Overview
Arborea is wild country that has never been cut, plowed or fenced. Its forests are old maple, birch and oak, so tall and close that little grows beneath them except moss and fern. Where the trees break there are meadows of wildflowers, fields of wheat and barley that sow themselves, and orchards in tidy rows that nobody planted. Snow sits on the high ground under a clear blue sky, and on the same day the trees below are in flower and in fruit.

The weather turns without warning. A storm can come up out of a clear afternoon, flatten the grass and strip leaves for a few minutes, then blow off and leave the woods steaming in the sun. Visitors say the air feels like the hour before a festival. There is nearly always music somewhere off in the trees. Sometimes it is the [[Species and Cultures/Races/Mannee\|Mannee]] or the [[Bestiary/Fey\|Fey]] playing, and sometimes it is only wind in the trunks.

The forest is only the first layer. Below it is an ocean with no shore, and below that a desert of white dust. What the three layers share is the weather and the mood. Arborea's joys run high and so do its griefs, and both pass quickly.

## Planar Links
Arborea touches the Beastlands and Ysgard. Portals between them are common, and the borders drift, so a traveler can walk out of Arborea into a neighboring plane without noticing when it happened.

## Divine Realms
**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Angels/Punane, the Red Progenitor Angel\|Punane]]**, the Eternal Martyr, holds the Fields of Glorious Sacrifice. It is the most powerful domain on Arborea. Punane's followers come here after dying for causes that were never their own, and they are honored here for it.

**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Arlaxena, God of Leisure\|Arlaxena]]**, Master of Well-Being, holds Stillwater Bend in Arvandor. It is a lazy river that loops through the forest without ever reaching the sea. Traps hang along its banks and catch dinner, so nobody there ever has to hunt.

## Inhabitants
The Mannee dead are the plane's best-known guardians. Their souls return as celestials here, and they are far stronger in death than the small, gentle people they were in life.

Azatas are common. They feed on the plane's strong feelings, and they are drawn to wherever those feelings run highest.

Most animals on Arborea are celestial or anarchic versions of material-world beasts. The celestial ones ignore travelers unless someone starts a fight, and then they fight gladly. The anarchic ones behave however they please, and nobody can predict them.

## Petitioners
Two kinds of petitioner are common on Arborea.

The first are the Mannee dead. Some of them dissolve into the plane itself. Others take new shapes as celestial or anarchic creatures and serve the Mannee Court as scouts and wardens, guarding its glades and castles.

The second are the Bacchae. In life they were people who loved eating, drinking and living well, and in death they wander Arborea in drunken crowds, half human and half goat. They are kind-hearted and chaotic in equal parts, and they want every moment to be the best one yet. They are rarely violent. Attack them and they run, or hand you bread and mead.

The danger is the party. A traveler who gets close to a big enough crowd of Bacchae gets pulled in and does not want to leave. The food and drink at the revel look real, but none of it nourishes. Victims dance and feast until they collapse from hunger and thirst, unless a friend drags them far enough away to break the spell.



## Travel and Conflict
Getting around Arborea is no harder than crossing wild country at home. In the forest of Arvandor, trails wind between thickets, and open clearings are about as common as briar. Anything that climbs or swings can travel through the canopy without touching the ground. Aquallor, the second layer, is all water, so crossing it means swimming.

The plane gives no edge in a fight to either side. The forests of Arvandor offer plenty of cover. In Aquallor every fight happens underwater.



## Layers

### Arvandor
Arvandor belongs to the Mannee Court, which travelers' stories call the Seldarine.

Under the trees are wide clearings, and in them live the Chosen of Arvandor. Their days are spent hunting and testing one another, and their evenings around the fire with food and stories.

Day and night keep the same pace as on Materia. A gold sun crosses the treetops by day, and at night a white moon hangs in a thick band of stars. The nights are warm. Huge fireflies drift among the oaks and lindens in shifting patterns.

**The Seldarine:** The lords of Arvandor build with an artist's eye. Some of their halls are grown from living trees into cathedrals. Others are palaces of crystal and white marble with gold towers. The tower of Pluen, the First Feather, is one of these, hung with bright banners and visible for miles. Only the Mannee and Pluen's friends are let inside to join the singing and the feast.

**Pluen, the First Feather:** Pluen was the first of the Mannee dead to rise on Arborea as a celestial, and leads the Mannee Court. Pluen keeps the gift of magic that [[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Primordials/Murasakino, Purple Primordial Spirit\|Murasakino]] gave the Mannee, and honors Murasakino as the source of the magic every Mannee is born with.

### Aquallor
The second layer is an endless sea. Whirlpools connect it to the Elemental Plane of Water, but Aquallor has a surface and a seabed, and its depths are pitch black.

There are no islands. Storms roam the surface and sink any ship that tries to cross. Under the water there is no air at all, so a swimmer needs magic or gear to breathe.

The weather here shows up as currents. A strong one can carry a swimmer miles off course before they know it.

The sea is full of life, mostly celestial or anarchic. The deeper a diver goes, the bigger the animals get, and the more likely they are to meet something stranger.

The River Oceanus, which runs through many of the upper planes, ends in Aquallor. Some say the whirlpools in the deep carry water back to the river's source, so the river never ends. A traveler can leave Aquallor by riding one of those whirlpools, though there is no telling what waits where it comes out.

### Mithardir
The third layer is a desert of fine white chalk that goes on forever. It was a forest once. Campfire stories say giants lived here, or beings that were close to gods. Nobody knows where they went.

Their towers and tombs are still here, the tops of spires poking out of the dust. Treasure hunters come looking for the giants' secrets, and the wind usually grinds them down to dust along with everything else.

Mithardir's weather is as sudden as the rest of Arborea's. Lightning storms roll across the desert pushing dust storms ahead of them, and a single storm can bury a traveler alive.
