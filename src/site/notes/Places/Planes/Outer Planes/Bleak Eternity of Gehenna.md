## Overview
Volcanic mountains float in an impenetrable, infinite void, apparently without base or peak. They are finite only in the strictest sense, measuring hundreds of thousands of miles in every direction. A single such mountain dominates each of Gehenna's four layers, while lesser volcanic earthbergs drift between them and occasionally smash into the greater peaks.

No naturally level ground exists anywhere on the plane. Every slope runs at forty-five degrees or steeper, and many amount to sheer cliffs. The fiendish inhabitants have carved artificial ledges, some broad enough to hold entire cities, and switchback paths connecting them. Anything not carved by the native yugoloths or by deities tends to break apart in time, sending its builders on a long sliding fall down the mountainside.

The topmost layer borders Hades and the Nine Hells. Powerful entities holding realms on Gehenna include many lords of the yugoloths, along with Melif the Lich-Lord.

## Planar Links
Like all the lower planes, Gehenna carries the River Styx through at least its first layer of Khalas, where it is the largest river on the layer. It moves through gorges and canyons at breathtaking speed, and its cataracts are legendary. The occasional ledge produces waterfalls of epic if polluted proportion. Attempting to change planes by way of the Styx is dangerous anywhere and particularly so here.

Portals to other planes are reasonably common, as are portals between Gehenna's own layers. Both generally appear as bottomless black chasms. They are sometimes marked as portals. Yugoloths have also been known to mark genuine bottomless chasms as portals, whether through error or malice.

## Inhabitants
The yugoloths, masters of schemes, are most at home on Gehenna, though sages note that the race actually originated in Hades. They have nonetheless occupied Gehenna longer than most of the deities who now keep realms there.

## Petitioners
The petitioners of Gehenna are the refuse of the planes. Greedy and grasping, they care for nothing beyond themselves, and no traveler should expect a favor from one without proof of immediate recompense in hand. They are more willful than the petitioners of most Outer Planes, moving between layers on personal quests for power. What they seek is the ultimate exercise of free will, and they are fated never to find it.

## Travel and Conflict
Moving about Gehenna resembles movement in the material world in every respect except the one that matters. Because every natural surface slopes at forty-five degrees or steeper, save for the occasional ledge or artificial construction, simply getting from one place to another is a climb and a hazard.

A traveler who loses their footing does not fall so much as roll, bounce, and rebound down an endless steep slope, and a practiced climber can sometimes catch themselves before the descent gathers speed. Those who cannot generally come to rest on some natural ledge a hundred feet or more below, badly battered by the journey. In certain places on Gehenna the fall ends sooner, in a river of lava.

Fighting here is fighting between climbers. Anyone on the open surface of a Gehennan mountain can neither dodge freely nor use a shield, and an attacker holds a clear advantage over a climbing opponent even while climbing themselves. A climber who takes a wound must immediately secure their grip or fall.

## Features
Each layer, called a mount, differs slightly from the others, and all four burn with an evil will. Lava flows appear to seek out the casual traveler, and fissures open beneath a visitor's feet as though the ground itself were hungry. As on Carceri, light seeps upward out of the sloping earth, so that shadows stretch toward the sky.

## Layers

### Khalas
The air of the first and lowest mount carries a crimson tint near the ground, the work of magma flows and pyroclastic ash, though it fades to black within a few dozen feet overhead. The next mount of Chamada is visible in that darkness, far enough away that it burns like a small and bloody moon.

The slopes of Khalas are streaked with waterfalls and cloaked in steam. None of the falls reaches the bottom of the layer, evaporating or vanishing into fissures instead. The mightiest are those the River Styx makes during its tumultuous passage across the layer.

**Teardrop Palace:** A nautilus-shaped pagoda set along the Styx with two smaller shrines beside it, the whole encircled by a wrought-iron fence and occupying a ledge that was plainly deity-carved. The pagoda measures miles on each side, as do the shrines. A crowded bazaar thrives between the two lesser buildings, full of milling yugoloths, petitioners, devils, demons, other outsiders, and the occasional mortal. The bazaar deals in everything, which makes it the ultimate black market, since nearly all of its stock was stolen from somewhere else on the Great Wheel. Prices run high and pickpockets are a constant hazard, and the market keeps a reputation for exotic goods available nowhere else.

### Chamada
The second mount is the most savage. Its slopes burn with magma flowing so thickly that cool solid ground is rare, and so brightly that the glare blots out the sky. Cascading lava rivers harden and briefly dam the flow behind them, then burst forth explosively in new directions. Vents open without warning to spew fresh ejecta, and miniature volcanoes are common. Feather-soft gray ash usually fills the air, falling everywhere like a dread snow and often dropping visibility to nothing.

**Nimicri:** Seen from the slopes of Chamada, Nimicri appears as a small moon some two thousand feet across, floating in splendid isolation above the burning mount. Spires, steeples, and humbler structures cover it, connected by a weblike network of streets. Everything is clean, the buildings are comfortable and well maintained, and every citizen is perfectly polite. Excellent goods of all kinds can be had at the trading post, and Nimicri is an established stop on several routes crossing the Outer Planes.

What visitors almost never discover is that Nimicri, buildings and people together, is a single vast creature imitating a town. It sometimes absorbs a visitor entirely and sometimes lets one leave unharmed. Should even a single drop of a visitor's blood fall on any surface in the city, Nimicri can reproduce that visitor exactly, memories included, up to the moment the blood was spilled. A citizen of Nimicri forcibly removed from the town dies at once, like a limb severed from a body.

**Tower Arcane:** A tower rising high above the lava flows and ash clouds of Chamada, decorated with blades and spikes promising pain and death to unwelcome visitors. Yugoloth wizards control it, and it is the record vault of their race. The history of the yugoloths is kept here, well protected and heavily encrypted behind glyphs of warding, symbols, and other protective magic.

The interior halls hold the bodies of flayed petitioners hanging from chains, whose blood the wizards use to write that history. Torture equipment lines whatever walls are not given to bookshelves and sealed archives. Far below the tower lies a library of particularly ghastly contracts with mortals, running for miles, protected by the most potent wards in the wizards' arsenal against molten rock and extraplanar thieves alike. Each contract is inscribed on the living skin of a petitioner, burned in with magic and branding irons. The petitioners are strung along chains in parallel lines of agony, and each one holds nothing in mind beyond its own contract and its own pain.

### Mungoth
The third mount is far less volcanically active than Chamada or Khalas. It is cold, in fact, and often under snowfall. Scattered volcanic vents cast about as much light as a full moon, which makes navigating Mungoth's icy slopes difficult, and a heavy fall of snow and ash sometimes snuffs even that. The combination of snow and acidic ash endangers any traveler without shelter, and the ice that accumulates on the slopes makes the climbing considerably worse than elsewhere on the plane.

**Valley of the Outcast:** A deep chasm holding a well-hidden realm sheltered from the constant acidic snow. A rough castle stands there, built of basaltic rock and giant bones in equal measure, scaled to the proportions of its master: an outcast fire giant wizard named Tastuo. Her eight siblings, outcasts alongside her, live there too, and all are wizards or sorcerers. The yugoloths of the layer hold several interlocking contracts with Tastuo, which helps secure the valley should her enemies ever locate it. She never names those enemies. Her own predicament leaves her sympathetic to travelers seeking asylum, and so the Valley of the Outcast doubles as a way station for those in need, provided they can find it.

### Krangath
The fourth mount, called the Dead Furnace, has no volcanic activity at all and went inactive ages ago. Its great mountain is a darkened pillar rumbling in the night. Silence shrouds the layer. No wind stirs and no light glimmers. Krangath is dead.

Petitioners are rare here, and those that move about the layer have learned to keep quiet, so that Melif does not hear them.

**Hopelorn:** An artificially maintained ledge holds a precisely fashioned complex of obsidian, dim reddish lights showing at tiny slitlike windows throughout. This is Hopelorn, the stronghold of Melif the Lich-Lord, a mortuary city where sarcophagi glow like streetlights and necromantic energies dance wisplike over every boulevard.

The undead are welcome. The living and the petitioners are not, Melif regarding both as pathetic failures unable to manage the passage of their own mortal lives. Here Melif and a cabal of liches and other powerful undead spellcasters pursue undying research into the nature of life, death, and being. They sometimes capture fiends for outrageous experiments, though never yugoloths, lest the wrath of that race fall on Hopelorn entire. It is rumored in any case that Melif was a yugoloth himself before he steeped himself in the eldritch arts and came at last to lichdom.
