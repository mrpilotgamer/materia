## Overview
Gehenna is volcanic mountains hanging in an endless black void. They seem to have no top and no bottom. Technically they do end, but only after hundreds of thousands of miles in every direction. One great mountain fills each of Gehenna's four layers, and smaller volcanic earthbergs drift between them and sometimes crash into the big peaks.

There is no flat ground anywhere on Gehenna. Every slope is at least forty-five degrees, and many are sheer cliffs. The fiends who live here have carved out ledges, some big enough for whole cities, with switchback paths between them. Anything not carved by the yugoloths or by a god breaks away sooner or later and sends its builders on a long slide down the mountain.

The top layer borders Hades and the Nine Hells. Many yugoloth lords hold realms on Gehenna, and so does Melif the Lich-Lord.

## Planar Links
Like every lower plane, Gehenna has the River Styx running through at least its first layer, Khalas, where it is the biggest river around. It tears through gorges and canyons at incredible speed, and its rapids are famous. Here and there it pours off a ledge in an enormous, filthy waterfall. Trying to travel between planes by the Styx is dangerous anywhere, and here most of all.

Portals to other planes are fairly common, and so are portals between Gehenna's layers. Both usually look like bottomless black chasms. Sometimes they are marked. Yugoloths have also been known to mark real bottomless chasms as portals, by mistake or on purpose.

## Inhabitants
The scheming yugoloths are most at home on Gehenna, though sages say the race actually began in Hades. Even so, they have lived on Gehenna longer than most of the powers who now hold realms here.

## Petitioners
Gehenna's petitioners are the dregs of the planes. They are greedy and selfish and care about nothing but themselves. No traveler should expect a favor from one without payment up front. They are more headstrong than most petitioners, and they travel between layers chasing power for themselves. What they want is total freedom of will, and they will never get it.



## Travel and Conflict
Getting around Gehenna is like getting around Materia in every way but one, and it's the one that matters. Every natural surface slopes at forty-five degrees or more, apart from the odd ledge or built structure, so just getting from one place to another means climbing, and climbing is dangerous.

A traveler who loses their footing doesn't so much fall as roll, bounce and smash their way down an endless steep slope. A skilled climber can sometimes catch themselves before they pick up speed. Those who can't usually end up on a ledge a hundred feet or more below, badly beaten up. In some places on Gehenna the fall ends sooner, in a river of lava.

Every fight on Gehenna is a fight between climbers. On the open face of the mountain, nobody can dodge properly or use a shield, and anyone attacking a climber has the advantage, even if they're climbing too. A climber who gets hurt has to tighten their grip right away or fall.



## Features
Each layer, called a mount, is a little different, and all four burn with a hateful will. Lava seems to go looking for careless travelers, and cracks open under visitors' feet as if the ground were hungry. As on Carceri, light comes up out of the ground, so shadows point at the sky.

## Layers

### Khalas
On the first and lowest mount, the air near the ground is tinged red by lava and ash, fading to black a few dozen feet up. In that blackness hangs Chamada, the next mount, so far away it looks like a small, bloody moon.

Waterfalls streak the slopes of Khalas and steam wraps around them. None of the falls reaches the bottom. They boil away or disappear into cracks. The biggest are the ones the River Styx makes as it rages across the layer.

**Teardrop Palace:** A pagoda shaped like a nautilus shell, standing beside the Styx with two smaller shrines next to it. An iron fence surrounds all three, and they sit on a ledge that was obviously carved by a god. The pagoda is miles across, and so are the shrines. Between the two shrines is a packed bazaar full of yugoloths, petitioners, devils, demons, other outsiders and the occasional mortal. It sells everything, and nearly all of it was stolen from somewhere else on the Great Wheel, which makes it the greatest black market there is. Prices are high and pickpockets are everywhere, but it is known for rare goods you can't find anywhere else.

### Chamada
The second mount is the most violent. Lava pours down it so thickly that solid, cool ground is hard to find, and it glows so brightly that the sky disappears in the glare. Rivers of lava cool and harden, briefly damming the flow behind them, then burst and spill out in new directions. Vents open without warning and spray fresh rock, and small volcanoes are common. The air is usually full of soft gray ash, falling everywhere like a dreadful snow and often cutting visibility to nothing.

**Nimicri:** From the slopes of Chamada, Nimicri looks like a small moon about two thousand feet across, floating alone above the burning mountain. It is covered in spires, steeples and plainer buildings, linked by a web of streets. Everything is clean, the buildings are comfortable and in good repair, and everyone is perfectly polite. The trading post sells excellent goods of every kind, and Nimicri is a regular stop on several trade routes across the outer planes.

What visitors almost never learn is that Nimicri, buildings and people alike, is one enormous creature pretending to be a town. Sometimes it swallows a visitor whole, and sometimes it lets them go unharmed. If even one drop of a visitor's blood falls anywhere in the town, Nimicri can make a perfect copy of them, memories and all, up to the moment the blood was spilled. A citizen of Nimicri dragged out of town dies on the spot, like a limb cut off a body.

**Tower Arcane:** A tower rising high above the lava and ash of Chamada, covered in blades and spikes that promise a painful death to anyone unwelcome. Yugoloth wizards control it, and it is where their race keeps its records. The whole history of the yugoloths is stored here, well guarded and heavily encrypted behind warding glyphs, symbols and other protective magic.

Inside, flayed petitioners hang from chains, and the wizards use their blood as ink. Torture equipment covers every wall that isn't lined with bookshelves or sealed archives. Far below the tower is a library of especially horrible contracts with mortals, stretching for miles and protected by the strongest wards the wizards know, against both lava and thieves from other planes. Each contract is burned into the living skin of a petitioner with magic and branding irons. The petitioners hang in long rows of chains, and none of them can think about anything except their own contract and their own pain.

### Mungoth
The third mount is much quieter than Chamada or Khalas. In fact it is cold, and often snowing. A few scattered vents give off about as much light as a full moon, which makes finding your way across Mungoth's icy slopes difficult, and heavy falls of snow and ash sometimes put out even that light. The snow mixed with acidic ash is deadly to anyone without shelter, and the ice that builds up on the slopes makes climbing much worse than anywhere else on the plane.

**Valley of the Outcast:** A deep chasm hiding a realm sheltered from the constant acid snow. In it stands a rough castle built of basalt and giant bones, sized for its owner: an outcast fire giant wizard named Tastuo. Her eight siblings, outcasts like her, live there too, and all of them are wizards or sorcerers. The yugoloths of the layer have several overlapping contracts with Tastuo, which help protect the valley if her enemies ever find it. She never says who those enemies are. Her own situation makes her sympathetic to travelers looking for refuge, so the valley also serves as a safe house for people in trouble, if they can find it.

### Krangath
The fourth mount, called the Dead Furnace, has no volcanic activity at all. It went cold ages ago. Its great mountain is a dark pillar rumbling in the night. The layer is silent. No wind blows and no light shines. Krangath is dead.

There are few petitioners here, and the ones who wander the layer have learned to stay quiet, so Melif won't hear them.

**Hopelorn:** On a carefully kept ledge stands a precise complex of black obsidian, with dim red light showing through narrow slit windows. This is Hopelorn, the fortress of Melif the Lich-Lord, a city of the dead where coffins glow like streetlamps and necromantic energy drifts like wisps along every street.

The undead are welcome here. The living and petitioners are not. Melif sees both as pathetic failures who couldn't even manage their own mortal lives. Here Melif and a circle of liches and other powerful undead casters carry on endless research into life, death and existence. They sometimes capture fiends for outrageous experiments, but never yugoloths, for fear the whole race would come down on Hopelorn. There are rumors that Melif was a yugoloth himself before he turned to dark magic and finally became a lich.

Wathoria's reach ends at Materia and Mortest, which is why liches who flee Materia often end up in places like Hopelorn.
