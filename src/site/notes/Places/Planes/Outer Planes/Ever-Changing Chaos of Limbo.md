## Overview
Limbo is chaos with nothing else mixed in. Left alone, it is a churning soup of fire, air, earth and water in every combination. Fireballs, pockets of air, lumps of rock and walls of water fight for space until the next surge washes each of them away. Scraps of landscape like those on Materia drift through the mess anyway: a patch of forest, a meadow, a ruined castle, a little island.

Limbo has living natives, the githzerai and the slaadi best known among them. Most of its petitioners are mindless, ghostly balls of swirling chaos. The plane has no layers, or if it does, they keep merging and splitting, each as chaotic as the next, and not even the wisest sage could tell them apart.

Limbo is full of strange sights. Bursts of elemental fire light it, some far away and showing only as a faint glow through floating seas, others as bright as a sun and much too close. Sometimes a traveler can't see a thing, buried in a surge of earth, and sometimes they can see for miles through a long pocket of open air. There is no day or night.

## Planar Links
Limbo has permanent portals to many other planes. On the Limbo side, a portal might open into a fireball, a pile of boulders or a howling windstorm, so arriving here is dangerous for anyone who isn't ready for chaos.

## The Shaping of Limbo
Limbo has three kinds of ground: raw, controlled and stabilized. Almost all of it is raw. Controlled and stabilized areas are tiny islands by comparison.

Raw Limbo changes without any pattern. One element surges through an area, wipes out whatever was there, and holds until another surge replaces it. Sometimes two elements mix: earth and fire together make a boiling ball of magma. Very rarely the elements fall into perfect balance and everything goes calm for a while, until the balance breaks.

Controlling raw Limbo is done with the mind, not the body. A traveler who concentrates can force order onto the chaos around them and choose which element, or mix of elements, takes over. Visitors from material worlds usually go for a lump of earth wrapped in a little bubble of air. How much a traveler can control depends on how strong their will is. A weak mind can't control anything. The strongest can hold a sphere many paces across.

Control lasts as long as the controller stays inside the area, or until someone else takes it from them. Controlled areas drift around at random. If two creatures try to take the same area at once, or two controlled areas bump into each other, the sharper mind wins.

A creature with a strong enough will can make a stabilized area in the middle of the area it controls. A stabilized area keeps its shape after the controller lets go. It drifts on Limbo's currents, and if nobody protects it, the surges wear it away eventually. A stabilized ball of fire might survive a few dunkings in water before it goes out. Hard-working creatures gather chunks of stabilized earth together and build on them, especially if they can set guards to look after them.



## Inhabitants
Limbo's best-known peoples are the slaadi and the githzerai. Only the slaadi have been here from the start. The githzerai came much later. They see the whole plane as a test of their will, and it is a hard training ground for their belief that pain is only weakness leaving the body.

Slaadi shape Limbo the same way travelers do, but their control never slips, even when they are asleep or knocked out. The area they control clings tight to their bodies, so it's not unusual to see a slaad lounging, unharmed, in a bath of fire.

The few githzerai who become monks also live by strict law, and they find Limbo's chaos especially hard. The walls of a githzerai monastery keep the chaos out completely. Most githzerai aren't monks, and they live together in big cities.

Limbo's petitioners are people who loved chaos above everything else. Some dissolve into the plane. Others hang on as mad spirits made of shifting chaos-stuff.



## Travel and Conflict
How you get around in Limbo depends on what's around you at the moment. There's no fixed gravity, so "down" is whatever direction you decide it is. A traveler in open air can just pick a direction and fall that way, fast. The same works in a surge of fire, for anyone who can survive it. Where water or earth has taken over, a traveler can swim or dig.

The usual way to travel is to take control of a patch of chaos and turn it into open air, or earth with air above it. Then you take control of the next patch from the edge of the first, step into it, and keep going. The most comfortable way is to choose a chunk of earth with some air over it as "down" and walk on it like normal ground.

Maps are useless here. Even solid buildings drift if you give them long enough. How long a trip takes depends mostly on how well the traveler knows where they're going.

If everyone in a fight agrees on which way is down and stands on something solid, fighting here is no different from fighting anywhere else. But those who have learned to work with Limbo's shifting gravity can make huge leaps, fly out of danger in an instant, and run on walls and ceilings as easily as floors. Anything thrown or shot takes on the thrower's sense of down and falls that way.

## Notable Locations

**Shra'kt'lor:** The biggest githzerai city, ruled by a general called the Great Githzerai. He is a master of both weapons and magic, and his people worship him as a god-king. The city is stern and plain. Seven rings of high, thick granite wall surround it, each pierced by huge iron gatehouses. Living space inside is cramped, but the open market is wide and well stocked with food grown on stabilized earth, along with goods both ordinary and amazing.

**Monastery of Zerth'Ad'lun:** One of many githzerai monasteries, this one following the teachings of Sensei Belthomais, a master of his art. Like many monasteries, it teaches its own fighting style, and students who fully commit to it are called Zerth cenobites. Those who practice zerthi ("Zerth's teaching" in the githzerai tongue) say they can see a moment into the future and fight better for it. From outside, the monastery looks like a small grove of stone spires and towers wrapped around a sphere a quarter-mile across. Inside, winding stairs run from floors onto walls and up onto ceilings, using Limbo's shifting gravity. Every surface is a floor to anyone willing to change which way is down.

**Spawning Stone:** The ancient home of the slaadi, deep in the part of Limbo they control most completely. Each kind of slaad gathers at the stone in turn for a mating season. The slaadi have no sexes, and they fertilize each other's internal egg sacs. When the next kind of slaad in the cycle takes the stone from the last, the departing slaadi carry off the fertilized eggs, which look like seeds, to plant in hosts later. Sometimes young slaadi are born at the stone itself, because in the frenzy the slaadi implant each other. Dead adult slaadi often float around the stone until Limbo breaks them down.

The stone drifts from place to place, and currents of chaos always flow away from it. Slaadi can recognize these currents and follow them upstream to find it. Whenever the stone changes hands, the currents swell into tidal waves and set off chaos storms. A single death slaad guards the stone against anyone who isn't a slaad. This ancient, dangerous sentry is called the Guardian of the Stone, and it is skilled with both weapons and sorcery. The slaadi believe that whoever takes the stone from the Guardian will be reborn as a death slaad with the power of an anarch, able to command the plane to create buildings, plants, animals or even a whole living land.
