## Overview
Limbo is a plane of pure chaos. Untended sections appear as a roiling soup of the four basic elements and every combination of them. Balls of fire, pockets of air, chunks of earth, and waves of water battle for ascendance until each is overcome by another chaotic surge. Landscapes resembling those of the material world drift through the miasma regardless: bits of forest, meadow, ruined castles, and small islands.

Living natives inhabit the plane, the githzerai and the slaadi most prominent among them. Most petitioners here take the form of unthinking ghostly spheres of swirling chaos. Limbo has no layers, or if it does, those layers merge and part continually, each as chaotic as the next, and the wisest sages would struggle to tell one from another.

The plane is filled with wonders beyond counting. Random surges of elemental fire light it, some far off and visible only as a dim glow spreading through floating seas, others bright as suns and far too close. The boil sometimes reduces visibility to nothing, inside a mass of surging earth for instance, and sometimes allows sight for miles through a contiguous stretch of open air. There is no day and no night in Limbo.

## Planar Links
Permanent portals connect Limbo to various other planes. A huge ball of fire, a pile of boulders, or a terrible windstorm may equally well surround the Limbo side of any given portal, which makes entering the plane dangerous for anyone unprepared to deal with chaos.

## The Shaping of Limbo
Three kinds of terrain exist here: raw areas, controlled areas, and stabilized areas. Raw Limbo makes up nearly all of the plane, and the controlled and stabilized areas are tiny islands by comparison.

Raw Limbo shifts without pattern. One element surges to dominance over a stretch of the boil, sweeps away whatever preceded it, and holds until the next surge displaces it in turn. Two elements sometimes mix and produce a hybrid, so that earth and fire together yield a boiling ball of magma. On rare occasions the elemental forces come into exact balance and a brief tranquility follows, which lasts only until the balance breaks.

Controlling a raw area is an exercise of the mind, and the body plays no part in it. A traveler who concentrates can impose order on the chaos immediately around them, allowing a chosen element or mixture to become dominant, and a favorite arrangement among travelers from the material world is a chunk of earth wrapped in a small atmosphere of air. How much can be held depends entirely on the strength of the will applied. The weak-minded manage nothing at all. The strongest hold a sphere many paces across.

Control lasts as long as the controller remains within the area, or until another creature wrests it away, and controlled areas drift at random through the surrounding chaos. Where two creatures seize the same area at once, or where two controlled areas overlap, the sharper mind prevails.

An area becomes stabilized when a creature of sufficiently powerful will creates it at the center of an area of control. A stabilized area keeps its character after the control that made it has lapsed. It drifts on Limbo's currents and, left unprotected, erodes eventually through repeated immersion in the elemental surges. A ball of fire made stable in this way will survive several dunkings in water and dissipate at last. Industrious creatures gather bits of stabilized earth together and use them as foundations for permanent structures, particularly where guardians can be set to tend them.

## Inhabitants
Slaadi and githzerai are the peoples commonly associated with Limbo. Only the slaadi have been natives since time began. The githzerai arrived far later, seeing the whole plane as a challenge to their indomitable spirit, and it makes a harsh proving ground for their particular ethos: pain is weakness leaving your body.

Natives though they are, the slaadi shape Limbo the same way travelers do. A slaad's control never falters even while it sleeps or lies senseless, and the shaped area clings tightly to its body, so that onlookers see a slaad lolling unhurt in a bath of fire.

The few githzerai who follow a monastic calling also follow the dictates of law, and such monks find the chaos of Limbo a particular challenge. The walls of a githzerai monastery hold that chaos out entirely. Most githzerai follow no monastic calling and congregate instead in large cities.

The petitioners of Limbo are those who revered chaos above all else. Some come to embody the plane. Others linger as insane spirits composed of shifting limbo-stuff.

## Travel and Conflict
Movement here depends entirely on local conditions, as everything else does. Direction in Limbo is a matter of will, since no fixed pull governs the plane, so a traveler in open air may simply decide which way is down and fall that way at considerable speed. The same holds in a surging mass of fire, for anyone able to survive the passage. Swimming through water and burrowing through earth are options where those elements dominate.

The usual method is to seize control of a stretch of the boil and shape it into open air, or into earth with air above it, then take hold of an adjacent stretch from the edge of the first, move into it, and repeat the process across the plane. The most comforting version of this is to let one's feet fall toward a chunk of earth with an atmosphere hanging over it and walk on it normally.

Maps are useless in the chaotic expanse. Even solid, permanent structures drift on Limbo's currents given enough time, and how long a journey takes depends chiefly on how well the traveler knows the place they are going.

Where every combatant agrees on which way is down and stands on a solid surface, a fight here differs in nothing from a fight anywhere else. Those who have learned to work with Limbo's shifting sense of direction can make amazing leaps, fly clear of a threat in an instant, and run along walls and ceilings as readily as floors. Anyone who shoots or hurls a weapon lends it their own sense of down, and it falls accordingly.

## Notable Locations

**Shra'kt'lor:** The largest githzerai city, ruled by a general called the Great Githzerai, a master of arms and magic both, revered as a deity-king. The city is austere, with massive iron gatehouses piercing seven concentric rings of high, thick granite wall. Quarters inside are cramped, though the open market is broad and well supplied with produce grown on stabilized earth alongside other goods mundane and wondrous.

**Monastery of Zerth'Ad'lun:** One of many githzerai monasteries, following the teaching of Sensei Belthomais, a master of his art. Belthomais teaches a specialized martial style, as many monasteries do, and students who fully embrace it are called Zerth cenobites. Those who practice zerthi, which means Zerth's teaching in the githzerai tongue, claim to see a moment into the future and to fight the better for it. From outside, the monastery resembles a small glade of stone spires and towers layered around a sphere a quarter-mile across. Taking full advantage of Limbo's shifting sense of direction, its interior runs winding stairs that connect floors to walls and to ceilings. Every surface is a floor to anyone willing to adjust their own sense of which way is down.

**Spawning Stone:** The primordial home of the slaadi, located in a realm of their greatest dominion. Each race of slaad converges on the stone for a season of mating. The unisex slaadi mate there in turn, fertilizing one another's internal egg sacs, and when the next race in the cycle wrests the stone away from the previous group, the slaadi carry the seedlike fertilized eggs off for later implantation into host bodies. Young slaadi are sometimes produced at the stone itself, because the slaadi implant one another in their mating frenzy, and dead adult slaadi routinely float about the stone until the chaos of Limbo destroys them.

The stone drifts from place to place, and currents of chaos-stuff always flow away from it. Slaadi recognize those currents and follow them upstream. The currents grow into tsunamis and give birth to chaos storms whenever the stone changes hands. One death slaad guards it at all times against non-slaad visitors, an ancient and formidable sentinel called the Guardian of the Stone, skilled in arms and sorcery alike. The slaadi believe that wresting control of the stone from the Guardian allows the victor to be reborn as a death slaad with the powers of an anarch, whose command over the plane can call into being buildings, vegetation, animals, and an entire ecosystem if desired.
