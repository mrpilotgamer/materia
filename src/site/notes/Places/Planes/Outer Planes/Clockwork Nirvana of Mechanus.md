## Overview
Mechanus is order taken as far as it can go. Light and dark are measured out equally, and so are heat and cold. Everything about it is as regular as a water clock. The plane is one endless machine of interlocking gears, each turning at its own set speed. Together they seem to be working out some enormous calculation. No god knows what it is for, only that it has something to do with law.

At first glance Mechanus looks simple, but it isn't. Every kind of law exists here, from plain common sense to fiendishly tangled codes of etiquette. For the most part there is no passion here, no illusion and no pain. The goal of the plane is perfection, and perfection means every mind folded into the whole.

Mechanus has no layers. It is one endless space full of giant gears, like the inside of a clock. Some mesh at right angles and some side by side. The gears, called cogs, are made of stone, soil and metal ore, as if a god had cut them out of the crust of a world.

Many cogs are over a thousand miles across and turn so slowly that nobody can see them move. They do move, though, as the small island-sized cogs meshed with them show. Those small ones seem to spin fast enough to make an onlooker dizzy, yet anyone standing on one feels nothing. Gravity pulls toward the face of every cog, so nobody is flung off unless they stand within a few steps of the edge.

A white light comes out of the empty space itself for twelve hours, then goes dark for twelve. Sight and hearing work normally, though near the edge of a cog you can sometimes hear the deep grind of gear against gear.

## Planar Links
Portals into Mechanus usually open at the center of a cog. Some lead to other outer planes, some to the inner planes, some to the Astral or Ethereal, and some only to the face of another cog.

Portals look like pale green gear-shaped rings, turning slowly. Most open once each time their cog completes a turn. Some only open when several cogs line up in a certain way, and which cogs those are isn't always obvious. Once a portal opens it usually stays open for at least an hour, sometimes a full day.

## Divine Realms
**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Dilliminner, God of Caution\|Dilliminner]]**, Lord of Preparations, holds the Hourglass Bastion on Mechanus. No Progenitor holds a realm here. The bastion is built into one vast, slow gear, and its walls grow a little thicker with every turn.

## Inhabitants
People on Mechanus live on the faces of the turning cogs. The bare cogs have no plants or animals of their own, but settlers from other planes, including material worlds, the Nine Hells and Celestia, have turned many cog faces into hedge mazes, parks and nature preserves.

The most common creatures here aren't alive. They are constructs called inevitables, and their job is to enforce the laws of the universe. Three kinds are well known. Kolyaruts hunt fugitives. Maruts go after those who cheat death. Zelekhuts enforce contracts. Each kind finds and punishes the one kind of lawbreaker it was built for, and it never stops. Maruts serve no god. Their hunt for those who cheat death runs alongside Wathoria's, but they act on their own.

## Petitioners
Petitioners on Mechanus often take a neat, simplified version of the body they had in life. However they look, they all share an unsettling honesty and very little sense of self. They take everything literally, and some refuse to follow any instruction at all in case they misunderstand it.



## Travel and Conflict
Getting around Mechanus is a lot like getting around Materia. Stepping from one turning cog to another looks frightening to newcomers, but with a little care it's easy enough. A bad slip drops a traveler between the gear teeth, which grind them to death unless they can climb out, and climbing out is hard. Anyone who can fly or teleport can skip all of this and cross the gaps directly.



## Notable Locations

**Naumannus:** Inevitables are built, and each is given a law to uphold. Nobody knows who built the first one, and the inevitables say they don't know either. They learned early how to build more of themselves, and Naumannus is one of their factories. It covers a small cog and has no ordinary life on it at all, only inevitables working day and night. Tall chimneys cover both faces of the cog and pour out soot from the forges below without a break.

Inside are room after room of crystal molds in every shape. Special constructs pour molten metal into the molds and shape and harden it. The finished parts are put together and brought to life in pools of a thick, oily liquid. After a short test, each new inevitable is sent out into the planes with its first job. The job is written onto it in glowing runes passed down by the Hub of Elders. Every factory has its own hub: a few hundred special inevitables who spend their time watching the cosmos for broken laws. Inevitables will sometimes take orders from other great powers of law on the outer planes for a short time.

**Delon-Estin Oti:** A perfectly symmetrical town that takes up a small part of a much bigger cog. It has twenty walls with a gate in each, and just inside the gates farmers grow fruit and vegetables and keep animals. From above, the town looks like a spiderweb: roads run out from the center in rings and spokes, and at the very center is an open lawn ringed by one circle of trees.

The town is quiet and calm, and so set in its routines that the people know what's going to happen next. They finish visitors' sentences for them and cut off strangers who haven't caught up yet. People who want perfect equality, and who want to be free of both base and lofty passions, come to live here. Its people come from every race on the Great Wheel, all equal, with nobody in charge. Many petitioners live in and around the town and are happy to work its farms. Delon-Estin Oti is known for producing seers and attracting fortune-tellers, so plenty of visitors come hoping to learn their futures.

**Fortress of Disciplined Enlightenment:** A fortress with a cog to itself, two miles across, with towers that rise twice that high into the void. Inevitables that enforce the law against trespassing walk its walls, watching for fiends trying to sneak in and formians trying to settle.

The fortress belongs to the Fraternity of Order, a group of mortals who believe that anyone who learns every law of the cosmos will have the power of a god. They built their fortress here on the plane of law to work on it. An army of clerks, lawyers, translators, mathematicians, philosophers and bureaucrats keep the place running, most of them members of the Fraternity. Outsiders are sometimes allowed to study in its libraries, which fill hundreds of rooms with books of law collected from all over the Great Wheel.
