*"The meeting place of opposed alignments"*
*"The fulcrum of the Outer Planes"*
*"The hub of the Great Wheel"*

## Overview
The Outlands is the only outer plane that touches every other one, so it is the common ground for beings from all of them. Fiends and celestials both turn up here, and so do creatures of law and of chaos.

The plane is an endless flat wheel with a great spire in the middle. Outlanders call the spire the heart of the outer planes, the axle the Great Wheel turns on. It can be seen from anywhere on the plane, climbing past the clouds and out of sight. Sigil, the City of Doors, floats at its top.

The land itself is varied: open prairie, high mountains and shallow, winding rivers. Settlements of petitioners and natives are scattered across it, tiny specks in a lot of wild country.

Past the portal towns and the domains of the neutral powers, the land opens up. There are grassy hills, deep canyons, thick forests and jagged mountains. Day and night come and go much as on Materia, but there is no sun and no stars. The sky simply gets light, and twelve hours later it gets dark.

Thick fog and mist are common. Travelers sometimes get lost in them and come out closer to a hostile portal town than they meant to.

## Outlands Magic
Magic works normally at the edges of the Outlands and gets weaker the closer you get to the spire. About eleven hundred miles out, the most powerful spells start to falter and take far more effort to cast. Further in, weaker and weaker magic falters the same way.

At about nine hundred miles, the most powerful spells stop working altogether, and every step closer shuts off more. Then the natural magical abilities of creatures stop working, and at last even the powers of gods are cancelled. On the steep face of the spire itself, almost nothing works. Only abilities that are completely natural, with no magic in them at all, still function.

## Planar Links
The Outlands borders the top layer of every other outer plane. About a thousand miles from the spire there is a ring of semipermanent settlements called portal towns. Each grew up around a portal to another plane, and traffic flows through them both ways.

The plane also has free-standing portals to material worlds and to Sigil. Since Sigil has portals to every portal town, the City of Doors is the center of the center of the outer planes.

## Divine Realms
No Progenitor and none of Materia's gods holds a realm on the Outlands.

## Inhabitants
Because the Outlands is the crossroads of the outer planes, natives of any plane can turn up here at any time. Visitors from elsewhere tend to gather in the portal towns that lead to their home planes.

## Petitioners
Petitioners are common in the portal towns and at certain spots around the plane, such as old libraries, museums and crypts. They usually look human or close to it, and they mostly live and let live. Leave them alone and they will leave you alone.

Petitioners found in the open country between towns are usually wanderers looking for new lands, or shepherds who are happy with their afterlife as it is.



Some petitioners on the Outlands look like they belong somewhere else: brave warriors who should be with the glorious dead of Ysgard, or half-melted lemures who belong in the Hells. Some got lost or sent the wrong way. Others were not quite good enough or evil enough to end up on an aligned plane. Whether they are looking for their reward or running from it, they break the usual rule that petitioners can't move between planes.

## Travel and Conflict
The Outlands is steadiest close to the spire. Past the ring of portal towns, the land shifts and changes.

Out there, distance stops behaving, and it works in a traveler's favor. However far you go past the ring, the nearest portal town is never more than a few weeks away. You can walk two thousand miles out from the spire, turn around, and find a portal town a few hundred miles off. Nobody knows why. It seems that the further you get from the spire, the less the ground in between counts. Nobody has ever found the edge of the plane.

The plane doesn't help or hurt anyone in a fight, though fog and mist give plenty of cover and the ground is rough.



## Notable Locations

**Portal Towns:** A rough ring of small towns about a thousand miles from the foot of the spire, each built near a natural gate to another outer plane. There are other portals elsewhere, but the towns are easy to find and a handy place to stop for anyone headed to a particular plane.

Each portal town takes on the nature of the plane its portal leads to. Towns next to lawful planes are tidy and orderly, and towns next to chaotic planes sprawl and ramble. Towns by good planes welcome peaceful travelers, and towns by evil planes are hostile. Most of the people in them are petitioners and natives of the plane next door, so there are demons in and around Plague-Mort, the town by the Abyss, and celestials near Fortitude and Excelsior.

**Sigil:** The heart of the Outlands, and by its own account the center of all the planes. It is called the City of Doors because every district is full of portals to the rest of the cosmos. Sigil is a ring floating above the spire, with the city built on the inside of the ring, so "down" is always toward the ring. Anyone who falls off the ring is suddenly in open air, usually tumbling down the side of the spire.

Magic barely works near the spire, but inside Sigil it works normally. Outside the city walls, the rules of the Outlands take over again. No god can enter Sigil. Whether that is the Lady of Pain's doing, an effect of the spire, or some agreement among the gods, nobody knows.

Nobody knows how many portals there are in Sigil, not even the people who live there. There are portals to every known outer plane, every inner plane, and rumored gates to other material worlds. Others link Sigil to places on the Outlands, usually the portal towns. Every one of them needs a command word or a key item to open.

Sigil is a trading city. Goods and news pour in from every plane, and there is a lively trade in information, especially the words and items that open particular portals. These portal keys are in high demand, and most visitors are hunting for either a certain portal or the key to it.

Sigil is run by factions, which could politely be called philosophers with clubs. They split along familiar lines of belief, and each controls its own part of the city and its own share of the city's services. Above them all is a mysterious being called the Lady of Pain, a floating woman with hair like blades. Nobody knows what she can do or how powerful she is, and most people assume she is at least as strong as the gods.

**Syngali:** The city of Syngali stands at the foot of the spire, a meeting place for opposing sides. Petitioners and mortals with clashing beliefs come here to argue about how the multiverse should be run, protected by the spire's effects and by the plane itself.

What the Lady of Pain is, and where she stands beside the Primordials, the Progenitors and the gods, the archive cannot say.
