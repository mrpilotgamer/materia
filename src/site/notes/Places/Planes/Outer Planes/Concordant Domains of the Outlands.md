## Overview
The Outlands is unique among the Outer Planes in bordering all the others, which makes it the common ground for extraplanar creatures. Beings from infernal and celestial planes alike are found here, as are those of law and of chaos.

The plane is an infinitely large wheel with a great spire rising from its center. Outlanders consider that towering cylindrical plinth the heart of the Outer Planes and the axle the Great Wheel turns on. It is visible from anywhere on the plane, rising above the clouds and ascending into unreachable heavens. Sigil, the City of Doors, floats at its top.

The Outlands itself is a broad region of varied terrain, with open prairies, towering mountains, and twisting shallow rivers. Settlements are scattered throughout, inhabited by petitioners and other natives, and they are small flecks against the greater wildness of the plane.

Beyond the portal towns and the domains of the neutral deities, the land opens up considerably. Rolling grass-covered hills, deep canyons, thick forests, and sharp-toothed mountains dot the landscape. The Outlands keeps a cycle of day and night much like the material world, with no obvious sun or stars. The sky simply brightens, and twelve hours later it darkens again.

Heavy fogs and mists are common. Travelers occasionally lose their way in them and find themselves nearer an unfriendly portal town than they had intended.

## Outlands Magic
Magic behaves normally in the far reaches of the Outlands and grows steadily more constrained as one approaches the central spire. Somewhere around eleven hundred miles from its base, the most demanding workings begin to falter and require far greater effort to complete. Closer in, progressively simpler magic falters in the same way.

At roughly nine hundred miles the constraint becomes a prohibition. The most demanding workings cannot be performed at all, and each further step inward closes off more. Abilities innate to a creature, owing nothing to deliberate working, fail in their turn, and at the last even the powers of deities are annulled. Where the surface of the plinth stands near vertical, almost nothing functions. Only those capacities that are wholly natural to a creature, owing nothing to magic, survive the approach.

## Planar Links
The Outlands borders the top layer of every other Outer Plane. A number of semipermanent settlements called portal towns stand roughly a thousand miles from the spire, grown up around existing portals to those other planes, and they see a regular flow of traffic in both directions.

The plane also holds freestanding portals to the material world, along with portals to Sigil. Because portals connect Sigil to each of the portal towns, the City of Doors is the heart of the heart of the Outer Planes.

## Inhabitants
As the crossroads of the Outer Planes, the Outlands may hold any native of any of them within its borders at any time. Creatures from elsewhere tend to cluster at the portal towns that open onto their own planes.

## Petitioners
Petitioners are common in the portal towns and at specific locations scattered across the plane, including ancient libraries, museums, and crypts. They are generally human or humanoid in appearance and tend toward a live-and-let-live attitude. Left alone, they have no interest in troubling anyone else.

Those encountered in the wide spaces between communities are usually wanderers seeking new lands, or shepherds content with their lot in the afterlife.

Petitioners who seem to belong to other planes also walk the Outlands: valiant warriors who should be among Ysgard's glorious dead, half-melted lemures who belong in Hell, lost or misdirected. They may equally have been not quite good or evil enough to merit inclusion on an aligned plane. Whether they are seeking their just reward or avoiding it, they stand as exceptions to the rule that petitioners cannot move between planes.

## Travel and Conflict
The Outlands seems most stable near the spire. Travelers who pass beyond the ring of portal towns find the land mutable and continually shifting.

Distance itself becomes unreliable out there, and in a useful direction. However far a traveler goes beyond the ring, the nearest portal town is never more than a few weeks away. One can walk two thousand miles outward from the spire, turn around, and find a portal town within a few hundred miles. Why distance distorts this way is unknown. It seems that the farther one goes from the spire, the less the intervening ground signifies. Nobody has yet found an edge to the plane.

The plane neither aids nor hinders a fight, though mists and fogs offering concealment are common and the terrain runs rugged.

## Notable Locations

**Portal Towns:** A string of small communities arranged in a rough circle about a thousand miles from the base of the spire, so called because they stand near natural entrances to the other Outer Planes. Other portals exist elsewhere, and these towns are easy to find and convenient stopping places for anyone seeking a particular plane.

Each portal town takes on the traits of the plane beyond its portal. Those bordering lawful planes tend to be neat and orderly, while those near the planes of chaos are unstructured and rambling. Those beside good planes are hospitable to peaceful travelers, and those beside evil ones are hostile. The inhabitants are usually petitioners and natives of the associated plane, so travelers find demons in and around Plague-Mort, the portal town to the Abyss, and celestial creatures near Fortitude and Excelsior.

**Sigil:** The heart of the Outlands and the self-proclaimed center of the planes, known as the City of Doors. Portals leading throughout the cosmos lace every district. Sigil sits atop the spire as a ring floating in space, with the city built along the inside of the band, so that down is toward the ring itself. Anyone who escapes the ring finds themselves suddenly in open air, generally plummeting down the side of the spire.

Magic is severely limited near the spire, and yet within Sigil it functions normally. Past the city's walls, the conditions of the surrounding Outlands resume. Sigil is also apparently proof against deities, whether by the Lady of Pain's desire, by the city's place atop the spire, or by some agreement among the deities themselves. None of them enters.

The city holds a vast number of portals, the total unknown even to its inhabitants. There are portals to every known Outer Plane, every Inner Plane, and rumored gates to alternate material worlds. Others connect Sigil to locations in the Outlands, usually the portal towns. All of them require command words or command items to function.

Sigil is a trader's city. Goods, merchandise, and information arrive from across the planes, and there is a brisk trade in information about the planes, particularly in the command words and items that operate particular portals. These portal keys are much sought after, and most travelers in the city are looking for either a specific portal or the key that opens it.

A number of factions control Sigil, all of which might politely be described as philosophers with clubs. They divide along familiar ideological lines and hold different parts of the city and different services within it. The ultimate ruler is an enigmatic being called the Lady of Pain, a floating female humanoid with blade-like hair. Her power and abilities are unknown, and it is widely assumed that she equals or exceeds the deities themselves.

**Syngali:** At the bottom of the spire sits the city of Syngali, a meeting point for many opposing sides. Petitioners and mortals of opposed ideals come here to discuss and argue the presidings of the multiverse, under the protection of the spire's effects and of the plane itself.
