## Overview
Hades lies at the lowest point of the lower planes, positioned between two fiendish races committed to each other's destruction. Its gray plains are darkened again and again by demon armies meeting devil armies, neither side asking quarter nor granting it. Archivists who catalogue the planes by their moral character place Hades at the pole of evil, and the accounts collected here do nothing to argue against that placement.

What distinguishes the Gray Waste from its neighbors is not violence. The rage of the Abyss and the cold scheming of the Nine Hells both yield here to something heavier. Evil in Hades works as a spiritual weight that drags on every creature within it, and hopelessness subordinates every other passion. Travelers describe the plane as killing ambition by degrees, leaving behind the dried husk of whatever fire a visitor arrived with. Those who remain long enough stop caring about the things that brought them.

The plane is divided into three layers, called glooms. The same uncaring malevolence saturates all three.

The name is literal. The earth is gray, the sky is gray, and the petitioners are gray. Color does not exist in the glooms, and visitors report that sight itself seems altered at the moment of arrival, the world resolving into white, black, and the shades between. No sun, moon, or stars hang overhead. A bleak radiance simply emanates from the sky with no visible source.

The grayness is not only a matter of vision. It reaches into the hearts of those who linger, and the petitioners, who linger permanently, are void of feeling altogether. They neither laugh nor weep. They despair, and their hope does not return. Two phenomena recorded separately by planar scholars, the plane's tendency to hold those who enter and the spiritual wasting known as the grays, are now generally understood as the same grayness expressed in different forms.

## Planar Links
The River Styx crosses the uppermost gloom, and several of its lesser tributaries are said to run deeper into the plane. As along every stretch of that river, ferrymen of ill repute work its length and will carry passengers to other planes for a price.

Portals are reasonably common on the uppermost gloom of Oinos. They take the form of great spinning coins, and their metal indicates their destination: gold for Carceri, silver for the Outlands, copper for Gehenna, and platinum, rarest of the four, for the Astral Plane. Because everything else in the Waste is drained of color, these coins can be sighted from miles away.

## Inhabitants
Foul creatures of nearly every description pass through the Gray Waste. As the battleground of the lower planes, it draws demons, devils, slaadi, formians, and on occasion even a deva, whether spying for the war effort or deserted from it. Yugoloths remain numerous despite the migration of most of their race to neighboring Gehenna, which was not their original home. Hades was.

Night hags are also plentiful. They hunt without pause for the petitioners called larvae, which serve them as a form of spiritual currency in their dealings with other evil powers.

Beyond the detritus of the Blood War, the hags, and the petitioners, the plane supports herds of fiery nightmares.

## Petitioners
Most petitioners in Hades are grayish ghosts, spirits so drained by the Waste that they have lost solidity. They seldom speak. Accounts consistently describe them crowding around living visitors the way moths crowd a candle, drawn to the warmth of emotion and hope that the living still carry.

The spirits of the particularly selfish and malicious arrive as a distinct kind of petitioner called a larva. Larvae take the form of worms bearing heads that still resemble the faces of their mortal bodies. They function as currency throughout the lower planes, valued by night hags, liches, demons, devils, and yugoloths alike, and are as often consumed as spent or burned to fuel a working. Occasionally one is elevated into a lesser fiend, an outcome the larvae themselves are said to regard as fortunate.

## Travel and Conflict
Moving through Hades is little different from moving through the material world, and the plane imposes no unusual burden on the body. It works instead on the will. The hatefulness of the place makes those who fight in it unwilling to withdraw even when badly wounded, and engagements in the Gray Waste tend to continue until one side is destroyed.

## Layers

### Oinos
The first gloom is a country of stunted trees, roving fiends, and virulent sickness, though war has shaped it more than any of these. Oinos is the central battlefield of the Blood War. Fiends, warrior-slaves, trained beasts, and hired companies assemble here for engagements of a scale that beggars the accounts describing them, and the fighting has ruined ground that was bleak to begin with. The noise of it, claws and weapons and screaming, carries across the whole layer.

**Khin-Oin the Wasting Tower:** A tower twenty miles high that resembles nothing so much as a spinal column standing upright. Some hold that it is exactly that, the backbone of a slain deity, killed by yugoloths in an age no record reaches. Khin-Oin descends as far below the gray soil as it rises above, so its lower levels run twenty miles into the earth.

An ultraloth prince named Mydianchlarus rules the tower. Certain accounts claim the yugoloth race itself began here, rising out of a pit at Khin-Oin's absolute base. No one but yugoloths has ever held the place, despite the fiendish armies permanently arrayed outside it. Its rooms and floors are reported to have no end. Visitors and spies have described spawning vats, magical laboratories, meditation chambers, orreries, yugoloth apartments, and entire floors given over to drill fields and battlegrounds.

Mydianchlarus rules from the summit, and the token of that rule is the Siege Malicious. Whoever holds the Wasting Tower carries the title of oinoloth. Any creature capable of fighting its way to the topmost chamber may claim the title, which requires defeating the sitting ruler and then taking the throne.

**The Siege Malicious:** An artifact of the first order, and the seat of the oinoloth. It is an immovable throne of enormous size, carved from the stone of the Wasting Tower and inlaid with tarnished silver, base copper, and brass. A circlet of rubies crowns the high seat, which was cut to hold a creature far larger than any human. Accounts note with some amusement that ordinary claimants sit in it with their legs swinging well clear of the floor.

The throne does not answer to those who have not earned it. A claimant who takes the seat while the previous oinoloth still lives is infected with a virulent strain of the wasting sickness that bears the plane's name, and the infection strips away presence and force of personality permanently. Those immune to disease take no such harm, and the throne simply remains inert to them.

For a claimant who has killed their predecessor, the throne yields its power, and it marks them for it. Skin sloughs from the new oinoloth in a manner witnesses describe as difficult to watch, and the disfigurement cannot be healed by any magic without also surrendering the title. What the mark purchases is absolute command of disease throughout Oinos. The oinoloth may conceive of a sickness, alter an existing one, or nullify it entirely, and may do so as quickly as the thought forms, though the naming of a new plague is said to take considerably longer than its invention.

The limits on this power are understood to be real. The oinoloth commands disease only while within Hades, though plagues devised on Oinos have escaped the layer. The throne need not be occupied for the power to function. An oinoloth carries it wherever they walk in the plane, and can infect a living creature at a distance of a few hundred feet with no opportunity for the victim to resist.

### Niflheim
The second gloom is a layer of gray mist, endlessly twisting among sickly trees and ominous bluffs. The fog limits sight to roughly a hundred feet even for eyes that see in darkness, muffles sound to the point that listening becomes unreliable, and saturates everything it touches with damp. Niflheim has suffered less from the Blood War than Oinos has, most likely because the mist makes coordinated battle impractical. Predators take advantage of the same concealment, and fiendish dire wolves and trolls are both common.

**Death of Innocence:** A small town set among misty pines and built from hewn timber taken out of the surrounding forest. More than five thousand mortals and petitioners live behind its walls, though they largely keep to their dwellings, which gives the streets an abandoned character. Great wooden gates bar the entrance, and gates and outer wall alike bristle with spikes. A broad avenue runs from the gate to a fountain of gray marble at the town's center.

The timber of the buildings and gates weeps blood in place of sap. This is taken as confirmation of the common belief that petitioners are bound inside the wood. Whatever the cause, the walls hold: neither the grays nor the plane's tendency to trap those who enter has any force within Death of Innocence, and its residents sometimes recover enough will to try to better their circumstances, which happens nowhere else in the Waste.

### Pluton
The third gloom is a layer of dying willows, shriveled olive trees, and poplars so dark they read as black. It is a place no one wishes to be and no one can recall choosing. The petitioners, of course, were given no choice.

The Blood War rarely reaches this far down. Raids have occurred when one side or the other wanted the spirit of some fallen mortal commander whose tactical judgment was worth retrieving.

**Underworld:** A realm enclosed by walls of gray marble running for hundreds of miles and visible for thousands more. A single double gate of beaten bronze pierces them, dented and scored by the heroes who have tried to force it. The gate is kept by a fiendish beast of enormous size, a three-headed hound assembled from the squirming, decaying bodies of hundreds of petitioners.

Past the gate the land differs little from the land outside it: blackened trees, stunted brush, wasted ground. Larvae writhe everywhere in the dust alongside gray wraithlike petitioners in the final stage of being drawn empty of feeling. When the last of it goes, what remains of them dissolves into the gloom of Pluton itself.

Heroes and desperate lovers out of the material world do reach this layer on occasion, by a tributary of the Styx or through portals hidden in the great volcanic fissures. They come believing they can find the spirit of someone they lost and carry it out of a hopeless eternity. Alongside the larvae, the faded petitioners, and these occasional mortals, demons, devils, and yugoloths walk the land looking for anything worth taking.
