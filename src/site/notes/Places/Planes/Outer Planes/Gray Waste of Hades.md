## Overview
Hades sits at the very bottom of the lower planes, wedged between two races of fiends that want each other wiped out. Over and over, armies of demons and devils meet on its gray plains, and neither side takes prisoners. Scholars who rank the planes by morality put Hades at the far end of evil, and nothing recorded here argues with them.

The Gray Waste is worse than violent. The fury of the Abyss and the cold plotting of the Nine Hells both give way here to something heavier. Evil in Hades is a weight on the spirit that drags on every creature in it, and hopelessness smothers every other feeling. Travelers say the plane kills ambition slowly, until whatever fire a visitor came in with is burned down to a dry husk. Stay long enough and you stop caring about whatever brought you here.

The plane has three layers, called glooms. The same uncaring malice soaks all three.

The name is meant literally. The ground is gray, the sky is gray and the petitioners are gray. There is no color in the glooms, and visitors say their eyes change the moment they arrive, so the world shows only in white, black and the grays between. There is no sun, moon or stars. A dull light just comes out of the sky from nowhere in particular.

The gray gets into the hearts of anyone who stays, and the petitioners, who stay forever, feel nothing at all. They don't laugh and they don't cry. They despair, and their hope never comes back. Planar scholars once wrote about two separate effects here: the way the plane holds on to anyone who enters, and a wasting of the spirit called the grays. Most now agree these are the same grayness showing up in two ways.

## Planar Links
The River Styx crosses the top gloom, and some of its smaller branches are said to run deeper. As everywhere along the Styx, shady ferrymen work the river and will take passengers to other planes for a price.

Portals are fairly common on Oinos, the top gloom. They look like huge spinning coins, and the metal tells you where they go: gold to Carceri, silver to the Outlands, copper to Gehenna, and platinum, the rarest, to the Astral Plane. Since everything else in the Waste is colorless, the coins can be spotted from miles away.

## Divine Realms
**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Blanc, the White Progenitor Demon\|Blanc]]**, Void of the Soul, rules the Blank Expanse. It is the strongest domain in Hades. Blanc is the emptiness at the heart of life, the pull that anyone who has felt the call of the void will recognize.

No god of Materia holds a realm in Hades.

## Inhabitants
Foul creatures of almost every kind pass through the Gray Waste. As the battlefield of the lower planes, it draws demons, devils, slaadi, formians, and now and then even a deva, either spying for one side or deserting from it. Yugoloths are still common here, even though most of their race has moved to Gehenna. Hades, not Gehenna, is where they came from.

Night hags are common too. They hunt constantly for the petitioners called larvae, which they use as money in their dealings with other evil powers.

Apart from the leftovers of the Blood War, the hags and the petitioners, the plane is home to herds of fiery nightmares.

## Petitioners
Most petitioners in Hades are grayish ghosts, spirits drained so badly by the Waste that they've lost their solid form. They rarely speak. Again and again, visitors report them crowding around the living like moths around a candle, drawn to the feeling and hope the living still carry.

The souls of the especially selfish and cruel arrive as a different kind of petitioner called a larva. A larva is a worm with a head that still looks like the face it had in life. Larvae are used as currency across the lower planes, and night hags, liches, demons, devils and yugoloths all value them. They are as likely to be eaten, or burned to power a spell, as spent. Once in a while one is raised up into a lesser fiend, which the larvae supposedly think of as good luck.



## Travel and Conflict
Moving through Hades is not much different from moving through Materia. The plane doesn't wear on the body. It wears on the will. The place is so hateful that fighters here won't pull back even when badly hurt, so battles in the Gray Waste usually go on until one side is destroyed.

## Layers

### Oinos
The first gloom is a land of stunted trees, roaming fiends and nasty diseases, but war has shaped it more than anything else. Oinos is the main battlefield of the Blood War. Fiends, slave soldiers, war beasts and hired companies gather here for battles too big for any account to really describe, and the fighting has wrecked land that was bleak to begin with. The noise, claws and blades and screaming, carries across the whole layer.

**Khin-Oin the Wasting Tower:** A tower twenty miles tall that looks exactly like a spine standing on end. Some say that's what it is: the backbone of a god the yugoloths killed in an age older than any record. Khin-Oin goes as far down into the gray soil as it rises above it, so its lower levels run twenty miles underground.

An ultraloth prince named Mydianchlarus rules the tower. Some accounts say the yugoloth race began here, climbing out of a pit at the very bottom of Khin-Oin. Nobody but yugoloths has ever held the tower, even though fiendish armies are always camped outside it. Its rooms and floors seem to go on forever. Visitors and spies have described breeding vats, magic laboratories, meditation rooms, orreries, yugoloth living quarters, and whole floors given over to drill yards and battlegrounds.

Mydianchlarus rules from the top floor, and the sign of his rule is the Siege Malicious. Whoever holds the Wasting Tower holds the title of oinoloth. Anyone who can fight their way to the top room can claim the title, by killing whoever holds it and taking the throne.

**The Siege Malicious:** A first-rank artifact and the seat of the oinoloth. It is a huge throne that cannot be moved, carved from the stone of the Wasting Tower and inlaid with tarnished silver, dull copper and brass. A ring of rubies crowns the tall back. It was made for something far bigger than a human, and visitors like to point out that ordinary claimants sit in it with their legs dangling well off the floor.

The throne won't serve anyone who hasn't earned it. If someone sits in it while the last oinoloth is still alive, they catch a vicious form of the wasting sickness the plane is named for, and it strips away their presence and force of personality for good. Those immune to disease aren't harmed, but the throne does nothing for them.

For someone who has killed the last oinoloth, the throne gives up its power, and it marks them for it. The new oinoloth's skin peels away in a way witnesses say is hard to watch, and no magic can heal it without giving up the title. In exchange, the oinoloth gains total command of disease across Oinos. They can dream up a new sickness, change an existing one or wipe one out, as fast as they can think it, though naming a new plague is said to take much longer than making it.

The power has real limits. The oinoloth only commands disease while in Hades, though plagues made on Oinos have escaped the layer. The throne doesn't need to be occupied for the power to work. The oinoloth carries it anywhere on the plane and can infect a living creature a few hundred feet away without giving them any chance to resist.

### Niflheim
The second gloom is a layer of gray mist, forever curling through sickly trees and threatening cliffs. The fog cuts sight to about a hundred feet, even for eyes that see in the dark. It muffles sound so much that you can't trust your ears, and it soaks everything it touches. The Blood War has hit Niflheim less than Oinos, probably because the fog makes organized battle almost impossible. Predators use the same cover, and fiendish dire wolves and trolls are both common.

**Death of Innocence:** A small town among misty pines, built from logs cut from the forest around it. More than five thousand mortals and petitioners live inside its walls, though most stay indoors, so the streets feel deserted. Big wooden gates close off the entrance, and the gates and walls are covered in spikes. A wide street runs from the gate to a gray marble fountain in the middle of town.

The wood of the buildings and gates bleeds in place of sap. Most people take that as proof that petitioners are trapped inside the wood. Whatever the reason, the walls work: neither the grays nor the plane's hold on visitors has any power inside Death of Innocence. Its people sometimes get back enough will to try to improve their lot, which happens nowhere else in the Waste.

### Pluton
The third gloom is a layer of dying willows, shriveled olive trees and poplars so dark they look black. Nobody wants to be here and nobody remembers choosing to come. The petitioners, of course, never had a choice.

The Blood War rarely reaches this deep. There have been raids when one side wanted the soul of some dead mortal commander whose battle sense was worth recovering.

**Underworld:** A realm walled in by gray marble that runs for hundreds of miles and can be seen for thousands. There is only one way in, a double gate of hammered bronze, dented and scratched by the heroes who've tried to break it. A giant fiendish hound guards the gate. It has three heads, and its body is built from the squirming, rotting bodies of hundreds of petitioners.

Past the gate, the land looks much like the land outside: black trees, stunted brush, dead ground. Larvae wriggle in the dust everywhere, alongside gray, ghostly petitioners in the last stage of being drained of all feeling. When the last of it is gone, what's left of them melts into the gloom of Pluton.

Heroes and desperate lovers from material worlds do sometimes make it here, down a branch of the Styx or through portals hidden in great volcanic cracks. They come believing they can find the soul of someone they lost and carry it out of a hopeless forever. Along with the larvae, the faded petitioners and these rare mortals, demons, devils and yugoloths roam the land looking for anything worth taking.
