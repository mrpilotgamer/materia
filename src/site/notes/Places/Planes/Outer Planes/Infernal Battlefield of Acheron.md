## Overview
The noise of battle is the first thing a soldier hears on arriving in Acheron and the last thing a refugee hears on leaving. Nothing else is recorded of the place: conflict, war, strife, and struggle, and little besides. Armies are numerous here and leaders are scarce, which produces the plane's characteristic population of rebels without a cause, drawn from petitioners, mortals, fiends, and celestials alike.

Acheron consists of four layers, each made of iron cubes the size of islands or continents, drifting in an airy void. The cubes collide from time to time, and the echoes of past collisions never entirely fade, mingling with the ring of sword on sword as armies fight across the cube faces.

Every cube is pitted and scarred, cracked and dented by collision and cratered by battle. Order asserts itself even in the damage: on Acheron the iron rusts and fractures along straight lines and right angles and never otherwise. Some cubes measure only a few hundred feet on a side. Others are large enough to hold whole cities and kingdoms. Shapes other than cubes exist but are uncommon, except on the third layer of Tintibulus, where the rule reverses.

Sight functions normally. A gray, fluctuating illumination lights the plane, ranging between something like bright moonlight and something like a dark and cloudy day. Hearing functions normally as well, though the echo of colliding cubes and the ring of battle never leaves the background.

## Planar Links
As on all the lower planes, the River Styx runs through the topmost layer, called Avalas. It behaves oddly there, welling up from a crater on one cube, running many miles across its face, draining into another crater, and reappearing on a different cube entirely. The river occasionally shifts to a new course, and entire cities have been washed away in a tide of forgetfulness and death when it does.

Portals are reasonably common and generally appear in the mouths of the tunnels that riddle most of Acheron's cubes.

## Inhabitants
Renegade armies containing every sort of creature wander the cube faces looking for enemy forces to fight. Mutiny or madness brings down even the strongest commander before long, which leaves most armies with no objective beyond the destruction of other renegade armies. Armies of undead or constructs sometimes last longer, being capable of carrying out their final orders without needing to believe in them.

Armies that have not yet gone entirely mad may still hold a purpose: defending a realm, securing provisions, deposing an impostor king, or any of a hundred similar causes. Because nearly all such causes mattered on some plane far from Acheron, even disciplined companies lose their focus in time and go renegade like the rest.

Achaierai, devils, imps, fomorians, rakshasas, dragons, and yugoloths also live here. Rakshasa clans hold several hidden cubes throughout the plane, concealed behind powerful illusions. Clockwork creatures out of Mechanus maintain a few mining colonies in the two lowest layers, similarly hidden.

Acheron also holds enormous flocks of birds. Ravens, vultures, gulls, bloodhawks, and swallows tumble on the wind, well fed on the carnage of the battlefields below.

## Petitioners
Deserters and petitioners fill out many of the renegade armies. A soldier who killed for a cause they did not believe in, and killed gladly, may find themselves here. Particularly fervent revolutionaries and terrorists slain in the material world also arrive in Acheron, frequently as commanders of the roving armies. Such renegade commanders find no rest until they are finally killed and their essence merges into the plane itself.

## Travel and Conflict
Moving about on Acheron is much like moving about in the material world. Walking from one cube face to another daunts the inexperienced and proves straightforward in practice. Crossing between cubes requires some means of flight. Travelers in Avalas and Thuldanin must watch for collisions, since everything caught between two cubes at the moment of impact is crushed out of existence entirely. Cubes on a collision course are visible a day or two beforehand, which is warning enough to evacuate.

## Layers

### Avalas
The first layer is also called the Battleplains, holding more cubes than any other layer and enough armies and fortresses to populate them. The clash of distant cubes cannot be distinguished from the clash of a battlefield nearby. Cubes here range from city-sized to continent-sized, and the smallest are generally the oldest, ground down to their present size by ages of collision.

### Thuldanin
The second layer resembles the first at a glance, though its population is far smaller. Its cubes are riddled with pockets and hollows, and surface pits lead down into labyrinths cluttered with the refuse of every war ever fought.

The wreckage of countless devices lies everywhere: great ships burst open, toppled siege towers, enormous weapons, steam-driven carriages, flying machines of every description, and contraptions whose power source and purpose no one has identified. Most of it no longer works, having been petrified to stonelike immobility by the preservative quality of the layer.

Salvage teams and opportunists scavenge Thuldanin for intact weapons, and the search rewards patience. Persistent searchers have recovered items of considerable power and mechanisms worth copying even when they could not be used. Experienced salvagers do not linger, because the preservative quality makes no distinction between an object and a creature.

**Resisting Preservation on Thuldanin:** The petrifaction that fixes the layer's wreckage in place threatens living creatures, undead, and petitioners equally. The onset is gradual and unpredictable, a matter of accumulated exposure and never of any single moment, and those who succumb turn to stone where they stand. No ordinary restorative magic reverses it. Only workings of the highest order have ever been recorded undoing the change. Veteran travelers accordingly limit each stay on Thuldanin to under thirty days and take their rest on another layer before returning.

### Tintibulus
Tintibulus inverts the rule of the layers above it. Four-sided, five-sided, eight-sided, nine-sided, twelve-sided, and other odd-sided solids outnumber cubes here. They are made of gray volcanic stone, each coated in ashen dust several inches deep and in places several feet. Collisions split a solid along its natural fault lines into two smaller solids, and the constant impacts produce a ringing, bell-like roar that never stops anywhere in the layer. Few creatures live here, petitioners or otherwise.

### Ocanthus
The fourth layer has no light at all and is filled instead with fast-flying, razor-thin shards. Some are little more than needles. Others are miles across, and the largest carry their own objective gravity like the cubes above, along with an atmosphere that is breathable if bitterly cold. The perpetual blizzard of blades makes Ocanthus hostile to creatures and objects without distinction.

The shards are black ice frozen into thin layers. Collisions break them into progressively smaller pieces, then into needles, then into dust. All of them originate from a single source: the night-black boundary of Ocanthus, a sheet of infinite, magically charged black ice.

Whether that sheet is a boundary or a barrier between Ocanthus and some deeper layer has never been established. Some accounts hold that it is the destination of the River Styx, and that every memory the river has taken still exists, frozen into the ice. The sheet possesses objective directional gravity, which leaves open the possibility that it is simply a shard of black ice so vast it has been mistaken for the edge of the layer.

**Surviving an Ocanthus Bladestorm:** Nothing short of an exceptionally strong artificial structure offers protection, and such structures are breached eventually in any case. Anything else in the layer is exposed without pause to shards cutting through the darkness like thrown knives.
