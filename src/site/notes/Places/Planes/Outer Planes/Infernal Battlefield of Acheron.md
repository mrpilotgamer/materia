## Overview
The sound of battle is the first thing a soldier hears on arriving in Acheron and the last thing a refugee hears on the way out. There's little else to say about the place. It is war and struggle, over and over. There are plenty of armies here and very few leaders, so the plane is full of rebels with no cause left: petitioners, mortals, fiends and celestials alike.

Acheron has four layers, each made of iron cubes the size of islands or continents floating in open air. Now and then two cubes crash together, and the echoes of old crashes never fully die away. They mix with the clang of swords as armies fight across the cube faces.

Every cube is pitted, cracked, dented from collisions and cratered by fighting. Even the damage is orderly. On Acheron, iron rusts and breaks only along straight lines and right angles. Some cubes are only a few hundred feet across. Others are big enough to hold whole cities and kingdoms. Other shapes exist but are rare, except on the third layer, Tintibulus, where it's the other way around.

Sight works normally. A gray, shifting light fills the plane, ranging from something like bright moonlight to something like a dark, overcast day. Hearing works normally too, though the echo of crashing cubes and the noise of battle are always there in the background.

## Planar Links
As on every lower plane, the River Styx runs through the top layer, called Avalas. Here it behaves strangely. It wells up out of a crater on one cube, runs for miles across its face, drains into another crater and comes back up on a different cube altogether. Sometimes the river changes course, and when it does, it has washed away whole cities in a flood of forgetting and death.

Portals are fairly common. They usually show up in the mouths of the tunnels that run through most of Acheron's cubes.

## Divine Realms
**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Progenitors/Demons/Viole, the Purple Progenitor Demon\|Viole]]**, Lord of Nobility, rules the Amethyst Kingdom. It is the strongest domain on Acheron. Viole is the lavish life and the right to rule.

**[[Religion and Deities/Deific Figures/Gods/Alfugel, God of Hatred\|Alfugel]]**, the Embodiment of Rage, holds the Breaking Ground on Avalas, the Battleplains. It is a cube where the fighting never stops long enough for anyone to build walls.

## Inhabitants
Rogue armies of every kind of creature wander the cube faces looking for someone to fight. Sooner or later mutiny or madness brings down even the strongest commander, so most armies end up with no goal except destroying other rogue armies. Armies of undead or constructs sometimes last longer, because they can keep carrying out their last orders without needing to believe in them.

Armies that haven't completely lost their minds may still have a purpose: guarding a realm, finding supplies, overthrowing a false king, or any of a hundred other causes. But almost all of those causes mattered on some plane far from Acheron, so even disciplined companies lose their way eventually and go rogue like the rest.

Achaierai, devils, imps, fomorians, rakshasas, dragons and yugoloths live here too. Rakshasa clans hold several hidden cubes around the plane, disguised by powerful illusions. Clockwork creatures from Mechanus run a few mining colonies on the two lowest layers, also hidden.

Acheron also has huge flocks of birds. Ravens, vultures, gulls, bloodhawks and swallows tumble on the wind, fat from the battlefields below.

## Petitioners
Deserters and petitioners fill out many of the rogue armies. A soldier who killed for a cause they didn't believe in, and enjoyed it, may end up here. Fanatical revolutionaries and terrorists killed on material worlds also come to Acheron, often as commanders of the roaming armies. Those commanders get no rest until they are finally killed and their essence melts into the plane.



## Travel and Conflict
Moving around on Acheron is a lot like moving around on Materia. Stepping from one face of a cube to the next looks frightening to beginners but turns out to be easy. Getting from one cube to another takes some way to fly. In Avalas and Thuldanin, travelers have to watch for collisions, because anything caught between two cubes when they hit is crushed out of existence. Cubes on a collision course can be seen a day or two ahead, which gives enough warning to get out.

## Layers

### Avalas
The first layer is also called the Battleplains. It has more cubes than any other layer, and enough armies and fortresses to fill them. The crash of faraway cubes sounds exactly like a battle nearby. Cubes here range from the size of a city to the size of a continent, and the smallest are usually the oldest, worn down by ages of collisions.

### Thuldanin
At first glance the second layer looks like the first, but far fewer people live here. Its cubes are full of pockets and hollows, and pits in the surface lead down into mazes cluttered with the junk of every war ever fought.

Wreckage of countless machines lies everywhere: huge ships split open, fallen siege towers, giant weapons, steam carriages, flying machines of every kind, and devices nobody can figure out the purpose or power source of. Most of it doesn't work anymore. The layer preserves things by turning them to something like stone.

Salvage crews and scavengers search Thuldanin for weapons that still work, and patience pays off. Determined searchers have found very powerful items, and machines worth copying even when they couldn't be used. Experienced salvagers never stay long, because the layer doesn't care whether it's preserving an object or a person.

**Resisting Preservation on Thuldanin:** The stone-turning that freezes the layer's wreckage threatens the living, the undead and petitioners alike. It comes on slowly and unpredictably, building up over time, and those it takes turn to stone where they stand. Ordinary healing magic can't undo it. Only the most powerful magic has ever been known to reverse it. Veteran travelers never stay on Thuldanin for thirty days at a stretch, and they go somewhere else to rest before coming back.



### Tintibulus
Tintibulus flips the rule of the layers above. Here, solids with four, five, eight, nine, twelve or other odd numbers of sides outnumber the cubes. They are made of gray volcanic rock, covered in ash several inches deep and in places several feet. When two collide, they split along their natural fault lines into smaller solids, and the constant impacts make a ringing roar like bells that never stops anywhere on the layer. Very few creatures live here, petitioners or otherwise.



### Ocanthus
The fourth layer has no light at all. It is full of fast-flying, razor-thin shards. Some are no bigger than needles. Others are miles across, and the biggest have their own gravity like the cubes above, along with air that can be breathed, though it's bitterly cold. The never-ending blizzard of blades makes Ocanthus deadly to creatures and objects alike.

The shards are thin sheets of black ice. When they collide they break into smaller pieces, then needles, then dust. All of them come from one place: the pitch-black edge of Ocanthus, an endless sheet of magically charged black ice.

Nobody knows whether that sheet is just the edge of Ocanthus or a wall between it and some deeper layer. Some say it is where the River Styx ends, and that every memory the river ever took is still in there, frozen into the ice. The sheet has its own gravity, so it could also just be a shard of black ice so huge that people mistook it for the edge of the layer.

**Surviving an Ocanthus Bladestorm:** Only an extremely strong built structure offers any protection, and even those break down eventually. Anything else on the layer is constantly exposed to shards slicing through the dark like thrown knives.
